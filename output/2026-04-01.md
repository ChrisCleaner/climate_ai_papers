# ACE2-NEMO Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
First multi-decadal (70-year) coupled climate simulation integrating a machine-learned atmospheric emulator (ACE2) with a full-depth dynamical ocean model (NEMO). Demonstrates feasibility of ML-atmosphere/physics-based-ocean hybrid systems for climate modeling.

**Method:**
- Couples ACE2 (ML atmospheric emulator trained on reanalysis/model data) to NEMO ocean model via interactive two-way feedback
- Runs 70-year historical (1950-2020) and fixed-1950s control simulations
- Compares results against EC-Earth3P (traditional coupled climate model sharing same ocean component)

**Data:**
Historical forcing (1950-2020) and fixed-1950s control conditions; validation against EC-Earth3P simulations.

**Climate Relevance:**
Tests whether ML atmospheric models can realistically simulate slow climate variability (El Niño, decadal oscillations) and climate sensitivity to CO₂ forcing—critical for understanding atmosphere-ocean coupling mechanisms.

**Implications:**
Suggests ML emulators could accelerate next-generation Earth system models by replacing computationally expensive atmospheric components while maintaining ocean physics; identifies specific tuning needs for tropical feedback mechanisms.

**Limitations:**
- Weak tropical Pacific atmospheric feedback produces unrealistic El Niño amplitude
- Reduced shortwave radiation bias causes CO₂ response divergence from EC-Earth3P
- Limited to single ML architecture; generalizability unclear

**TL;DR:**
ML-atmosphere/physics-ocean hybrid models are viable for multi-decadal climate simulation but require improved tropical feedback parameterization.

# Research Summary: Calibrated Conformal Prediction for Microphysical Processes

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that conformal prediction methods can provide statistically valid, well-calibrated uncertainty quantification for ML emulators of atmospheric microphysical processes without modifying the underlying models. Shows conformalized quantile regression outperforms split conformal prediction for climate variables spanning multiple orders of magnitude.

**Method:**
- Split conformal prediction applied to deterministic ML emulators
- Conformalized quantile regression (CQR) applied to quantile regression emulators
- Both methods generate prediction intervals with statistical validity guarantees
- Comparison based on calibration and sharpness metrics

**Data:**
ML emulators trained on ICOsahedral Nonhydrostatic (ICON) model output from limited-area numerical weather prediction simulations; six microphysical process rates evaluated.

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates (precipitation formation, aerosol-cloud interactions) are critical for accurate weather and climate modeling. Reliable uncertainty quantification improves confidence in ML-based climate predictions and parameterizations.

**Implications:**
Enables practical deployment of ML emulators in operational weather/climate models with quantified prediction uncertainty; reduces computational costs while maintaining physical interpretability through interval-based outputs.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to ICON model outputs; generalization to other climate models or observational data not explicitly addressed; computational cost comparison with alternative uncertainty methods not detailed.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal prediction methods provide computationally efficient, statistically valid uncertainty quantification for atmospheric ML emulators, with conformalized quantile regression proving most reliable across variable scales.

# Research Summary: Data-Driven Eddy Parameterization in CESM

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that a machine learning-based eddy parameterization (Zanna & Bolton 2020) significantly alters coupled climate dynamics when integrated into an idealized CESM model, producing hemispheric asymmetries and large-scale circulation changes through enhanced ocean heat transport.

**Method:**
Implements a data-driven eddy momentum parameterization featuring upgradient (backscatter) momentum flux within an eddy-permitting MOM6 ocean model coupled to atmospheric components; evaluates climate state changes via regional heat-budget analysis to isolate dynamical mechanisms.

**Data:**
Idealized fully-coupled CESM configuration; the parameterization itself is trained on prior eddy-resolving simulations (ZB20 framework), though specific training datasets are not detailed in the abstract.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical gap in climate modeling: mesoscale ocean eddies (~10-100 km) drive heat and momentum transport but remain unresolved in most climate models; improved parameterization directly impacts ocean circulation strength and poleward heat redistribution.

**Implications:**
Results provide a benchmark for implementing AI-derived parameterizations in comprehensive climate models; demonstrates potential to refine projections of Southern Ocean dynamics, meridional overturning circulation, and atmospheric jet positioning—all critical for climate prediction.

**Limitations:**
Idealized model configuration may not capture full complexity of realistic climate systems; hemispheric asymmetry results are specific to Southern Ocean geometry; transferability to higher-resolution or more complex models unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning eddy parameterization strengthens ocean heat transport and reshapes large-scale climate patterns through upgradient momentum fluxes, with pronounced effects on Southern Hemisphere circulation.
