### [Uncertainty in wind and solar projections depends on global and regional climate models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20052v1)

**Authors:** Nina Effenberger, Reto Knutti
**Published:** 2026-03-20
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, stat.AP
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Wind and Solar Uncertainty in Climate Projections

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies how regional climate models (RCMs) and global climate models (GCMs) contribute differently to uncertainty in projected wind and solar resources. Demonstrates that uncertainty sources vary by variable, region, and season—critical for energy planning under climate change.

**Method:**
Variance decomposition analysis applied to 31 RCM-GCM model pairs, isolating contributions from each model type across spatial and seasonal dimensions. Separates historical field variability from projected change uncertainty.

**Data:**
31 regional-global climate model combinations; spatially and seasonally resolved wind speed and solar radiation fields (historical and projected).

**Climate Relevance:**
Wind and solar resources are climate-sensitive renewable energy sources. Accurate uncertainty quantification is essential for climate-resilient energy infrastructure planning and climate mitigation strategies.

**Implications:**
- GCMs dominate uncertainty in wind speed projections (except mountains, where RCMs matter more)
- RCMs dominate summer solar projections; GCMs dominate winter
- Findings enable better-designed multi-model ensembles tailored to specific energy applications and regions
- Supports uncertainty-aware decision-making for renewable energy systems under climate change

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to 31 model pairs; results may not generalize to newer model generations or alternative ensemble designs.

**TL;DR:**
GCM and RCM contributions to renewable energy uncertainty are variable-, region-, and season-dependent, requiring tailored ensemble designs for robust energy planning.

# CNN-Based NAO Forecasting: Research Summary

Develops a CNN-based statistical model to predict early winter North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) from sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies, demonstrating superior performance over linear methods by capturing nonlinear SST-NAO teleconnections.

**Method:**
• Convolutional neural network trained on SST fields from 1, 2, and 3 months prior
• Linear regression model as benchmark
• Sensitivity analysis to identify influential spatial regions
• Training period: 1940–2023 using ERA5 reanalysis

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis SST fields and NAO indices spanning 84 years; focus on November-December early winter period.

**Climate Relevance:**
The NAO is the primary driver of North Atlantic climate variability, affecting European temperature and precipitation patterns. Understanding SST-NAO coupling—particularly the weak, nonlinear relationships—improves mechanistic understanding of seasonal predictability.

**Implications:**
Enhanced medium-range NAO forecasting capability could improve seasonal climate predictions for Europe. Demonstrates practical value of deep learning for capturing complex climate teleconnections that dynamical models struggle to represent.

**Limitations:**
Skill appears ENSO-dependent (stronger during strong ENSO events); unclear generalization to neutral ENSO phases. Statistical approach may not capture future climate regime shifts.

**TL;DR:**
CNNs outperform linear models for early winter NAO prediction by learning nonlinear SST relationships, with skill modulated by ENSO strength.
