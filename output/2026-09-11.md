# Neptune: AI Model for Global Ocean Subseasonal Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Neptune is a data-driven neural network framework that emulates global ocean and sea-ice dynamics for subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting up to 60 days ahead. It replaces computationally expensive physics-based Ocean General Circulation Models with a hybrid deep learning architecture capable of high-resolution predictions.

**Method:**
Combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for local feature extraction with Spherical Fourier Neural Operators (SFNOs) to capture global cross-scale interactions on the spherical Earth. Two variants (Neptune-1 at 1° and Neptune-025 at 0.25° resolution) predict multiple ocean variables: temperature, salinity, currents, sea surface height, sea ice thickness/concentration, with daily outputs at surface and depth.

**Data:**
Trained on historical ocean model outputs and atmospheric forcing fields; evaluated against multiple observational and model-based metrics. Specific datasets not detailed in abstract.

**Climate Relevance:**
S2S ocean forecasting directly supports climate-sensitive sectors: agricultural planning, water resource management, disaster risk reduction, energy demand forecasting, and insurance risk assessment. Accurate ocean state prediction is critical for understanding coupled ocean-atmosphere dynamics and climate variability.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates feasibility of replacing expensive OGCMs with efficient neural emulators for operational forecasting systems. Enables faster iteration and improvement cycles while maintaining physical coherency and long-term stability.

**Limitations:**
Limited to prescribed atmospheric forcing (not fully coupled); evaluation metrics and dataset details not provided in abstract; generalization to extreme events unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Neptune uses CNNs and spherical Fourier operators to emulate global ocean dynamics for 60-day forecasts, offering a computationally efficient alternative to traditional circulation models for subseasonal climate prediction.

**Method:**
