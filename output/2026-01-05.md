### [Carbon mineralization in CO2-seawater-basalt systems: Reactive transport dynamics and vesicular pore architecture controls](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00710v1)

**Authors:** Mohammad Nooraiepour, Mohammad Masoudi, Helge Hellevang
**Published:** 2026-01-02
**Categories:** physics.geo-ph, physics.flu-dyn
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Carbon Mineralization in Basaltic CO₂ Storage

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that carbonate precipitation in basaltic rocks is nucleation-controlled and stochastic, with pore network topology (not just chemistry) critically determining CO₂ storage viability. Reveals that basalts' low pore coordination numbers create serial flow paths vulnerable to rapid permeability collapse from precipitation.

**Method:**
• Flow-through experiments (80°C) with CO₂-acidified seawater on basaltic glass
• Geochemical modeling (reactive transport simulation)
• Multi-scale pore imaging: micro-CT analysis and post-experiment phase identification (XRD/SEM)
• Comparison of connected vs. total porosity across three basalt facies

**Data:**
Flow rate variations (0.05–0.005 mL/min); porosity measurements (18–42% total, 1.3–32% connected); pore coordination numbers; mineral phases (calcium carbonate, smectite); permeability changes during precipitation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses permanent CO₂ sequestration via mineral carbonation in basalt—a proposed negative-emissions pathway for climate mitigation. Basaltic formations offer rapid mineralization compared to silicate-dominated reservoirs.

**Implications:**
Probabilistic reactive transport models incorporating realistic pore topologies are essential for predicting CO₂ storage permanence. Seawater chemistry reduces efficiency; pore architecture design becomes critical to avoid catastrophic permeability loss during long-term storage.

**Limitations:**
Limited to laboratory scale and basaltic glass; field-scale validation needed. Seawater complexity may not fully represent in-situ conditions; longer-term precipitation dynamics unexplored.

**TL;DR:**
Basalt's serial pore networks make CO₂ storage risky due to stochastic precipitation causing permeability collapse, requiring probabilistic modeling frameworks for safe geological sequestration.
