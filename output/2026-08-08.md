# FESOM2-JAX v1.0 Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
A fully differentiable, GPU-native Python reimplementation of the FESOM2 ocean-sea-ice model that maintains scientific fidelity to the original Fortran code while enabling gradient-based optimization and machine learning integration.

**Method:**
- JAX-based re-implementation preserving unstructured-mesh, cell-vertex finite-volume formulation
- End-to-end automatic differentiation via reverse-mode autodiff through full time loops
- Kernel-by-kernel verification against original Fortran model using finite differences
- Scales from single CPU/GPU to 256 GPUs with identical physics

**Data:**
1958–2019 hindcast forcing; validation against observational climatologies for temperature, salinity, heat content, and sea ice.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables gradient-based calibration of ocean-sea-ice dynamics and hybrid physics-ML parameterizations—critical for improving climate model accuracy and reducing structural uncertainty in coupled Earth system models.

**Implications:**
- Accelerates parameter optimization and sensitivity analysis (113 simulated years/day on 4 GH200 superchips)
- Lowers barriers to testing novel numerics and physics parameterizations
- Facilitates bidirectional knowledge transfer between differentiable and traditional climate modeling communities
- Supports integration of learned components into operational climate predictions

**Limitations:**
Communication bandwidth (not arithmetic) limits scaling; unstructured mesh complexity may restrict adoption compared to structured-grid alternatives.

**TL;DR:**
First CMIP-class ocean model on unstructured mesh with native automatic differentiation, enabling gradient-based optimization while maintaining climate simulation accuracy.

**Method:**
Sequence-to-sequence ConvLSTM architecture that processes temporal sequences of LAI and meteorological inputs (temperature, precipitation) to generate multi-step daily forecasts. Combines convolutional operations for spatial feature extraction with LSTM recurrence for temporal dynamics.

**Data:**
Historical LAI sequences and daily meteorological forcing (temperature, precipitation) over the South-Central United States; evaluation across multiple plant functional types (forests, grasslands, shrublands, croplands).

**Climate Relevance:**
LAI is a critical biophysical variable controlling land-atmosphere interactions (evapotranspiration, carbon cycling, albedo). Prognostic LAI forecasts enable improved subseasonal land surface and climate model initialization, addressing a key gap in vegetation-climate coupling.

**Implications:**

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: Network-Based Streamflow Alteration Mapping

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces the Streamflow Alteration Index (SAI), a network-based screening metric that maps how dams and water infrastructure alter streamflow across river networks at high spatial resolution, enabling assessment at both gauged and ungauged locations.

**Method:**
SAI propagates point-based alteration signals (from confirmed and potential sources) downstream through a hydrologic routing network using two paired indices (SAI_I and SAI_II, 0-100%). Classification algorithm categorizes subbasins as natural, conditional, or altered based on cumulative downstream influence.

**Data:**
Ontario Lake and River Routing Product Version 2 (OLRRP v2); inventory of 643 alteration sources from provincial datasets; validation against Reference Hydrometric Basin Network (RHBN) gauges and Water Survey of Canada (WSC) natural/regulated classifications.

**Climate Relevance:**
Streamflow alteration from infrastructure disrupts hydrologic regimes critical for aquatic ecosystems and water availability—both climate-sensitive systems. Enables rapid assessment of human-induced hydrologic changes across large domains without detailed operational data.

**Implications:**
Provides objective, scalable screening tool for hydrologic modeling, environmental flow assessment, and ecosystem management; applicable at national-to-global scales for identifying priority reaches requiring detailed study or restoration.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to Ontario; does not require dam operating information (strength for data-sparse regions, but may miss seasonal/operational nuances); classification accuracy varies by gauge type (74% for regulated vs. 86% for natural).

**TL;DR:**
Network-based metric maps streamflow alteration from infrastructure across river systems, achieving 86% accuracy in classifying natural gauges and enabling seamless high-resolution mapping for hydrologic and ecosystem applications.
