# Neptune: AI Model for Global Ocean Subseasonal Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Neptune is an end-to-end data-driven framework that emulates global ocean and sea-ice dynamics for subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting up to 60 days ahead. It replaces computationally expensive physics-based Ocean General Circulation Models with a hybrid neural network approach capable of high-resolution predictions.

**Method:**
Combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for local feature extraction with Spherical Fourier Neural Operators (SFNOs) to capture global cross-scale interactions. Two variants provided: Neptune-1 (1° resolution) and Neptune-025 (0.25° resolution), both producing daily outputs across ocean surface and water column.

**Data:**
Trained on historical ocean state variables (temperature, salinity, currents, sea surface height, sea ice properties) forced by prescribed daily atmospheric fields. Specific datasets not detailed in abstract.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports S2S forecasting critical for water management, agriculture, disaster risk reduction, energy planning, and insurance—sectors dependent on reliable 2-8 week ocean predictions. Enables representation of ocean dynamics essential for climate variability (ENSO, Indian Ocean Dipole).

**Implications:**
Provides faster, more accessible alternative to traditional OGCMs for operational forecasting systems. High spatio-temporal resolution enables improved decision-making in climate-sensitive sectors and potential integration into next-generation coupled forecast systems.

**Limitations:**
Long-term stability claims require validation beyond 60-day window. Dependence on prescribed atmospheric forcing limits full coupled system representation. Generalization to extreme events or climate change scenarios not addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Neptune uses hybrid CNN-SFNO architecture to emulate global ocean dynamics at high resolution for subseasonal forecasting, offering computational efficiency gains over traditional physics-based models.

# Research Summary: Macroturbulence Scaling Theory for Planetary Atmospheres

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a new scaling theory for atmospheric eddy heat flux that works across marginally critical regimes where existing theories fail, capturing both domain-averaged and local eddy behavior across diverse planetary conditions.

**Method:**
- Analyzes hundreds of idealized dry GCM simulations varying rotation rates, meridional temperature gradients, stratification, and seasonality
- Uses scaling arguments from zonal momentum balance and kinematic eddy mixing effects
- Introduces thermal Rossby number as key non-dimensional parameter accounting for nonlinear eddy momentum fluxes across jets

**Data:**
Idealized general circulation model simulations spanning wide parameter ranges; validation against simple energy balance model predictions of global near-surface temperature distributions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Near-surface eddy heat flux directly controls atmospheric mass transport and surface circulation patterns—fundamental to understanding Earth's climate dynamics and how it responds to changing planetary conditions (rotation, stratification, temperature gradients).

**Implications:**
Provides theoretical framework applicable to diverse climate states and potentially extendable to moist atmospheres, improving predictive capability for general circulation behavior under varied climate scenarios.

**Limitations:**
Theory developed for dry atmospheres; extension to realistic moist conditions remains unresolved. Validation limited to idealized models rather than observational data.

**TL;DR:**
New scaling theory explains atmospheric eddy heat flux across marginally critical regimes where previous theories fail, with implications for general circulation theory across climate states.

**Method:**

• Comparison of theoretical incentives against observed generation behavior
• Temporal analysis across policy regimes (2005–2025)

Reanalysis weather data for wind/solar potential reconstruction; German renewable generator registry with support scheme classifications; wholesale electricity price data; observed generation output.

**Climate Relevance:**
