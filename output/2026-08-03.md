# TerraNova: Foundation Model for the Anthropocene – Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces TerraNova, a foundation model that unifies Earth-system physical data (gridded fields) with societal data (national indicators) by respecting their native geometric representations, eliminating lossy border-averaging in coupled human-Earth modeling.

**Method:**
- Dual encoders for gridded Earth-system fields (512) and national indicators (512)
- Cross-modal transformers fuse location, country, time, and task embeddings into shared spatiotemporal state
- Hypernetwork generates task-specific decoder with evidential uncertainty quantification
- Two contrastive objectives: population-weighted country-territory alignment and alignment with pretrained geospatial embeddings

**Data:**
1,024 multimodal records: 512 gridded Earth-system fields (continuous spatial data) and 512 national socioeconomic indicators (administrative-unit data).

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables integrated modeling of coupled human-Earth systems critical for understanding anthropogenic climate impacts, adaptation strategies, and policy-relevant climate projections at national scales while preserving physical Earth observations.

**Implications:**
Supports dense field reconstruction from sparse observations, rapid adaptation to unseen variables, and country-level climate-policy applications; runs efficiently on consumer hardware, democratizing access to coupled Earth-system modeling.

**Limitations:**
Apparent reliance on national-level aggregation may obscure subnational heterogeneity; generalization to regions with sparse observational coverage unclear; computational efficiency claims need empirical validation.

**TL;DR:**
Foundation model bridging gridded geophysical data and national socioeconomic indicators enables efficient, uncertainty-aware coupled human-Earth system modeling.

# Research Summary: Memory Compression vs. Physical State for AMOC Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that memory-based (scalar compression) and physics-based (thermohaline structure) approaches optimize different prediction horizons for Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation forecasting. Shows that compact memory outperforms explicit physical state at recursive prediction steps, while physical augmentation excels at direct 20-year forecasts.

**Method:**
- Compared two neural network strategies across 30 CMIP6 trajectories using leave-one-family-out cross-validation
- Memory compression: recurrent encoding of observed AMOC index history
- Physical augmentation: direct inclusion of salinity, temperature, density fields
- Resolvent analysis to explain learned model stability properties

**Data:**
30 branch-consistent CMIP6 trajectories from eight model families; evaluated under SSP5–8.5 emissions scenario.

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC is a critical component of global heat redistribution and climate regulation. Improved prediction skill directly enhances seasonal-to-decadal climate forecasting and early warning systems for circulation disruption.

**Implications:**
Hybrid prediction frameworks should employ memory-based models for long-horizon recursive forecasting and physics-informed models for near-term direct projections. Results inform operational climate prediction system design.

**Limitations:**
Model-dependent performance (negative transfer in MIROC); forcing-dependent compression limits (NorESM); findings specific to CMIP6 ensemble structure.

**TL;DR:**
Scalar memory compression and physical state augmentation optimize different AMOC prediction timescales, suggesting complementary rather than competing approaches.

# Research Summary: Weather Emulators for Heat Extremes Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Comprehensive evaluation of six deep learning weather emulators against traditional dynamical and statistical models for 10-15 day temperature and extreme heat forecasting. Demonstrates that AI models can match or exceed physics-based forecasts in deterministic skill but reveal critical trade-offs in extreme event representation.

**Method:**
Comparative benchmarking of neural network-based weather emulators (Pangu-Weather, FuXi, ArchesWeather, AIFS, GraphCast, Aurora) using deterministic skill metrics and spectral analysis. Models evaluated against IFS (Integrated Forecasting System) and statistical baselines on global near-surface temperature and heat extremes.

**Data:**
Global atmospheric datasets at 10-15 day lead times; specific datasets not detailed in abstract but typical for weather ML studies (likely ERA5 reanalysis and operational forecast data).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in extended-range extreme heat prediction—a key climate adaptation need as warming increases heat wave frequency and intensity. Early warnings at 10-15 day horizons enable public health and infrastructure preparedness.

**Implications:**
AI emulators show promise for operational extended-range forecasting, but deployment requires resolving spectral blurring and peak intensity under-representation to ensure actionable early warnings for extreme events.

**Limitations:**
Emulators demonstrate reduced spectral fidelity and systematically under-represent extreme heat intensities compared to traditional models, limiting reliability for high-impact event forecasting.

**TL;DR:**
Deep learning weather emulators rival traditional forecasts in 10-15 day temperature skill but struggle with extreme heat intensity representation, requiring further development for operational deployment.

# Research Summary: Emission-Forecasting-Based Spatial-Temporal Carbon Response

**Main Contribution:**

**Method:**
- Hierarchical deep learning architecture with dual-stage attention mechanism
- LLM-based multi-agent cooperation system for enhanced forecasting resilience
- Spatial-temporal carbon scheduling model incorporating geographically dispatchable loads (GDLs): mobile energy storage systems (MESSs) and distributed data centers (DDCs)
- Day-ahead NCI prediction feeds into dynamic dispatch optimization

**Data:**
Modified IEEE 33-bus test system (no real-world dataset explicitly mentioned; appears to be simulation-based validation)

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses power sector decarbonization by enabling carbon-aware dispatch that responds to renewable energy variability. Reduces system emissions through temporal and spatial load shifting aligned with low-carbon generation periods.

**Implications:**
Accelerates grid decarbonization by reducing dispatch latency from hours to minutes, achieving >30% emission reductions. Applicable to grids with high renewable penetration and flexible loads (data centers, EVs, storage).

**Limitations:**
- Validation limited to synthetic test system; real-world grid performance unclear
- LLM integration methodology not detailed; computational overhead unspecified
- Scalability to larger, more complex grids not demonstrated
