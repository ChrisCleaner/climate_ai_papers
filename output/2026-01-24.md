# THOR: Foundation Model for Earth Observation

**Main Contribution:**
THOR is a compute-adaptive foundation model that unifies multi-sensor Earth observation data (Sentinel-1, -2, -3) across heterogeneous resolutions (10–1000 m) in a single architecture. It enables flexible inference-time patch sizing without retraining, allowing dynamic computational-accuracy trade-offs.

# Research Summary: The Interdecadal Bipolar Oscillation

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies the Interdecadal Bipolar Oscillation (IBO), a 60–80 year internal climate variability mode in atmospheric water vapor that explains asynchronous warming between Arctic and Antarctic regions. Demonstrates that the IBO's phase shift in the late 1980s amplified Arctic warming while suppressing Antarctic change, with potential phase reversal implications for future polar climate.

**Method:**
Spectral and temporal analysis of reanalysis data combined with CMIP6 model simulations (historical and pre-industrial control runs) to isolate internal variability modes from forced climate signals. Phase detection and projection analysis to assess future oscillation behavior.

**Data:**
Atmospheric reanalysis datasets (8 decades); CMIP6 historical simulations; CMIP6 pre-industrial control runs for internal variability characterization.

**Climate Relevance:**
Explains the observed polar asymmetry in climate response—rapid Arctic amplification versus delayed Antarctic warming—through an internally-driven mechanism rather than external forcing alone. Critical for understanding polar climate predictability and regional climate risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Potential IBO phase reversal could accelerate Antarctic warming and atmospheric moistening, triggering rapid climate change in the Southern Hemisphere. Improves seasonal-to-decadal climate prediction skill for polar regions if IBO phase can be forecast.

**Limitations:**
Model uncertainty in capturing polar water vapor dynamics acknowledged; phase reversal timing and magnitude remain uncertain; mechanism linking water vapor oscillation to polar temperature asymmetry could be more explicitly mechanistic.

**TL;DR:**
A 60–80 year water vapor oscillation between poles explains why the Arctic warmed rapidly while Antarctica lagged, with a potential phase flip that could accelerate Southern Hemisphere climate change.

• Combines nonlinear deep learning with mechanistic climate understanding for interpretability

**Implications:**
