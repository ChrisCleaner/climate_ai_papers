# PosteriorBench Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces PosteriorBench, a benchmark for evaluating whether generative models solving inverse problems capture the full posterior distribution rather than just point estimates. Addresses the critical gap that existing metrics miss mode collapse and uncertainty miscalibration in ill-posed problems.

**Method:**
Constructs reference posteriors using rejection sampling and MCMC for ground truth. Evaluates solvers across five metrics: posterior-mean error, posterior-standard-deviation error, maximum mean discrepancy, sliced Wasserstein distance, and radially averaged power-spectrum error. Tests neural operators with varying guidance weights and generation noise.

**Data:**
Four physics-based inverse problems: Darcy flow inversion, Poisson source recovery, carbon capture and storage (CCS), and light transport material inference. Includes sparse sensing, low-resolution observations, nonlinear forward models, and multimodal priors.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to carbon capture and storage (CCS) inversion—a key climate mitigation technology. Darcy flow inversion is essential for subsurface characterization in CO₂ sequestration and groundwater modeling. Addresses uncertainty quantification critical for climate risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Enables rigorous evaluation of generative solvers for climate-relevant inverse problems. Improves reliability of AI-based subsurface imaging and CCS monitoring. Provides framework for uncertainty-aware decision-making in climate adaptation strategies.

**Limitations:**
Computationally expensive reference generation limits scalability. Focuses on relatively low-dimensional problems; applicability to high-dimensional climate models unclear. Limited discussion of computational cost trade-offs for practitioners.

**TL;DR:**
PosteriorBench evaluates whether generative inverse solvers capture full posterior distributions rather than point estimates, with direct applications to climate-critical problems like carbon capture and subsurface characterization.

### [Disentangled Fingerprints suggest no historical weakening of Atlantic Overturning and Subpolar Gyre](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18551v1)

# Research Summary: Disentangled AMOC and Subpolar Gyre Fingerprints

**Main Contribution:**
Develops statistically optimized fingerprints that clearly separate Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) and Subpolar Gyre (SPG) signals, replacing traditional fingerprints that conflate the two systems. Challenges the narrative of substantial recent AMOC weakening while maintaining concern about stability loss.

**Method:**
Uses statistical optimization trained on CMIP6 pre-industrial control simulations to derive fingerprints from sea surface temperature and salinity data. Compares performance against traditional AMOC fingerprints through correlation analysis and reconstruction accuracy across independent model segments and historical experiments.

**Data:**
CMIP6 model ensemble (piControl and historical simulations); sea surface temperature and salinity observations; validation across multiple independent model segments.

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC and SPG regulate heat transport and regional climate stability in the North Atlantic. Detecting genuine weakening versus natural variability is critical for assessing tipping point risk under anthropogenic forcing, given limited observational records (~150 years).

**Implications:**
Provides improved observational monitoring tools for distinguishing AMOC and SPG changes, enabling better detection of destabilization precursors. Suggests recent AMOC decline estimates may be artifacts of fingerprint methodology rather than robust physical signals.

**Limitations:**
Fingerprints trained on pre-industrial conditions; applicability to future climate states uncertain. Observational validation limited by short instrumental records and sparse spatial coverage of ocean data.

**TL;DR:**
Optimized fingerprints disentangle AMOC and SPG variability, suggesting traditional methods overestimate recent AMOC weakening but cannot exclude stability loss.

### [A common warming-dependent decline emerges from divergent projections of tropical cyclone frequency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16521v1)

**Published:** 2026-09-15
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph

# Research Summary: Tropical Cyclone Frequency Under Climate Warming

**Main Contribution:**
Reconciles conflicting tropical cyclone frequency projections by demonstrating a consistent late-century decline driven by nonlinear environmental interactions rather than individual predictors. Shows that global TC frequency will decline by 2–8% per 2°C of warming, with confidence intervals narrowing when conditioned on specific warming levels.

**Method:**
Environment-dependent probabilistic TC model driven by 14 CMIP6 climate models under SSP3-7.0 (high) and SSP2-4.5 (intermediate) emissions scenarios. Employs counterfactual decomposition to isolate contributions from environmental predictors (absolute vorticity, vertical wind shear, relative humidity, potential intensity) and their nonlinear interactions.

**Data:**
CMIP6 multi-model ensemble (14 models); environmental variables extracted for TC genesis and intensity modeling; projections spanning 21st century with focus on late-century (2080–2100) changes.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical uncertainty in climate impact assessments; TC frequency projections directly inform disaster risk reduction and coastal adaptation planning. Demonstrates that nonlinear climate system responses can reconcile seemingly contradictory model outputs.

**Implications:**
Enables more confident risk assessment for tropical cyclone hazards under warming scenarios; supports policy decisions on infrastructure resilience and climate adaptation in vulnerable regions. Linear scaling relationship provides practical tool for rapid assessment at different warming targets.

**Limitations:**
Relies on CMIP6 model biases in environmental predictors; probabilistic model parameterization may not capture all physical mechanisms; regional variability masked by global aggregation.

**TL;DR:**
Tropical cyclone frequency will decline globally by 2–8% per 2°C warming, driven by nonlinear interactions among environmental factors rather than individual predictors.

# Research Summary: Structural Error-Aware Parameter Estimation for Climate Models

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an automated, interpretable method for calibrating Earth system model parameters that explicitly accounts for structural model errors rather than masking them. The approach uses history matching and iterative emulation to improve parameter estimation from Perturbed Parameter Ensembles (PPEs).

**Method:**
- History matching with structural error detection via simplified, interpretable emulators
- Decomposes high-dimensional calibration into linked low-dimensional subproblems
- Sequentially excludes structurally inconsistent variables to prevent error compensation
- Integrates constraints across subproblems to identify jointly plausible parameter regions
- Iteratively refines ensemble through simulation-emulation-calibration cycles

**Data:**
100-member PPE with 34 perturbed parameters from CAM6 (Community Atmosphere Model 6) with machine learning-based warm rain microphysics; validation against observed zonal climatologies.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical challenge in climate modeling: parameter uncertainty and structural deficiencies in cloud microphysics parameterizations, which significantly affect precipitation and radiative forcing predictions.

**Implications:**
Enables more robust climate model calibration by preventing parameters from compensating for model structural flaws, yielding ensemble members with improved performance and more reliable uncertainty quantification for climate projections.

**Limitations:**
Application demonstrated on single model version (CAM6); generalizability to other ESMs and parameterizations unclear; computational cost of iterative emulation-calibration cycles not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
An automated calibration method that detects and isolates structural model errors during parameter estimation, preventing compensatory parameter adjustments that mask fundamental model deficiencies.

- Outperforms linear baseline models in out-of-sample validation

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**
