# Research Summary: ML for CO2 Storage Dynamics

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a machine learning framework combining convolutional neural network surrogates with Bayesian inference to enable fast, accurate prediction and parameter estimation for CO2-brine multiphase flow in geological media—a critical challenge for carbon storage.

**Method:**
- CNN surrogate trained on high-fidelity numerical simulations of CO2 saturation and dissolved CO2 concentration fields
- Surrogate embedded in Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) framework for Bayesian parameter inference
- Validates against experimental observations from the FluidFlower rig (controlled laboratory system with heterogeneous porous media)

**Data:**
High-resolution, time-resolved observations from the FluidFlower experimental apparatus; training data from numerical simulations spanning wide ranges of multiphase flow properties.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports geological carbon dioxide storage (GCS)—a key climate mitigation strategy for reducing atmospheric CO2. Improved predictive capability and parameter uncertainty quantification enhance confidence in long-term CO2 containment safety.

**Implications:**
Enables computationally tractable exploration of high-dimensional parameter spaces for CO2 storage site characterization; identifies which geological features (faults, sealing layers) provide most informative observations for constraining model parameters; accelerates forward simulations by orders of magnitude.

**Limitations:**
Framework demonstrated on single laboratory experiment; generalization to field-scale heterogeneity and longer timescales requires validation; parameter identifiability varies depending on observation timing and spatial coverage.

**TL;DR:**
Neural network surrogates combined with Bayesian inference enable fast, accurate CO2 plume prediction and parameter estimation for geological storage applications.

# Conformal Risk Sharing: Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a distribution-free mechanism for fairly allocating financial costs of rare adverse events across groups while guaranteeing no participant is materially worse off. Combines interpretable cost-sharing policies with conformal prediction to provide certified, trustworthy obligation caps.

**Method:**
• Pairs a tunable sharing intensity policy (trained on data) with split conformal calibration
• Uses held-out calibration data to generate per-agent, distribution-free guarantees valid under exchangeability
• Produces both a redistribution rule and individual obligation bounds without assuming specific probability distributions

**Data:**
Synthetic datasets plus real-world applications including precipitation records and energy-cooperative data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to climate risk pooling mechanisms—precipitation extremes and energy system disruptions are key climate impacts. Framework enables equitable cost-sharing for climate-related disasters (e.g., crop failures, grid failures) where rare but severe events disproportionately burden vulnerable agents.

**Implications:**
Enables credible climate risk-sharing cooperatives and insurance mechanisms by providing mathematically certified guarantees that participants won't face unexpectedly large obligations, increasing adoption of collective climate adaptation strategies.

**Limitations:**
Exchangeability assumption may not hold for non-stationary climate data; scalability to large heterogeneous populations unclear; real-world deployment requires careful calibration data selection.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal prediction enables fair, certified cost-sharing for climate disasters with guaranteed individual obligation caps and no distributional assumptions.

# RIFTES: RTM- and Iteration-Free Temperature-Emissivity Separation

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes RIFTES, a computationally efficient framework that replaces iterative temperature-emissivity separation (TES) with a closed-form mathematical solution and physics-informed deep learning for atmospheric correction. Achieves superior accuracy (RMSE 1.06 K in simulations) while reducing computational time by 62–74% compared to existing methods.

**Method:**
- Reformulates iterative TES into mathematically equivalent non-iterative closed-form solution
- Employs deep residual neural network with integrated radiative transfer physics for atmospheric correction
- Incorporates masking mechanism to flexibly apply atmospheric constraints when available
- Eliminates need for radiative transfer model (RTM) iterations

**Data:**
- Synthetic simulation data (validation against split-window, TES, and hybrid algorithms)
- In-situ measurements from globally distributed validation sites
- ECOSTRESS (spaceborne thermal radiometer) and ABI (Advanced Baseline Imager) satellite data

**Climate Relevance:**
Land surface temperature (LST) is a critical climate variable for monitoring surface energy balance, evapotranspiration, and land-atmosphere interactions. Improved LST retrieval accuracy directly enhances climate modeling and environmental monitoring capabilities.

**Implications:**
Enables faster, more accurate LST products for operational satellite systems; reduces computational barriers for real-time or near-real-time climate monitoring; improves uncertainty quantification in regional and global climate assessments.

**Limitations:**
Not explicitly stated; potential constraints may include dependence on training data quality for neural network and applicability to non-clear-sky conditions (algorithm designed for clear-sky only).

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed deep learning replaces iterative algorithms to achieve 24–32% more accurate LST retrieval with 62–74% faster computation.
