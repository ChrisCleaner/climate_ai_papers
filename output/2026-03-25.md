### [Uncertainty in wind and solar projections depends on global and regional climate models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20052v1)

**Authors:** Nina Effenberger, Reto Knutti
**Published:** 2026-03-20
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, stat.AP
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Wind and Solar Uncertainty in Climate Projections

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies how uncertainty in wind and solar resource projections arises from regional climate models (RCMs) versus global climate models (GCMs) using variance decomposition across 31 model pairs. Demonstrates that uncertainty sources vary by resource type, region, and season, providing actionable guidance for ensemble design.

**Method:**
Spatially and seasonally resolved variance decomposition analysis separating RCM and GCM contributions to projected changes in wind speed and solar radiation. Compares absolute historical fields against projected future changes to isolate model-specific uncertainty sources.

**Data:**
31 RCM-GCM coupled model pairs from regional climate modeling ensembles; analysis includes spatial and seasonal resolution across multiple regions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Wind and solar resources are climate-sensitive variables critical for renewable energy planning. Accurate uncertainty quantification is essential for assessing climate change impacts on future energy availability and grid reliability.

**Implications:**
Enables more efficient ensemble design by identifying which model component (RCM vs. GCM) drives uncertainty for specific resources and regions. Supports climate-informed energy system planning by clarifying where model uncertainty is largest, improving decision-making under climate change scenarios.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to 31 model pairs; findings may not generalize to newer model generations or alternative RCM-GCM combinations not represented in the ensemble.

**TL;DR:**
RCM and GCM contributions to wind/solar projection uncertainty vary by resource, region, and season, with GCMs dominating wind changes and seasonal patterns controlling solar uncertainty.

# FuXi-ONS: Data-Driven Ensemble Ocean Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
First machine-learning ensemble system for probabilistic global ocean forecasting, delivering 5-day forecasts up to 365 days ahead across multiple ocean variables at 1° resolution without repeated numerical model integration.

**Method:**
Neural network-based ensemble approach that learns physically-structured perturbations to generate ensemble members and incorporates an atmospheric encoding module to maintain forecast stability over extended lead times, replacing computationally expensive traditional ensemble generation.

**Data:**
GLORYS12 reanalysis used for evaluation; training data sources not explicitly detailed in abstract but implied to be observational/reanalysis ocean datasets.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in probabilistic ocean forecasting for seasonal-to-annual timescales, essential for understanding ocean variability (SST, currents, subsurface properties) that drives regional climate patterns and extreme events like El Niño (Niño3.4 index).

**Implications:**
Enables rapid, efficient probabilistic ocean forecasts for climate risk assessment and seasonal prediction; orders-of-magnitude speedup over conventional ensemble systems facilitates operational deployment for climate services and decision-making.

**Limitations:**
Abstract does not discuss skill degradation mechanisms, regional performance variations, or physical constraint validation; long-term forecast stability beyond 365 days unexplored.

**TL;DR:**
Machine-learning ensemble system provides fast, skillful probabilistic global ocean forecasts competitive with traditional methods while running vastly faster.

# Research Summary: AI in Indian Agriculture

Demonstrates that structural data constraints—temporal misalignment, spatial fragmentation, and governance barriers—prevent effective AI adoption, particularly for smallholder farmers.

**Method:**
Systematic review of major national agricultural datasets and digital platforms, supplemented by comparative international case studies and implementation evidence analysis to identify recurring features of scalable digital agriculture systems.

**Data:**
Soil Health Cards, crop insurance databases, AgriStack platform, and selected state-level agricultural platforms; analysis covers temporal, spatial, and accessibility characteristics of these datasets.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses climate adaptation capacity in agriculture by examining data infrastructure barriers to AI-driven decision support for crop management, soil health monitoring, and risk management—critical for climate-resilient farming in India's vulnerable smallholder sector.

**Implications:**
Scalable AI agriculture requires: (1) incentive structures for data provision, (2) service bundling through local institutions, and (3) sensor-enabled risk management systems; current infrastructure disproportionately disadvantages 86% of Indian farmers lacking resources to compensate for weak data systems.

**Limitations:**
Primarily diagnostic rather than prescriptive; limited discussion of implementation costs or timeline for infrastructure remediation; focuses on India's context with unclear generalizability to other developing regions.

**TL;DR:**
India's fragmented, temporally misaligned agricultural data infrastructure prevents AI scaling, requiring governance reform and institutional bundling to serve smallholder farmers effectively.
