# CERBERUS: Vertical Cloud Profile Inference

**Main Contribution:**
CERBERUS is a probabilistic deep learning framework that reconstructs three-dimensional cloud vertical structure (radar reflectivity profiles) from two-dimensional satellite observations, addressing a critical scale mismatch in climate modeling.

**Method:**
Three-headed encoder-decoder neural network architecture predicting zero-inflated distributions of Ka-band radar reflectivity. Incorporates geostationary satellite brightness temperatures, surface meteorology, and temporal context as inputs to capture cloud microphysical variability and uncertainty.

**Data:**
Ground-based Ka-band radar observations from ARM Southern Great Plains site; geostationary satellite brightness temperatures; near-surface meteorological variables. Training/evaluation on withheld test periods to assess generalization.

**Climate Relevance:**
Clouds remain a dominant source of uncertainty in climate models due to poor vertical resolution in satellite data. Generating vertically-resolved cloud profiles bridges observational scales and enables better constraint of cloud processes in weather and climate simulations.

**Implications:**
Produces "synthetic observations" with physically meaningful uncertainty estimates that can validate model cloud parameterizations. Particularly valuable for multilayer and dynamically complex cloud regimes where 2D observations are most ambiguous.

**Limitations:**
Trained/evaluated at single ARM site; generalization to other regions and cloud regimes unclear. Zero-inflation approach may not capture all cloud microphysical complexity. Dependence on geostationary satellite availability limits applicability in polar regions.

**TL;DR:**
AI-based probabilistic inference recovers 3D cloud structure from satellite data, providing model-relevant uncertainty estimates to improve climate model cloud physics.
