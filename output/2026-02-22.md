### [Weak 21st-century AMOC response to Greenland meltwater in a strongly eddying ocean model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17235v1)

**Authors:** Oliver Mehling, Henk A. Dijkstra
**Published:** 2026-02-19
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: AMOC Response to Greenland Meltwater

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies Greenland meltwater's impact on Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) weakening through 2100 using a high-resolution eddy-resolving ocean model. Demonstrates that meltwater-induced AMOC weakening (0.6 ± 0.2 Sv) is modest compared to warming-driven weakening and is independent of model resolution.

**Method:**
High-resolution (1/10° horizontal) ocean model with explicit mesoscale eddy representation under SSP5-8.5 forcing scenario. Compares AMOC response to meltwater forcing across different ocean states (present-day vs. 21st-century conditions) to isolate state-dependent mechanisms.

**Data:**
Climate model simulations with Greenland meltwater forcing; SSP5-8.5 emissions scenario; output analyzed through 2100.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical uncertainty in AMOC projections—a key climate tipping point affecting regional temperatures, precipitation, and sea levels across the North Atlantic. Greenland ice sheet melt is an accelerating climate change signal requiring accurate representation in projections.

**Implications:**
Suggests that coarse-resolution climate models may adequately capture meltwater effects on AMOC despite neglecting eddies, reducing computational burden for ensemble projections. Indicates background ocean state is the dominant control, not model resolution, informing model development priorities.

**Limitations:**
Single model study; results may not generalize across different ocean model formulations; limited exploration of alternative forcing scenarios beyond SSP5-8.5.

**TL;DR:**
Greenland meltwater causes modest AMOC weakening independent of model resolution, with ocean state being more important than eddy representation for determining meltwater impacts.

# Research Summary: Fast Radiative Feedbacks via ML Weather Emulators

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that machine-learning weather emulators trained on historical data can accurately simulate fast radiative feedbacks (atmospheric processes operating on weekly timescales) without retraining, enabling study of climate responses to CO₂ perturbations outside the historical record.

**Method:**
• ML-based weather emulators trained on historical meteorological reanalyses
• Applied to radiative-convective equilibrium (RCE) simulations under perturbed CO₂ conditions
• Validation against full-physics Earth System Models (ESMs)
• Leverages the fact that fast feedback physics already exist in training data under historical boundary conditions

**Data:**
Historical meteorological reanalyses (training); ESM outputs for validation; top-of-atmosphere radiative balance and sea-surface temperature observations as boundary conditions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Fast radiative feedbacks govern precipitation and hydrological cycle responses to greenhouse gas forcing. Understanding these mechanisms is critical for projecting near-term climate impacts, as they operate on timescales relevant to current and near-future conditions.

**Implications:**
ML emulators offer computationally efficient alternatives to ESMs for studying fast climate processes and can extrapolate beyond historical conditions, potentially accelerating climate impact assessments and reducing computational barriers to climate research.

**Limitations:**
Approach limited to fast feedbacks; slow feedbacks (ocean-driven, decadal-scale) require different treatment. Generalizability to extreme climate states beyond training distribution not explicitly addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Historically trained ML weather emulators can quantify fast radiative feedback responses to CO₂ changes without retraining, matching full ESM results.

### [Ensemble-size-dependence of deep-learning post-processing methods that minimize an (un)fair score: motivating examples and a proof-of-concept solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15830v1)

**Authors:** Christopher David Roberts
**Published:** 2026-02-17
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Ensemble-Size-Dependent Deep Learning for Fair Climate Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies a critical problem where deep-learning post-processing methods violate fairness assumptions of the adjusted continuous ranked probability score (aCRPS) by introducing structural dependencies between ensemble members, causing ensemble-size-dependent performance. Proposes trajectory transformers as a solution that maintains conditional independence while improving forecast reliability.

**Method:**
- Compares two aCRPS-minimizing approaches: linear member-by-member calibration and transformer self-attention across ensemble dimension
- Demonstrates both introduce problematic member coupling that violates exchangeability assumptions
- Introduces trajectory transformers: applies self-attention over lead time (temporal dimension) rather than ensemble dimension, preserving conditional independence required by fair scoring

**Data:**
ECMWF subseasonal forecasting system weekly mean 2-meter temperature (T₂m) forecasts; tested with varying ensemble sizes (3, 9, and 100 members).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical reliability issues in subseasonal climate forecasts, where ensemble size constraints often limit operational forecast skill. Fair and size-independent post-processing directly improves predictability of weekly temperature patterns.

**Implications:**
Enables practical deployment of deep-learning post-processing across different ensemble configurations without retraining; reduces computational requirements for operational climate centers while maintaining forecast reliability and calibration.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to single variable (T₂m) and one forecast system; generalization to other variables, regions, or forecast centers requires further validation.

**TL;DR:**
Trajectory transformers solve ensemble-size dependence in neural post-processing by applying self-attention temporally rather than across ensemble members, maintaining statistical fairness while improving subseasonal forecast skill.

# Research Summary: Generative Deep Learning for Historical Climate Reconstruction

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a probabilistic generative deep learning framework that reconstructs global historical climate records (1850-present) from sparse observations while preserving climate dynamics statistics and uncertainty quantification. Overcomes systematic biases in conventional interpolation methods that smooth local features and underestimate extremes.

**Method:**
Employs a learned generative prior of Earth system dynamics to perform probabilistic inference on sparse instrumental data, recovering spatiotemporally consistent temperature and precipitation fields. The approach maintains higher-order statistical properties of climate variability rather than relying on disparate statistical interpolation.

**Data:**
Historical instrumental observations from the early 20th century (sparse and fragmented); comparison against widely-used reference products including those underlying IPCC assessments.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses a critical gap in climate science: accurate quantification of anthropogenic warming trends and extreme weather variability during the instrumental record period (1850-1980). Provides improved baseline for detecting climate change signals and validating climate models.

**Implications:**
Reveals higher early 20th-century global warming (+0.15–0.29°C/decade Arctic trend above benchmarks for 1900–1980); corrects potential Arctic warming overestimation in modern era while identifying previously unresolved regional hotspots (Barents Sea, Northeastern Greenland); demonstrates intensification of global hydrological cycle with ocean and under-monitored region precipitation recovery.

**Limitations:**
Not explicitly stated; potential concerns include dependence on generative model training data quality and validation against independent observations in data-sparse regions.

**TL;DR:**
Generative deep learning reconstructs global climate records from 1850 onward with improved fidelity to extremes and regional variability, revealing previously undetected early warming and modern regional climate features.

# Agent-Based Macroeconomics for UK Carbon Budget 7

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a data-driven agent-based macroeconomic model (ABM) to quantify the economic impacts (growth, employment, inflation, inequality) of the UK's seventh carbon budget (2038-2042), providing the first integrated macroeconomic assessment accompanying a UK carbon budget decision.

**Method:**
Three-phase approach: (WP1) calibrate UK macro-ABM using household microdata; (WP2) model decarbonization as exogenous shocks to financial flows and technical coefficients, constrained by Climate Change Committee projections; (WP3) implement endogenous social and technological learning to generate alternative decarbonization pathways.

**Data:**
UK household microdata for model initialization; Climate Change Committee projections for green investment and technological change; macroeconomic baseline parameters for UK economy.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports UK Climate Change Act implementation by quantifying macroeconomic trade-offs of meeting legally-binding carbon budgets, enabling evidence-based climate policy design that accounts for distributional impacts.

**Implications:**
Informs June 2026 policy decision on CB7; demonstrates feasibility of integrating ABM-based macroeconomic assessment into climate policymaking; provides framework for evaluating employment and inequality consequences of decarbonization pathways.

**Limitations:**
WP1-2 results are preliminary; WP2 relies on exogenous government projections rather than endogenous modeling; full endogenous learning mechanisms (WP3) not yet implemented; timeline constraints may limit sophistication of final assessment.

**TL;DR:**
Agent-based macroeconomic model quantifies growth, employment, and inequality impacts of UK's 2038-2042 carbon budget to inform climate policy decisions.

# Research Summary: Southern Ocean Latent Heat Flux and Ocean Mesoscales

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies how ocean mesoscale (100 km) and submesoscale (1-10 km) features drive latent heat flux variability over the Southern Ocean, showing these fine-scale processes account for up to 80% of LHF variance in eddy-rich regions—a critical mechanism currently unresolved in climate models and reanalysis products.

**Method:**
Year-long, fully coupled ocean-atmosphere simulation at kilometer-scale resolution with spectral analysis to decompose LHF variance across spatial scales; comparison with ERA5 reanalysis to quantify observational gaps.

**Data:**
Global coupled simulation output; ERA5 reanalysis for validation and gap identification.

**Climate Relevance:**
Latent heat flux is a primary ocean-atmosphere heat and moisture exchange pathway. The Southern Ocean's role in global climate regulation makes understanding air-sea coupling at fine scales essential for accurate climate projections and heat budget closure.

**Implications:**
Current climate models and reanalysis significantly underestimate ocean-driven LHF variance (~17% missing) due to insufficient resolution, potentially biasing atmospheric circulation predictions and regional climate sensitivity estimates. Improved parameterization or higher-resolution modeling of submesoscale processes could enhance climate model fidelity.

**Limitations:**
Single-year simulation; limited to Southern Ocean domain; non-local atmospheric response mechanisms require further investigation.

**TL;DR:**
Kilometer-scale ocean eddies and fronts drive 80% of Southern Ocean latent heat flux variance but remain unresolved in climate models, creating significant gaps in air-sea coupling representation.
