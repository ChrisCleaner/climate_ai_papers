# Research Summary: Koopman Autoencoders for Coastal-Ocean Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a Koopman autoencoder surrogate model for coastal-ocean hydrodynamics that incorporates meteorological forcings and boundary conditions, demonstrating superior long-term predictive accuracy compared to traditional POD-based reduced-order models.

**Method:**
- Koopman autoencoder with learned linear temporal operator in latent space
- Eigenvalue regularization for temporal stability promotion
- Temporal unrolling technique for stable multi-step predictions
- Systematic comparison against POD-based surrogates across three dynamical regimes

**Data:**
Three coastal-ocean test cases with distinct dynamical characteristics; predictions evaluated at 30-minute temporal resolution over horizons up to one year.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables efficient long-term coastal-ocean simulations critical for climate impact assessment, sea-level rise projections, and understanding coastal dynamics under varying meteorological forcing.

**Implications:**
300–1400× speedup over physics-based models facilitates ensemble forecasting and extended climate simulations; prediction errors (0.01–0.13 RMSE, 0.65–0.996 R²) are acceptable for operational coastal management and climate studies, with water-surface-elevation errors of a few centimeters.

**Limitations:**
Largest errors in current velocity predictions; surrogate performance depends on training data representativeness; comparison to observations limited to water-surface elevation.

**TL;DR:**
Koopman autoencoders with temporal unrolling achieve 300–1400× speedup for year-long coastal-ocean predictions with acceptable accuracy for climate and forecasting applications.

### [Data Driven Air Entrainment Velocity Parameterization by Breaking Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04067v1)

**Authors:** Xiaohui Zhou, Anton S. Darmenov, Kianoosh Yousefi
**Published:** 2026-02-03
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph

# Research Summary: Data-Driven Air Entrainment Velocity Parameterization

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a machine-learning parameterization of air-entrainment velocity (Va) from breaking waves that significantly improves representation of air-sea exchange processes in climate models. Replaces traditional single-variable formulas with a physics-informed neural network using seven wave and wind predictors.

**Method:**
Multilayer perceptron trained on 43-year WAVEWATCH III hindcast data with physically motivated inputs (wind speed, wave height, age, steepness, direction, depth). Model learns nonlinear relationships between sea state and bubble-injection dynamics across global ocean conditions.

**Data:**
• WAVEWATCH III 43-year simulation (spectral wave model with resolved breaker-front distribution)
• HiWinGS field observations (independent validation dataset)

**Climate Relevance:**
Wave-breaking processes control air-sea fluxes of CO₂, heat, momentum, and sea-salt aerosols—critical for ocean carbon cycling and aerosol-climate feedbacks. Current models systematically misrepresent these fluxes, introducing biases in climate projections.

**Implications:**
Reduces CO₂ transfer velocity and sea-salt aerosol emission errors by ~order of magnitude. Corrects known regional biases: overestimation in swell-dominated regions and underestimation in storm tracks, improving coupled atmosphere-wave-ocean model fidelity.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to deep-water HiWinGS observations; coastal/shallow-water performance unclear. Dependence on WAVEWATCH III training data may inherit model biases.

**TL;DR:**
Machine-learning model improves ocean bubble parameterization for climate models, reducing air-sea exchange errors by an order of magnitude globally.

# Research Summary: OPTiMEM and Heat Conjecture for Social Cost of Greenhouse Gases

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces OPTiMEM (Ocean-Heat-Content Physics and Time Macro Economic Model), a physics-based framework linking ocean heat accumulation to economic damages, proposing that social cost of carbon (SCC) must be represented as a dynamic "economic phase space" rather than single values.

**Method:**
- Couples fossil fuel consumption functions to CO₂ emissions with 18 uncertainty scenarios
- Applies "heat conjecture": weather damage curves scale stochastically with ocean heat increase
- Implements 1:N year loss risk models (1:10, 1:100, 1:1000) for probabilistic damage assessment
- Proposes long-term carbon bonds as alternative to traditional discount rate approaches, decoupling from pure rate of time preference (PRTP)

**Data:**
Validation against observational datasets for greenhouse gas concentrations, temperature, and ocean heat content (OHC); incorporates DICE model carbon and growth assumptions for scenario comparison.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses the physical mechanism linking cumulative ocean heat to climate damages, providing a mechanistic alternative to traditional economic damage functions that often underestimate climate impacts.

**Implications:**
- Enables risk-based decision-making for policymakers and actuaries through probabilistic loss models
- Highlights feasibility constraints: delayed climate action may exhaust low-cost fossil fuels needed for renewable energy transition
- Suggests SCC estimates vary dramatically across discount scenarios, with DICE-based projections reaching +18°C by 2210 CE

**Limitations:**
- "Heat conjecture" (stochastic proportionality between OHC and damages) requires empirical validation
- Long-term projections (2210–2300 CE) carry substantial uncertainty; extreme temperature outcomes may reflect model sensitivities rather than physical inevitability
- Economic phase space representation, while conceptually novel, may complicate policy implementation

**TL;DR:**
Physics-based model linking ocean heat to economic damages proposes SCC as a dynamic function rather than fixed value, with implications for long-term climate risk assessment and energy transition feasibility.

# Research Summary: Bayesian Inversion for Subsurface Flow Simulation

**Main Contribution:**
Compares variational inference and Monte Carlo methods for estimating subsurface reservoir properties from seismic and pressure data. Introduces physically structured variational inference (PSVI), which exploits spatial correlations to improve computational efficiency in high-dimensional inverse problems.

**Method:**
- Bayesian inversion framework with automatic differentiation variational inference (ADVI), Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD), stochastic SVGD (sSVGD), and Metropolis-Hastings MCMC
- PSVI variant constrains parameter correlations to local spatial neighborhoods, reducing dimensionality
- Benchmarked on 2D and 3D nonlinear inverse problems

**Data:**
Synthetic fluid pressure measurements and seismic observations from CO₂ storage scenarios inspired by the Endurance field (UK North Sea).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports carbon capture and storage (CCS) deployment by enabling accurate characterization of subsurface reservoirs for safe, long-term CO₂ sequestration—a critical climate mitigation technology.

**Implications:**
Enables faster, more accurate reservoir property estimation for CCS site selection and monitoring. PSVI offers practical balance between accuracy and computational cost for operational applications.

**Limitations:**
Tested on synthetic data; validation on real field data not demonstrated. Computational costs for SVGD/sSVGD remain high despite improvements, potentially limiting scalability to larger 3D problems.

**TL;DR:**
PSVI efficiently estimates subsurface permeability for CO₂ storage by exploiting spatial parameter correlations, balancing accuracy and computational speed better than standard variational methods.

# MSWEP V3 Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
MSWEP V3 is the first fully global, machine learning-powered precipitation dataset providing hourly estimates at 0.1° resolution from 1979 to present with ~2-hour latency. It significantly outperforms existing gridded precipitation products (median daily KGE of 0.69 vs. 0.61 for ERA5, 0.46 for IMERG-L).

**Method:**
Two-stage ensemble approach: (1) ML model stacks trained on 15,959 gauge stations integrate satellite and reanalysis precipitation/temperature data with static variables to generate baseline fields; (2) daily and monthly gauge corrections applied using 57,666–86,000 additional stations globally.

**Data:**
Training: 15,959 hourly/daily precipitation gauges; Validation: 15,958 independent gauges; Correction: 57,666 daily and 86,000 monthly gauge observations; Input sources: satellite products, ERA5 reanalysis, IMERG, GSMaP, CHIRP.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need for accurate, timely global precipitation monitoring essential for understanding hydrological cycles, extreme weather attribution, and water resource management under climate change.

**Implications:**
Enables improved drought/flood forecasting, water availability assessments, climate model validation, and operational hydrometeorological services with near-real-time updates.

**Limitations:**
Gauge correction improvements modest (0.09 correlation gain) due to already-strong baseline; performance may vary regionally depending on gauge density; continuous updates require sustained data infrastructure.

**TL;DR:**
ML-ensemble precipitation dataset achieving state-of-the-art global accuracy with hourly resolution and near-real-time delivery for climate and water risk applications.
