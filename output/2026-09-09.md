# Research Summary: Earth System World Model for What-If Simulations

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an action-conditioned machine learning framework that transforms passive Earth-system emulators into interactive, intervention-aware digital twins capable of simulating user-specified "what-if" scenarios without manual intervention annotations.

**Method:**
- **Transition-action pretraining:** Leverages naturally observed state changes as implicit action supervision, enabling the model to learn both prescribed dynamics and intervention responses simultaneously
- **Masked response learning:** Infers unobserved variables under partial state edits and captures coupled system dependencies across multiple ecosystem components

**Data:**
Ecosystem dynamics simulations across six global regions with multiple stand ages (specific simulator/dataset not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need for interactive Earth-system surrogates in climate adaptation and mitigation planning, enabling rapid exploration of ecosystem responses to management interventions (e.g., reforestation, land-use changes) without expensive full simulations.

**Implications:**
- Accelerates scientific workflows requiring iterative scenario exploration
- Enables development of Earth-system digital twins for decision support
- Maintains emulation accuracy while adding controllability for policy and management applications

**Limitations:**
- Evaluation limited to terrestrial ecosystems; generalization to other Earth-system components (ocean, atmosphere) unclear
- Reliance on quality of naturally observed state transitions as supervision signal

**TL;DR:**
An action-conditioned world model enables interactive, intervention-aware ecosystem emulation for rapid what-if scenario exploration without manual intervention labels.

# Neptune: AI Model for Global Ocean Subseasonal Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Neptune is a data-driven neural network framework that emulates global ocean and sea-ice dynamics for subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting up to 60 days ahead. It replaces computationally expensive physics-based Ocean General Circulation Models with a hybrid deep learning approach capable of high-resolution predictions.

**Method:**
Combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for local feature extraction with Spherical Fourier Neural Operators (SFNOs) to capture global cross-scale interactions on the spherical Earth. Two variants provided: Neptune-1 (1° resolution) and Neptune-025 (0.25° resolution), both producing daily outputs across multiple ocean variables and depths.

**Data:**
Trained on historical ocean state variables (temperature, salinity, currents, sea surface height, sea ice properties) forced by prescribed daily atmospheric fields; specific datasets not detailed in abstract.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical S2S prediction timescale (2-60 days) essential for understanding ocean-atmosphere coupling and predictability windows relevant to climate variability phenomena like ENSO and Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD).

**Implications:**
Enables rapid, high-resolution ocean forecasting for water management, agriculture, disaster risk reduction, energy planning, and insurance sectors; reduces computational barriers to operational S2S forecasting systems.

**Limitations:**
Relies on prescribed atmospheric forcing (not coupled to atmospheric models); long-term stability claims require validation beyond 60-day window; generalization to extreme events unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Neptune uses CNNs and Fourier neural operators to emulate global ocean dynamics at high resolution for subseasonal forecasting, offering a computationally efficient alternative to traditional circulation models.

**Data:**
• Training: 32 CMIP6 Earth system models + observational/reanalysis data
• Output: Public dataset covering SSP scenarios (1.2–6.1°C warming range), Paris pledges (1.5–3.3°C), and CMIP7 scenarios (1.2–4.2°C)

**Climate Relevance:**

**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: Macroturbulence Scaling Theory for Planetary Atmospheres

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a new scaling theory for atmospheric eddy heat flux that works across weakly supercritical regimes where existing theories fail. The theory successfully predicts both domain-averaged and local eddy behavior without requiring an inverse energy cascade.

**Method:**
- Scaling analysis of zonal momentum balance combined with kinematic mixing effects
- Introduces thermal Rossby number as key non-dimensional parameter
- Accounts for nonlinear eddy momentum flux contributions across jets
- Validated against 100+ idealized GCM simulations with varying rotation rates, temperature gradients, stratification, and seasonality

**Data:**
Hundreds of dry general circulation model simulations spanning wide parameter ranges; validation against simple energy balance model predictions of global near-surface temperature distributions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Eddy heat flux drives the surface branch of atmospheric circulation and controls meridional heat transport—fundamental to understanding Earth's climate dynamics. Current theories assume supercritical conditions rarely met in real planetary atmospheres, limiting predictive capability.

**Implications:**
Provides theoretical foundation for improved general circulation models and climate projections. Framework potentially extends to moist atmospheres, enhancing weather and climate prediction across diverse planetary states.

**Limitations:**
Analysis restricted to dry atmospheres; moisture effects remain unexplored. Applicability to highly supercritical or subcritical regimes unclear.

**TL;DR:**
New scaling theory explains atmospheric eddy heat flux across realistic marginally-critical regimes using thermal Rossby number, improving general circulation theory foundations.

**Method:**

- Emulator: Machine learning model trained to reproduce land-ocean warming response across CMIP6 ensemble
- Analysis: Decomposition of radiative feedbacks and MSE-transport feedbacks by surface type (land vs. ocean)

**Data:**
CMIP6 coupled climate model ensemble (22 models); surface air temperature, radiative fluxes, and atmospheric circulation fields from model simulations under greenhouse forcing scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**
- Provides diagnostic framework for evaluating climate model fidelity in simulating differential warming

- Suggests that model spread reflects differences in radiative feedback strength rather than fundamental dynamical disagreement

**TL;DR:**
Land warms faster than ocean due to interplay between radiative feedbacks and atmospheric energy transport, with the balance determining model climate sensitivity.

# PCSDiff: Diffusion-Based Precipitation Forecast Correction

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces PCSDiff, a cascaded diffusion framework that simultaneously corrects systematic biases in medium-range precipitation forecasts and downscales them to higher spatial resolution while preserving physically realistic rainfall patterns over 10-day lead times.

**Method:**
- **Bias Correction:** Precipitation Intensity-aware Multi-branch Decoder (PIMD) module captures synoptic-temporal features to model lead-time-dependent error evolution across multiple days
- **Super-Resolution:** Two-phase conditional diffusion model reconstructs fine-scale precipitation details while maintaining meteorological constraints
- **Architecture:** Task-decoupled cascaded design separates bias mitigation from spatial downscaling; streaming inference pipeline enables operational deployment

**Data:**
Global training data; evaluation against CMA-CRA observations over China; tested on ECMWF medium-range forecasts (3–10-day lead times).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gaps in operational precipitation forecasting essential for flood-drought risk assessment and water resource management; improved forecast skill directly impacts climate adaptation and disaster preparedness.

**Implications:**
Enables practical operational deployment with low-latency rolling forecasts; 16.1% RMSE reduction and 13.9% anomaly correlation improvement over raw forecasts; consistent gains on extreme-precipitation events critical for early warning systems.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to China region; generalization to other climate zones unclear; computational cost of diffusion models for operational systems not detailed.

**TL;DR:**
A diffusion-based AI framework corrects and downscales 10-day precipitation forecasts with improved accuracy and physical realism, ready for operational meteorological deployment.

- Non-stationary Generalized Extreme Value (GEV) distributions fitted to surface temperature extremes
- Linear and non-linear regression models relating GEV parameters to AMOC strength
- Regional analysis identifying physical mechanisms driving extremal behavior changes

**Data:**
CCSM4 Last Glacial Maximum simulation exhibiting stadial-interstadial switching dynamics; validated against paleoclimate proxies for air and water temperature.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses how rapid climate reorganizations (like those potentially triggered by AMOC weakening) alter the statistics of dangerous cold extremes—critical for understanding past abrupt climate events and informing future climate risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Provides a quantitative framework for comparing extreme value statistics across climate models and states; enables prediction of regional extreme cold changes based on AMOC monitoring, with applications to paleoclimate reconstruction and climate model validation.

**Limitations:**
Results are model-dependent (single CCSM4 simulation); regional teleconnections require careful interpretation; transitions between climate states show non-linear behavior complicating predictive generalization.

**TL;DR:**
GEV-based statistical modeling reveals that extreme cold temperatures during abrupt climate transitions scale linearly with AMOC strength, enabling quantitative cross-model comparisons of regional extremal behavior.
