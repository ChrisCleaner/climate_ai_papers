### [Evaluating the Generalizability of Foundation Models for Extreme Environmental Events: Case Study of California Wildfire PM2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07951v1)

**Authors:** Yongcan Huang, Li Jiang, Ze Yu Liu
**Published:** 2026-07-08
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Foundation Models for Wildfire PM2.5 Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**  
First systematic benchmark evaluating time series foundation models (TSFMs) against traditional deep learning baselines for extreme PM2.5 forecasting during wildfire smoke events, revealing that larger pretrained models do not universally outperform smaller, fully-trained recurrent networks on this critical environmental task.

**Method:**  
Compared six TSFM configurations (TimesFM, Chronos-2, Moirai-2, Time-MoE—both zero-shot and LoRA fine-tuned) against LSTM/BiLSTM/Transformer baselines using leave-one-incident-out (LOIO) cross-validation. Evaluated performance via MAE, RMSE, and exceedance F1 scores at EPA AQI thresholds across 6–24 hour forecast horizons.

**Data:**  
12-year hourly PM2.5 measurements (2013–2025) from 79 California air quality monitoring sites covering 1,375 wildfire incidents.

**Climate Relevance:**  
Addresses critical gap in forecasting rare, hazardous-level air pollution extremes driven by climate-amplified wildfire activity, directly supporting public health decision-making during smoke events.

**Implications:**  
BiLSTM achieves superior performance (MAE 5.16 μg/m³, exceedance F1 0.63 at Hazardous threshold) compared to all foundation models (max F1 0.54). Suggests domain-specific fine-tuning of smaller models may be more practical than deploying large pretrained models for operational wildfire air quality forecasting.

**Limitations:**  
Single-region focus (California); unclear whether findings generalize to other geographic regions or pollutants; zero-shot TSFM instability may reflect suboptimal hyperparameters rather than fundamental model limitations.

**TL;DR:**  
Specialized recurrent networks outperform foundation models for wildfire PM2.5 forecasting, challenging assumptions about pretrained model superiority in extreme environmental prediction.

# Research Summary: Domain-Adaptive Climate Downscaling Under Temporal Distribution Shift

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes a domain-adaptive deep learning framework that addresses temporal distribution shift in climate downscaling by aligning historical and future climate distributions. Demonstrates improved robustness when applying models trained on historical data to future climate projections.

**Method:**
Combines supervised high-resolution (HR) reconstruction on historical data with domain alignment techniques to bridge the distributional gap between training (historical) and application (future) periods. Uses paired low-resolution (LR) and HR climate model simulations from the Continental United States.

**Data:**
Paired LR-HR climate model simulations over the Continental United States; evaluated across multiple future validation periods to assess temporal OOD performance.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical operational challenge in climate science: downscaling coarse-resolution global climate models to decision-relevant local scales while accounting for non-stationary climate conditions. Essential for reliable regional climate projections under anthropogenic forcing.

**Implications:**
Enables more trustworthy high-resolution climate projections for regional impact assessments, water resource planning, and climate adaptation strategies. Particularly valuable for complex terrain where topographic effects amplify downscaling challenges.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to temperature over one geographic region; generalization to other variables (precipitation, wind) and global domains unclear. Computational cost and scalability not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Domain adaptation mitigates temporal distribution shift in neural climate downscaling, improving projection reliability for future periods with substantially different climate statistics.

### [A harmonised dataset for Earth system foundation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03298v1)

**Authors:** Carlos Rodriguez-Pardo, Massimo Tavoni
**Published:** 2026-07-03
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, econ.GN
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# WorldTensor: A Harmonised Dataset for Earth System Foundation Models

**Main Contribution:**
WorldTensor is a unified global dataset that harmonises hundreds of environmental and socioeconomic variables onto a standardised 0.25° spatial grid with annual temporal resolution, enabling training of multimodal Earth system foundation models that integrate human and natural systems.

**Method:**
Regridding heterogeneous datasets to common spatial/temporal frameworks; rasterisation of point/vector data; temporal reconciliation across daily-to-multiyear coverages; standardised NetCDF output with CF metadata conventions for machine learning compatibility.

**Data:**
Reanalysis products, remote sensing, emissions inventories, land use reconstructions, hydrological observations, infrastructure datasets, hazard inventories, and socioeconomic indicators.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in foundation model training by coupling physical climate-weather data with human system drivers (emissions, land use, infrastructure, socioeconomics), enabling models to capture bidirectional climate-society interactions at planetary scale.

**Implications:**
Provides reproducible infrastructure for training next-generation Earth system AI models capable of understanding coupled human-environment dynamics; supports improved climate impact assessments and policy evaluation across sectors.

**Limitations:**
Temporal resolution (annual) may obscure sub-annual climate variability; sparse socioeconomic snapshots limit temporal continuity of human system data; regridding introduces interpolation artifacts requiring careful validation.

**TL;DR:**
WorldTensor standardises 400+ environmental and socioeconomic variables onto a common grid to enable foundation models that learn coupled human-natural Earth system dynamics.

# Research Summary: Microseismicity and Lithology via Slip Avalanche Models

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that a universal micromechanical model of slip avalanches predicts microseismic event statistics across vastly different spatial scales (nanometers to meters) and diverse geological materials, enabling prediction of induced earthquake sizes and frequencies.

**Method:**
• Physics-based slip avalanche model applied to two experimental systems: nanoindentation (nanometer-micrometer scale) and microseismic catalogs (meter scale)
• Statistical comparison of observed slip distributions against model predictions across varying lithologies, temperatures, and mineral compositions
• Mechanistic explanation of deviations in layered formations through finite-size and stress-integration effects at bedding planes

**Data:**
• Microseismic catalogs from fluid injection operations in geological basins with varied lithologies
• Nanoindentation experiments on shale samples across temperature ranges and mineral compositions
• High-pressure injection field data from unconventional oil/gas and geothermal operations

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to geologic carbon storage (CCS) safety and enhanced geothermal systems (EGS)—both critical climate mitigation technologies requiring induced seismicity prediction and risk management.

**Implications:**
Enables probabilistic forecasting of induced earthquake magnitudes; provides diagnostic tool for characterizing subsurface bedding plane structure; supports operational safety protocols for CO₂ injection and geothermal development.

**Limitations:**
Model validation limited to specific lithologies; real-world applicability depends on accurate characterization of basin structure and stress conditions; temporal prediction remains challenging.

**TL;DR:**
A universal slip avalanche model predicts induced microseismic statistics across scales and materials, improving safety for carbon storage and geothermal energy operations.
