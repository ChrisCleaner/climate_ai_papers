### [AIMIP Phase 1: systematic evaluations of AI weather and climate models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06944v1)

**Authors:** Brian Henn, Christopher S. Bretherton, Nikolay Kodunov...
**Published:** 2026-05-07
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# AIMIP Phase 1 Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Establishes the first systematic intercomparison framework for AI-based weather and climate models, analogous to traditional climate model intercomparison projects (CMIPs). Provides standardized evaluation protocols and publicly available datasets to assess AI model reliability and identify architectural differences affecting performance.

**Method:**
- Common experimental design: AI models simulate atmospheric conditions forced by historical sea surface temperatures (1979-2024)
- Standardized training constraints: models trained exclusively on reanalysis data
- Five evaluation metrics: biases, trends, El Niño response, temporal variability, out-of-sample generalization
- Comparative analysis against conventional physics-based models

**Data:**
Historical reanalysis data (1979-2024) with specified sea surface temperature forcing; AIMIP Phase 1 dataset publicly released for community evaluation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Validates whether AI models can reproduce observed climate statistics, long-term warming trends, and climate system responses to natural forcing (ENSO), which are critical for climate projections and attribution studies.

**Implications:**
Enables broader adoption of computationally efficient AI models in operational weather/climate prediction if performance parity with physics-based models is demonstrated; identifies which AI architectures are most trustworthy for climate applications.

**Limitations:**
Some AI models underestimate historical warming trends; significant divergence in out-of-sample generalization suggests potential extrapolation failures beyond training conditions; Phase 1 limited to atmosphere-only simulations with prescribed SSTs.

**TL;DR:**
Systematic evaluation of AI climate models reveals comparable performance to physics-based models in many metrics but highlights concerns about trend representation and generalization beyond training data.

### [Interpretable Neural Networks to Predict Momentum Fluxes of Orographic Gravity Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05052v1)

**Authors:** Elias Haslauer, Mierk Schwabe, Andreas Dörnbrack...
**Published:** 2026-05-06
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Neural Networks for Orographic Gravity Wave Parameterization

**Main Contribution:**
Develops interpretable neural networks to predict momentum fluxes from orographic gravity waves at Earth system model (ESM) resolution, demonstrating that ML can improve subgrid-scale parameterizations with physically meaningful learned relationships.

**Method:**
- Trains neural networks on ERA5 reanalysis data coarse-grained to ESM resolution
- Uses MODES software to extract inertia-gravity waves via linear wave theory filtering
- Applies SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for interpretability analysis
- Evaluates four cases: full/subgrid-scale wave spectra over all land/mountainous terrain only
- Compares performance against physics-based Lott-Miller parameterization scheme

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis (one full year for training, separate year for offline evaluation); coarse-grained to typical ESM grid resolution.

**Climate Relevance:**
Gravity waves drive upper-atmosphere dynamics and affect momentum transport in climate models; current ESMs cannot explicitly resolve these small-scale processes, making accurate parameterization critical for climate projections.

**Implications:**
ML-based parameterizations could operationally replace or improve traditional physics-based schemes, enhancing ESM fidelity for gravity wave effects on resolved-scale atmospheric circulation.

**Limitations:**
Offline evaluation only (not coupled to ESM); limited to one year of training data; performance varies significantly by case (R² = 0.56–0.72); generalization to future climates untested.

**TL;DR:**
Neural networks trained on reanalysis data successfully predict gravity wave momentum fluxes at climate model resolution with interpretable, physically consistent behavior.

### [Prediction of Drought and Flash Drought in Africa at the Seasonal-to-Subseasonal Scale using the Community Research Earth Digital Intelligence Twin Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05255v1)

**Authors:** Stuart Edris, Amy McGovern, Jason Hickey
**Published:** 2026-05-05
**Categories:** stat.AP, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: DroughtFormer for African Drought Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Develops DroughtFormer, a CrossFormer-based deep learning model within the CREDIT framework to predict drought and flash drought across Africa at seasonal-to-subseasonal timescales. Addresses the gap in ML drought prediction capability for data-sparse African regions where drought impacts are most severe.

**Method:**
Transformer-based architecture (CrossFormer) incorporating physical constraints (dry air mass, moisture conservation) to predict soil moisture, vegetation health, and drought indices. Model trained on multi-source reanalysis and satellite data with 90-day forecast horizon.

**Data:**
ERA5 and GLDAS2 reanalyses; IMERG and MODIS satellite observations; drought-related surface variables and indices.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses drought prediction in Africa, where agricultural-dependent populations face existential threats from both slow-onset droughts and rapidly developing flash droughts. Extends recent ML weather prediction advances (GraphCast, AIFS) to underrepresented regions.

**Implications:**
Enables early warning systems for agricultural planning and food security in vulnerable African regions; demonstrates feasibility of skillful drought forecasting to 90-day lead times, supporting climate adaptation strategies.

**Limitations:**
Lower accuracy in precipitation and flash drought index prediction; struggles with anomaly magnitude representation despite skillful anomaly patterns; sparse observational data in Africa may constrain model generalization.

**TL;DR:**
A physics-informed transformer model successfully predicts African drought conditions 90 days ahead, showing promise for agricultural early warning despite challenges in precipitation forecasting.

# Research Summary: Diffusion Model Climate Emulators for Extreme Event Likelihoods

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates how diffusion model climate emulators (specifically Climate in a Bottle/cBottle) can estimate probability densities of atmospheric states to quantify extreme event likelihoods under conditional guidance. Introduces importance sampling methods to reduce sampling error when estimating rare event probabilities.

**Method:**
• Leverages cBottle diffusion model trained on atmospheric data conditioned on boundary conditions (solar position, sea surface temperatures)
• Uses guided diffusion to steer generation toward extreme events (tropical cyclones)
• Compares probability densities between guided and unguided model outputs to calculate odds ratios
• Applies importance sampling to reduce variance in probability estimates versus standard Monte Carlo

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; training data appears to be historical atmospheric states compatible with specified boundary conditions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need for efficient extreme event attribution and likelihood estimation under climate scenarios. Enables cost-effective exploration of TC occurrence probabilities without expensive full climate simulations, supporting climate adaptation planning.

**Implications:**
Enables rapid scenario planning and risk assessment for extreme weather events; probabilistic framework could improve climate impact projections and inform adaptation strategies for vulnerable regions.

**Limitations:**
Authors acknowledge early-stage results; applicability of model probability densities to attribution experiments remains uncertain; generalization to other extreme event types unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Diffusion model climate emulators can estimate extreme event likelihoods through guided generation and probability density comparison, enabling efficient importance sampling for rare event attribution.

**Published:** 2026-05-08
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph

# GPROF-IR Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces GPROF-IR, a CNN-based infrared precipitation retrieval that improves single-channel IR estimates by leveraging temporal information from half-hourly geostationary observations. Designed for integration into IMERG V08 to enhance consistency between PMW and IR retrievals in merged satellite precipitation products.

**Method:**
Convolutional neural network that exploits spatial and temporal information in geostationary IR observations. Model trained to be climatologically consistent with GPROF-NN (PMW retrieval), enabling better integration into merged precipitation frameworks.

**Data:**
Geostationary IR observations (half-hourly resolution from 1998 onward); validated against independent global reference measurements; compared with IMERG V07, GSMAP, and CMORPH products.

**Climate Relevance:**
Accurate global precipitation estimation is critical for climate monitoring, weather forecasting, and hydrological modeling. Improved consistency between satellite sensors reduces artifacts in precipitation time series used for climate analysis.

**Implications:**
Enables quasi-global precipitation estimates at 30-minute resolution spanning 25+ years, providing a consistent observational foundation for climate studies, extreme weather analysis, and model validation without reliance on time-propagated microwave data.

**Limitations:**
Performance remains below PMW accuracy over oceans and in shallow precipitation regimes; improvements primarily evident over continental landmasses.

**TL;DR:**
CNN-based IR precipitation retrieval improves single-channel satellite estimates through temporal information exploitation, advancing merged precipitation products for climate applications.

### [Cascading disruptions in natural gas, fertilizers, and crops drive structural food supply vulnerabilities globally](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06411v1)

**Authors:** Pavel Kiparisov, Christian Folberth
**Published:** 2026-05-07
**Categories:** physics.soc-ph, econ.GN
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Cascading Food Supply Vulnerabilities

**Main Contribution:**
Develops the first integrated cascading-impact model linking natural gas, fertilizer, and crop supply chains to quantify global food security vulnerabilities. Demonstrates that complete trade isolation would eliminate up to 22% of global caloric consumption, with structural vulnerabilities increasing substantially since the 1990s.

**Method:**
Cascading-impact network model integrating bilateral trade flows across three supply chain layers (natural gas → fertilizers → crops). Analyzes shock propagation from upstream to downstream sectors across 208 countries and 20 geopolitical blocs (1992-2023). Evaluates systemic resilience under complete trade isolation scenarios.

**Data:**
Bilateral trade data for natural gas, nitrogen/phosphorus/potassium fertilizers, and 11 staple crops (1992-2023); 208 countries; food stock inventories by nation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Identifies critical infrastructure dependencies underlying climate-vulnerable food systems. Reveals how energy-intensive fertilizer production and geopolitical gas supply concentration create climate-adaptation barriers, particularly relevant for understanding food security under climate-driven supply disruptions.

**Implications:**
Pinpoints upstream gas/fertilizer markets as systemic bottlenecks requiring policy intervention. Suggests targeted resilience strategies: diversifying fertilizer sourcing, building strategic food reserves (currently <3 months for half of humanity), and reducing supply chain concentration.

**Limitations:**
Assumes complete trade isolation (unrealistic scenario); doesn't model partial disruptions or dynamic market responses; temporal coverage ends 2023; climate impacts on production capacity not explicitly modeled.

**TL;DR:**
Global food security faces critical vulnerabilities from concentrated upstream energy and fertilizer supplies, with cascading risks amplifying downstream to crops.
