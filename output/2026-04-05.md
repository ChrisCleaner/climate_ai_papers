### [Gray Swan Factory: Making Extreme Events from Ordinary Cyclones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00348v1)

**Authors:** Gregory J. Hakim, Aishwarya Agrawal
**Published:** 2026-04-01
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, nlin.CD
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Gray Swan Factory: Making Extreme Events from Ordinary Cyclones

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a gradient descent optimization method using differentiable weather prediction models to generate "gray swans"—plausible but unobserved extreme weather events. Demonstrates the approach by transforming hurricane Fiona (2022) into Sandy-like scenarios with intensified pressure and altered tracks.

**Method:**
• Gradient descent optimization on a differentiable weather model loss function
• Constrains initial condition perturbations to realistic magnitudes
• Identifies perturbations that produce target extreme outcomes at future times
• Applied to reanalysis data with 5-day forecast horizon

**Data:**
Reanalysis initial conditions and observational records of Atlantic hurricanes (Fiona 2022 and four additional cases); comparison against historical hurricane Sandy (2012).

**Climate Relevance:**
Extends understanding of extreme cyclone hazards beyond the limited observational record (~50 years), critical for assessing tail-risk events in a changing climate and informing infrastructure vulnerability to compound storm scenarios.

**Implications:**
• Emergency preparedness and scenario planning for unprecedented storm tracks
• Infrastructure design standards based on physically plausible extremes
• Insurance and hazard assessment for rare but consequential events
• Synthetic training data generation for machine learning applications

**Limitations:**
Hurricane core not resolved by the optimization model (impact unknown); method produces plausible solutions but does not quantify probability of occurrence; lacks probabilistic framework for risk assessment.

**TL;DR:**
Uses differentiable weather models with gradient descent to generate physically plausible extreme hurricane scenarios from historical storms, enabling hazard assessment beyond observational records.

### [ACE2-NEMO: Coupling an ML atmospheric emulator to a full-depth dynamical ocean model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28704v1)

**Published:** 2026-03-30
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph

# ACE2-NEMO: ML Atmospheric Emulator Coupled to Ocean Model

**Main Contribution:**
First multi-decadal (70-year) coupled climate simulations integrating a machine-learned atmospheric model (ACE2) with a full-depth dynamical ocean model (NEMO). This represents a novel test of ML atmospheric emulators' viability in coupled Earth-system modeling.

**Method:**
Hybrid dynamical-ML framework coupling ACE2 (trained ML emulator of atmospheric physics) to NEMO ocean model via interactive air-sea exchange. Simulations span 1950-2020 historical forcing and fixed-1950s control runs, compared against EC-Earth3P full GCM outputs.

**Data:**
70-year coupled simulations (historical + control); EC-Earth3P reference simulations for validation; tropical Pacific observations implicitly referenced for El Niño assessment.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses fundamental question of how fast atmospheric variability influences slow climate modes (El Niño, decadal oscillations) and climate sensitivity to CO₂ forcing—critical for understanding Earth-system feedbacks.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates feasibility of replacing computationally expensive atmospheric components with ML emulators in next-generation climate models, potentially enabling faster ensemble predictions and sensitivity studies while maintaining ocean-atmosphere coupling physics.

**Limitations:**
Weak tropical Pacific atmospheric feedback produces unrealistic El Niño amplitude; reduced shortwave radiation in ACE2 causes CO₂ response divergence from EC-Earth3P; emergent behavior of ML components in coupled systems remains partially unexplained.

**TL;DR:**
ML atmospheric emulator coupled to ocean model produces realistic air-sea coupling but reveals deficiencies in tropical feedback and radiation that must be resolved before operational use in climate projections.

### [Calibrated Conformal Prediction Intervals for Microphysical Process Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27699v1)

**Authors:** Miriam Simm, Corinna Hoose, Tom Beucler
**Published:** 2026-03-29
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Calibrated Conformal Prediction for Microphysical Processes

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that conformal prediction methods can provide statistically valid, well-calibrated uncertainty quantification for ML emulators of atmospheric microphysical processes without requiring model retraining. Shows conformalized quantile regression outperforms split conformal prediction for climate variables spanning multiple orders of magnitude.

**Method:**
- Split conformal prediction: wraps deterministic ML emulators with prediction intervals
- Conformalized quantile regression: applies conformal calibration to quantile regression models
- Both methods guarantee marginal coverage without modifying underlying emulators

**Data:**
ML emulators trained on simulation output from ICON (ICOsahedral Nonhydrostatic) model in limited-area numerical weather prediction configuration; applied to six microphysical process rates.

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates govern cloud formation, precipitation, and aerosol-cloud interactions—critical for accurate weather prediction and climate modeling. Reliable uncertainty quantification improves model credibility for climate projections.

**Implications:**
Enables rapid deployment of ML emulators in operational weather/climate systems with quantified confidence bounds; reduces computational cost while maintaining statistical rigor compared to ensemble methods.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to ICON model outputs; generalization to other climate models or observational data not demonstrated; computational efficiency gains over alternatives not explicitly quantified.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal prediction methods provide computationally efficient, statistically valid uncertainty intervals for climate ML emulators, with conformalized quantile regression preferred for multi-scale atmospheric variables.

# Research Summary: LST Remote Influences on SST Patterns

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that regional land surface temperature (LST) changes exert remote influences on sea surface temperature (SST) patterns through atmospheric teleconnections. Identifies specific land regions where LST warming triggers dynamically consistent ocean responses, particularly in the tropical Pacific.

**Method:**
- Coupled ocean-land-atmosphere model simulations
- LST nudging experiments (prescribed regional LST perturbations)
- Idealized time-dependent ramp warming scenarios
- Stationary Rossby wave analysis to diagnose dynamical pathways
- Historical simulations with observed LST constraints

**Data:**
Observational LST data used to constrain historical model simulations; model outputs from coupled climate simulations (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
SST patterns are primary drivers of global climate variability and regional weather. Understanding LST-SST teleconnections is critical for explaining model biases in tropical ocean states and improving seasonal-to-decadal climate predictions.

**Implications:**
- LST uncertainty may contribute to persistent SST biases in climate models
- Land-use and land-cover changes could have underestimated impacts on ocean circulation
- Improved LST representation may enhance predictability of ENSO-like variability

**Limitations:**
Geographically selective responses (Maritime Continent and Tibetan Plateau show minimal effects); mechanism may be model-dependent; historical cooling implications remain tentative.

**TL;DR:**
Regional land warming drives remote SST changes via atmospheric waves, with implications for climate model biases and predictability.
