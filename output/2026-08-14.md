### [Paleoclimate Boundary Conditions as an Out-of-Sample Test for the Forced Response of Ocean Climate Emulators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13494v1)

**Authors:** Adam Subel, Laure Zanna
**Published:** 2026-08-13
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Paleoclimate Boundary Conditions as Out-of-Sample Tests for Ocean Climate Emulators

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that autoregressive neural network emulators trained on short-term ocean dynamics can generalize to novel orbital forcings in paleoclimate settings, but reveals critical failures in reproducing slow interior ocean evolution and internal variability amplitudes.

**Method:**
Autoregressive, full-depth ocean emulators trained on short time steps; validated against midHolocene climate model experiments. Includes baseline inference models and linear superposition analysis of individual forcing component responses.

**Data:**
MidHolocene experiment outputs from a numerical climate model; in-distribution, out-of-sample climate scenario with different orbital forcings than training data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Tests whether AI emulators can reliably project forced ocean responses across paleoclimate timescales (months to centuries), essential for understanding climate sensitivity and ocean circulation changes under different forcing scenarios.

**Implications:**
Paleoclimate experiments provide controlled validation environments for climate emulators before deployment on future projections. Reveals that training convergence on mean-state metrics does not guarantee capture of dynamical processes needed for accurate forced responses.

**Limitations:**
Emulators underestimate response amplitudes; fail to reproduce internally driven ocean interior evolution; validation limited to single paleoclimate experiment; unclear transferability to truly out-of-distribution future climates.

**TL;DR:**
Paleoclimate validation reveals that ocean emulators capture large-scale forced responses to novel forcings but fail on slow dynamics and variability, requiring improved training objectives beyond short-term accuracy metrics.

# Research Summary: SST-Prescribed Simulations and Earth's Climate Feedback

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that the standard prescribed sea-surface temperature (SST) method for estimating Earth's climate feedback parameter is fundamentally limited by statistical noise and missing atmospheric forcing, making it unreliable for detecting temporal changes in feedback over observational timescales.

**Method:**
• Controlled experiments prescribing SSTs from fully-coupled historical simulations into an atmospheric-only model
• Moving-window regression analysis to detect temporal feedback variations
• 4000-year pre-industrial control simulation to assess noise thresholds
• Comparison of prescribed-SST results against coupled model evolution

**Data:**
Historical climate simulations with prescribed SSTs; pre-industrial control run output; observed SST records (implied).

**Climate Relevance:**
Earth's feedback parameter directly determines climate sensitivity—the warming response to doubled CO₂. Accurate feedback estimates are essential for constraining climate projections and understanding how SST patterns influence global temperature response to forcing.

**Implications:**
Prescribed-SST experiments cannot reliably infer how changing ocean patterns affect climate feedback on decadal-to-centennial scales. Alternative methods (e.g., fully-coupled simulations or longer observational windows) are needed for robust feedback attribution.

**Limitations:**
• Findings apply specifically to timescales <~100 years; longer-term trends may be detectable
• Limited to atmospheric models; results may not generalize to other modeling frameworks
• Assumes statistical noise dominates over genuine SST-feedback coupling

**TL;DR:**
Prescribed-SST simulations cannot reliably detect feedback changes over observational timescales due to statistical noise and missing atmospheric forcing.
