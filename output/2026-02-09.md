### [Toward generative machine learning for boosting ensembles of climate simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06287v1)

**Authors:** Parsa Gooya, Reinel Sospedra-Alfonso, Johannes Exenberger
**Published:** 2026-02-06
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Generative ML for Climate Ensemble Boosting

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) to generate large synthetic climate ensembles from limited physics-based model simulations, addressing the computational trade-off between ensemble size and model resolution.

**Method:**
- Conditional VAE trained on CMIP6 climate model output
- Generates physically consistent samples reproducing realistic statistical moments and extremes
- Incorporates output noise to improve multiscale variability representation
- Chosen for mathematical transparency and computational efficiency over more complex generative architectures

**Data:**
CanESM5 Earth system model output from CMIP6 historical and future scenario experiments (monthly resolution).

**Climate Relevance:**
Quantifying uncertainty from internal climate variability is essential for robust climate projections and decision-making; the method enables higher-resolution ensemble uncertainty assessment without proportional computational cost increases.

**Implications:**
Enables generation of arbitrarily large ensembles for improved uncertainty quantification in climate projections; demonstrates realistic capture of global teleconnection patterns even under unseen climate conditions, supporting climate impact assessments.

**Limitations:**
Overly smooth outputs, spectral bias, and underdispersion; mitigation strategies proposed but not fully resolved; generative approach trades some physical rigor for computational gain.

**TL;DR:**
A conditional VAE efficiently generates large synthetic climate ensembles from limited simulations, improving uncertainty quantification while maintaining physical consistency.

# Research Summary: Koopman Autoencoders for Coastal-Ocean Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a Koopman autoencoder surrogate model that incorporates meteorological forcings and boundary conditions for coastal-ocean dynamics, demonstrating superior long-term prediction accuracy compared to POD-based alternatives over multi-year timescales.

**Method:**
- Koopman autoencoder with learned linear temporal operator in latent space
- Eigenvalue regularization for temporal stability promotion
- Temporal unrolling technique for stable long-term predictions
- Systematic comparison against POD-based surrogates across three distinct dynamical regimes

**Data:**
Three coastal-ocean test cases with prediction horizons up to one year at 30-minute temporal resolution; validation against in-situ observations.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables efficient long-term coastal-ocean simulations critical for climate impact assessment, sea-level rise projections, and understanding forced hydrodynamic responses to meteorological variability.

**Implications:**
300–1400× inference speedup facilitates ensemble forecasting and extended climate simulations; prediction errors (0.01–0.13 RMSE, 0.65–0.996 R²) of few centimeters are acceptable for operational coastal management and climate modeling workflows.

**Limitations:**
Largest errors in current velocity predictions; surrogate performance relative to physics-based models varies by variable type; generalization to novel forcing regimes not explicitly addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Koopman autoencoders with temporal unrolling provide accurate, computationally efficient surrogates for year-long coastal-ocean predictions, enabling practical climate and forecasting applications.

### [Data Driven Air Entrainment Velocity Parameterization by Breaking Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04067v1)

**Published:** 2026-02-03
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Data-Driven Air Entrainment Velocity Parameterization

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a machine-learning parameterization of air-entrainment velocity (Va) from breaking waves that significantly improves representation of air-sea exchange processes in climate models. Replaces traditional single-variable formulas with a physics-informed neural network using seven predictors.

**Method:**
Multilayer perceptron trained on 43-year WAVEWATCH III simulation data with physically motivated inputs (wind speed, wave height, wave age, steepness, direction, depth). Model reproduces spectral-reference Va with high skill and validates against independent HiWinGS observations.

**Data:**
• 43-year WAVEWATCH III global wave simulation (training)
• HiWinGS field observations (validation)
• Resolves breaker-front distribution and energetics

**Climate Relevance:**
Wave breaking drives critical air-sea fluxes (momentum, heat, CO2, sea-spray aerosols) that regulate ocean-atmosphere coupling and climate feedbacks. Current parameterizations in coupled models introduce substantial biases, particularly in swell-dominated and storm regions.

**Implications:**
• Reduces CO2 transfer velocity errors by ~order of magnitude
• Improves sea-salt aerosol emission estimates
• Corrects systematic overestimation in low latitudes and underestimation in storm tracks
• Enables more accurate climate projections of ocean carbon cycling and aerosol forcing

**Limitations:**
Deep-water validation only; coastal/shallow-water applicability unclear. Generalization to future climate states not explicitly addressed.

**TL;DR:**
ML model trained on wave simulations parameterizes air-entrainment velocity with seven predictors, dramatically improving air-sea exchange representation in climate models.

# Research Summary: OPTiMEM and Heat Conjecture for Social Cost of Greenhouse Gases

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces OPTiMEM (Ocean-Heat-Content Physics and Time Macro Economic Model), a physics-based framework linking ocean heat accumulation to economic damages, and proposes the "heat conjecture" that weather damage scales stochastically with ocean heat increase rather than using traditional discount-rate approaches.

**Method:**
- Couples fossil fuel consumption functions to CO₂ emissions modeling
- Implements 18 emission scenarios to quantify uncertainty ranges
- Uses stochastic damage functions proportional to ocean heat content (OHC)
- Develops risk models at 1:10, 1:100, and 1:1000 year loss frequencies
- Proposes long-term carbon bonds as alternative to traditional discounting frameworks

**Data:**
Validates against observational datasets for greenhouse gas concentrations, global temperature, and ocean heat content; incorporates DICE model assumptions for scenario comparison.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses the physical link between cumulative carbon emissions → ocean heat accumulation → climate damages, avoiding the contentious pure rate of time preference (PRTP) debate that obscures climate risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Provides actionable risk metrics for policymakers and actuaries; suggests current economic models (DICE) significantly underestimate long-term warming (+18°C by 2210, +110°C by 2300 under high-emission scenarios); highlights energy return constraints if decarbonization is delayed.

**Limitations:**
Stochastic proportionality assumption between OHC and damages requires empirical validation; extreme temperature projections warrant scrutiny against peer-reviewed climate models; EROEI-monetary value coupling needs formalization.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-grounded model linking ocean heat to economic damages offers alternative SCC framework with risk-based metrics, suggesting conventional models underestimate long-term climate costs.

# Research Summary: Bayesian Inversion for Subsurface Flow Simulation

**Main Contribution:**
Compares variational inference and Monte Carlo methods for estimating subsurface reservoir properties from seismic and pressure data. Introduces physically structured variational inference (PSVI), which balances computational efficiency with posterior approximation accuracy by exploiting spatial correlations in remote sensing problems.

**Method:**
- Bayesian inverse problem framework with automatic differentiation variational inference (ADVI), Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD), stochastic SVGD (sSVGD), and Metropolis-Hastings MCMC
- PSVI variant constrains parameter correlations to spatially-local scales, reducing dimensionality
- Benchmarked against full-rank and mean-field approaches

**Data:**
Synthetic 2D and 3D fluid pressure and seismic measurements from CO₂ storage scenarios inspired by the Endurance field (UK North Sea).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports carbon capture and storage (CCS) deployment by improving reservoir characterization for CO₂ injection monitoring and containment prediction—critical for climate mitigation strategies.

**Implications:**
Enables faster, more reliable uncertainty quantification in subsurface modeling, reducing computational barriers to large-scale CCS site assessment and operational decision-making.

**Limitations:**
Tested on synthetic data only; real seismic/pressure data integration and scalability to field-scale heterogeneity not demonstrated. Computational costs of SVGD/sSVGD remain high relative to PSVI.

**TL;DR:**
PSVI offers efficient Bayesian parameter estimation for CO₂ storage reservoirs by exploiting spatial correlation structure in seismic and pressure inversions.
