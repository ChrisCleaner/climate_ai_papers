# FL-MHSM Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes a deep learning workflow for joint flood-landslide multi-hazard susceptibility mapping that preserves spatial heterogeneity through adaptive fusion of early and late fusion strategies with a Mixture of Experts ensemble model.

**Method:**
- Two-level spatial partitioning with overlapping lattice grids for data-parallel processing
- Probabilistic Early Fusion (EF) combining flood and landslide data at input level
- Tree-based Late Fusion (LF) baseline for comparison
- Soft-gating Mixture of Experts (MoE) as final predictive model integrating EF and LF outputs
- GeoDetector analysis to identify spatially-varying dominant risk factors

**Data:**
Regional case studies in Kerala (India) and Nepal with multi-hazard susceptibility labels; specific input features include topographic, land-cover, drainage, and glacier-related variables.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses compound flood-landslide hazards intensified by climate change and extreme precipitation events; enables spatially-informed risk assessment in mountainous and monsoon-affected regions vulnerable to cascading disasters.

**Implications:**
Supports evidence-based land-use planning and disaster preparedness by mapping zone-specific hazard susceptibility; demonstrates that spatially adaptive models outperform uniform approaches for heterogeneous landscapes.

**Limitations:**
Limited to two geographic regions; cross-hazard dependence mechanisms not explicitly modeled; computational scalability for continental-scale deployment unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Spatially adaptive deep learning ensemble improves multi-hazard susceptibility mapping by integrating complementary fusion strategies while revealing regionally-specific hazard drivers.

# HydroFirn Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Develops the first large-scale multidimensional model for firn hydrology on the Greenland Ice Sheet, moving beyond existing one-dimensional approaches. Incorporates multiphase flow, thermomechanics, and dynamic ice layer formation with computational efficiency.

**Method:**
- Novel pressure-equation algorithm that solves only in saturated regions (reduces computational cost)
- Coupled unsaturated-saturated flow physics with thermodynamic phase change
- Spatially heterogeneous boundary conditions for realistic subsurface conditions
- Validated against analytical solutions for 1D and 2D problems

**Data:**
Field observations from southwest Greenland; model benchmarked against analytic solutions rather than extensive observational datasets.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical uncertainty in surface mass balance estimates and sea-level rise projections by accurately modeling meltwater percolation and refreezing in firn—key processes in Greenland's contribution to ocean rise under warming.

**Implications:**
- Improves conversion of satellite altimetry to actual ice mass loss
- Constrains freshwater flux estimates to the ocean
- Reveals that lateral heterogeneities significantly affect melt depth and ice layer distribution, challenging 1D model assumptions
- Provides physics-based framework for future ice sheet modeling

**Limitations:**
Limited validation against extensive field datasets; application demonstrated primarily for southwest Greenland region.

**TL;DR:**
A computationally efficient 2D/3D firn hydrology model reveals that lateral heterogeneities substantially influence meltwater dynamics, improving sea-level rise and freshwater flux estimates.

# Research Summary: Energy Use Drivers in the U.S.

**Method:**
• LMDI (Logarithmic Mean Divisia Index) decomposition: separates energy use changes into intensity, activity, and structural components
• Stochastic frontier analysis: distinguishes between the efficiency frontier and inefficiency above it
• Variable-importance methods: attributes variance in energy use to specific factors (prices, policies, GDP, climate)

**Data:**
Panel dataset covering 50 U.S. states plus DC, 2006–2022 period (17 years).

**Climate Relevance:**
Identifies policy levers for reducing energy-related carbon emissions; demonstrates that regulatory and pricing mechanisms are more effective than relying on economic growth or climate factors alone for achieving energy efficiency gains.

**Implications:**
Policymakers should prioritize carbon pricing and efficiency regulations over demand-side approaches; results suggest state-level policies can meaningfully reduce energy consumption independent of economic conditions.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to U.S. states; cannot isolate causal effects of specific policies; frontier methodology assumes technical efficiency is the primary constraint (behavioral/structural barriers not explicitly modeled).

**TL;DR:**
Energy prices and state efficiency policies drive 39% of cross-state U.S. energy variation, far outweighing GDP and climate factors, suggesting regulation and pricing are the most effective levers for reducing energy consumption.
