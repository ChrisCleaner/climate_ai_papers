**Method:**

Enables evidence-based seasonal drought forecasting months in advance, supporting water management and agricultural decision-making. Demonstrates how AI interpretability methods can build stakeholder confidence in climate predictions by revealing physically plausible mechanisms.

**Limitations:**
Prediction skill depends on analogue year availability; framework tested primarily on one region and phenomenon; real-world validation awaits 2026 observations.

**Published:** 2026-08-14
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, physics.comp-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Wasserstein Distance for Climate Variability

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a distribution-free metric using 1-Wasserstein distance to quantify the relative magnitude of internal variability in climate model ensembles. The metric is parameter-free, computationally simple, and robust across non-Gaussian distributions.

**Method:**
Computes two 1-Wasserstein distances (optimal transport costs) to measure forced and internal variability separately, then calculates their ratio. Implementation requires only sorting and differencing quantiles; no distributional assumptions needed.

**Data:**
Validated on synthetic data (Gaussian, uniform, lognormal distributions) and applied to Community Earth System Model Large Ensemble (CESM-LE) for 2m air temperature and precipitation under two forcing scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
Separating forced climate change signals from natural internal variability is critical for attribution studies and uncertainty quantification in climate projections. Accurate variability metrics improve confidence in detecting anthropogenic climate change.

**Implications:**
Enables more reliable analysis of single-model initial-condition large ensembles (SMILEs), which are increasingly used for regional climate impact assessments. The metric's stability with ≥40 ensemble members makes it practical for operational climate centers.

**Limitations:**
Requires minimum ensemble size (~40 members); performance on very small ensembles not thoroughly explored. Validation limited to temperature and precipitation; generalization to other climate variables unclear.

**TL;DR:**
A robust, parameter-free Wasserstein distance metric quantifies internal climate variability in large ensembles without assuming specific probability distributions.
