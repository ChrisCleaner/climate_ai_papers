# Research Summary: ECMWF Seasonal Forecasts for Climate Attribution

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces counterfactual seasonal hindcasts using the ECMWF coupled forecasting system to perform detection and attribution studies, demonstrating that initialized seasonal forecast models can better reproduce observed decadal climate trends than traditional free-running climate models.

**Method:**

- Derived observation-based forced ocean temperature signal to amplify or remove anthropogenic warming from initial conditions
- Compared control, enhanced forcing (doubled CO2 + ocean warming), reduced forcing (fixed CO2, removed ocean warming), and aerosol-only experiments
- Evaluated impacts on temperature trends while monitoring prediction skill and model drift

**Data:**
ECMWF coupled seasonal forecasting system hindcasts with modified forcing scenarios; observation-based estimates of forced ocean temperature signals.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses the critical gap in climate attribution science: free-running models fail to capture observed decadal trends. This approach enables better quantification of anthropogenic forcing contributions to observed climate change through dynamic attribution.

**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: AI Climate Models and Climate-Zone Response to Warming

**Method:**
- Applied Köppen-Geiger climate-zone classification as a diagnostic framework
- Compared AIMIP Phase 1 AI models against AMIP physics-based models
- Assessed baseline classification skill, per-zone responses (temperature, precipitation, specific humidity), and spatial departure structures
- Focused on hybrid physics-AI model (NeuralGCM-HRD) versus pure emulators

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis climatology (1979-2014) for baseline validation; AIMIP and AMIP model outputs under prescribed warming scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**
Only physics-informed hybrid models reliably capture thermodynamic and hydrological scaling laws governing climate-zone migration. Pure AI emulators show systematic failures traceable to architectural limitations in land-cell treatment, limiting their utility for long-term climate projections without structural improvements.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to +4K forcing scenario; architectural failures in pure emulators identified but not comprehensively addressed; generalization to other warming magnitudes unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Hybrid physics-AI models outperform pure neural emulators at reproducing physically consistent climate-zone responses to warming, highlighting the necessity of physical constraints in AI climate models for projection reliability.
