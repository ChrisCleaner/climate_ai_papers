### [Precipitation Downscaling Using Foundation Model-Conditioned Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25858v1)

**Authors:** Victor Nascimento Ribeiro, Jorge Guevara, Jorge Sebastian Moraga...
**Published:** 2026-08-26
**Categories:** cs.CV, cs.LG, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Precipitation Downscaling Using Foundation Model-Conditioned Diffusion

**Main Contribution:**
Compares three conditioning strategies for diffusion models applied to precipitation downscaling, demonstrating that cross-attention conditioning—particularly with pretrained foundation models—outperforms simple concatenation for generating realistic high-resolution precipitation fields with improved extreme event representation.

**Method:**
Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) conditioned via: (1) channel concatenation of upsampled coarse predictors, (2) cross-attention with learned convolutional encoder, (3) cross-attention with frozen Prithvi WxC weather foundation model encoder. Evaluated against unconditioned baseline using CRPS, MSE, spectral, and extreme-event metrics.

**Data:**
Daily precipitation fields for the Colorado River Basin; foundation model approach trained on only five years of data versus full dataset for learned encoder baseline.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap between coarse global climate model outputs (~100+ km resolution) and fine-scale hydrological impact assessments requiring high-resolution precipitation (~1-10 km). Essential for water resource management and flood/drought risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Foundation model conditioning enables effective downscaling with limited training data, reducing computational barriers for climate impact studies. Better extreme event fidelity improves hazard assessment accuracy compared to over-smoothed alternatives.

**Limitations:**
Limited extreme event samples create uncertainty in >100mm/day event estimates; evaluation restricted to single basin; computational cost comparison incomplete for foundation model approach.

**TL;DR:**
Cross-attention conditioning with pretrained weather foundation models produces more realistic probabilistic precipitation downscaling than concatenation, with particular advantages for extreme events and data-limited scenarios.

### [Missing the Butterfly and Predicting the Past: Features or Bugs of Accurate AI Weather Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25835v1)

**Authors:** Pedram Hassanzadeh, Weidong Li, Y. Qiang Sun...
**Published:** 2026-08-26
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.AI
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: AI Weather Models and Physical Fidelity

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies a fundamental trade-off in AI weather prediction (AIWP) models: their forecast accuracy stems from implicit learning of subgrid-scale effects, but this comes at the cost of violating thermodynamic principles and missing chaotic sensitivity (butterfly effect). The study reveals that data coarse-graining—unavoidable during training—is the root cause of both their surprising skill and their physical inconsistencies.

**Method:**
Hierarchical analysis comparing AI models across three systems: observational reanalysis data, a general circulation model (GCM), and the Lorenz system. Key diagnostic: backcasting (predicting past states) to reveal whether models learn reversible vs. irreversible dynamics. Tests include sensitivity analysis by varying coarse-graining levels and examining emergence of arrow-of-time and butterfly-effect signatures.

**Data:**
Observation-based reanalysis, GCM outputs, and synthetic Lorenz system trajectories. Includes analysis of operational Pangu-Weather model.

**Climate Relevance:**
Critical for validating AI-based weather and climate emulators now operationally deployed. Raises questions about whether AIWP models can reliably simulate long-term climate dynamics, where thermodynamic consistency and chaotic error growth are essential.

**Implications:**
AIWP models achieve high short-term skill by learning effective parameterizations of subgrid physics without explicit physics constraints. However, their lack of thermodynamic fidelity and missing butterfly effect suggest caution for climate projection applications. Backcasting emerges as a diagnostic tool for assessing physical realism.

**Limitations:**
Analysis primarily uses idealized systems (Lorenz) alongside real data; generalization to full Earth system complexity unclear. Trade-off between accuracy and physical fidelity may be fundamental but not fully resolved.

**TL;DR:**
AI weather models achieve forecast accuracy by implicitly learning subgrid effects through data coarse-graining, but this violates thermodynamics and chaos—a feature, not a bug, that works for short-term prediction but threatens climate emulation reliability.

### [AI worsens climate change, integrated assessment shows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24670v1)

**Published:** 2026-08-25
**Categories:** econ.GN

# Research Summary: AI and Climate Change Integration

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an integrated analytical framework embedding AI's climate impacts into the DICE integrated assessment model, distinguishing between ICT-like and Industrial Revolution-like AI scenarios to quantify net climate costs of AI development.

**Method:**
- Extends the DICE model (dynamic integrated climate-economy model) with three AI impact channels: emissions, economic output, and climate damages
- Calibrates scenarios to empirical evidence for two AI development pathways (ICT-like: incremental productivity; IR-like: transformative)
- Optimizes investment rates under different climate mitigation constraints (low abatement vs. 2°C target)

**Data:**
Best available evidence on AI's energy consumption, productivity impacts, and economic returns; DICE model baseline parameters for climate-economic dynamics.

**Climate Relevance:**
Quantifies AI's net warming contribution (0.1–0.8°C by 2100 depending on scenario) and demonstrates that climate mitigation and AI investment are complementary rather than competing strategies for maximizing welfare.

**Implications:**
- Climate costs offset 20–25% of AI's economic gains under current policy
- Meeting 2°C targets requires higher optimal AI investment rates, not lower ones
- Policy should prioritize emissions abatement alongside AI development rather than treating them as trade-offs

**Limitations:**
Model-dependent results; uncertainty in long-term AI productivity trajectories and energy efficiency improvements not fully explored; assumes DICE framework adequately captures AI-climate interactions.

**TL;DR:**
AI accelerates warming by 0.1–0.8°C by 2100, but stronger climate mitigation actually increases optimal AI investment by making the economy more efficient.

# Research Summary: UODEs for Warm-Rain Parameterization

**Main Contribution:**
Applies Universal Ordinary Differential Equations (UODEs) to learn the dynamics of warm-rain cloud microphysics directly from simulation data, eliminating the need for explicit autoconversion/accretion rate targets while providing interpretable insights into existing parameterizations.

**Method:**
• Neural network-augmented ODEs trained on super-droplet model trajectories
• Learns closure operators using only prognostic state variables (liquid water content, rain water content)
• Validates learned dynamics against kinetic collection equation (KCE) solutions
• Interprets learned operator structure to refine analytical parameterizations

**Data:**
Super-droplet model simulations approximating the KCE; training domain spans typical warm-rain formation conditions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Warm-rain processes are critical for cloud feedback mechanisms in climate models. Current parameterizations introduce significant uncertainty in precipitation formation, affecting regional climate predictions and extreme weather modeling.

**Implications:**
• UODEs serve as both accurate surrogate models and diagnostic tools for understanding physical processes
• Refined analytical formulation improves KCE agreement while maintaining computational efficiency for climate models
• Framework generalizable to other cloud microphysical closures

**Limitations:**
Generalization beyond training domain not explicitly tested; applicability to ice-phase processes or different cloud regimes unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning-enhanced ODEs reveal that empirical accretion suppression in standard parameterizations primarily corrects autoconversion overestimation, enabling improved warm-rain closures for climate models.
