# Research Summary: Rapid SST Estimation from Sparse Observations

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates Sparse Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (S-DEIM) for reconstructing high-resolution sea surface temperature (SST) fields from sparse in situ observations (0.2% spatial coverage). Achieves 40% improvement over prior empirical interpolation methods with 91% of estimates within ±1°C accuracy.

**Method:**
Hybrid approach combining: (1) instantaneous empirical interpolation from sparse observations, and (2) RNN-learned temporal patterns from historical SST time series. No physics-based model required; computationally efficient (1-minute training, <1-second inference).

**Data:**
NOAA weekly high-resolution SST dataset (1989–2021) for training; test period January 2022–January 2023. Validation uses 100 sparse in situ observations per reconstruction.

**Climate Relevance:**
SST is a critical variable for weather forecasting, seasonal climate projections, and ocean-atmosphere coupling studies. Sparse observational networks (buoys, ships) limit real-time monitoring; this method bridges the gap between sparse measurements and gridded products.

**Implications:**
Enables rapid, real-time SST field reconstruction for operational forecasting systems. Reduces computational burden compared to data assimilation approaches; applicable to other sparse geophysical variables.

**Limitations:**
Sensor placement robustness tested only on random distributions; performance with systematic observational gaps unclear. Generalization to other variables or regions not demonstrated.

**TL;DR:**
S-DEIM combines empirical interpolation and RNNs to reconstruct full-resolution SST fields from sparse observations in real time with 40% improved accuracy over prior methods.
