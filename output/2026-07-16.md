# Research Summary: Ocean Heat Content Estimation via Spatio-Temporal Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a joint mapping method for ocean heat content (OHC) across two pressure layers that accounts for vertical correlation, reducing global uncertainty estimates by up to 15% compared to independent layer mapping.

**Method:**
• Bivariate locally stationary Gaussian processes to model two ocean pressure layers simultaneously
• Conditional simulations to capture spatio-temporal dependence between layers
• Uncertainty quantification integrated into the joint estimation framework

**Data:**
Argo profiling float observations (2004–2022); global ocean temperature profiles at varying depths with sparser data at greater depths.

**Climate Relevance:**
Ocean heat content is the primary metric for tracking Earth's energy imbalance and climate system heat accumulation. Reliable OHC estimates with quantified uncertainties are essential for validating climate models and detecting climate change signals.

**Implications:**
Improved uncertainty characterization enables statistically robust detection of regional and global OHC anomalies, strengthening confidence in climate trend analysis and informing climate monitoring systems.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to two pressure layers; method scalability to finer vertical resolution not addressed. Argo data coverage remains sparse in certain regions and depths, potentially affecting local estimates.

**TL;DR:**
Joint modeling of correlated ocean layers reduces OHC uncertainty by 15%, enabling more reliable climate change detection.

# Research Summary: Early-Warning Signals in Climate Tipping

- Extreme Value Theory (EVT) to characterize tail behavior and clustering of extreme temperature anomalies
- Data-Adaptive Harmonic Mode (DAHM) decomposition for full-field phase coherence analysis
- Applied to the Ghil–Sellers energy balance model (stochastic, metastable regime)

**Data:**
Synthetic model output from the Ghil–Sellers energy balance model; no observational datasets explicitly mentioned.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical challenge of detecting abrupt climate transitions (e.g., ice-albedo feedback, thermohaline circulation collapse) by identifying precursors in temperature dynamics and spatial organization before irreversible tipping occurs.

**Implications:**
Provides operational framework for monitoring real climate systems: practitioners can track spectral response, extreme event statistics, and phase coherence in observational data to assess proximity to tipping points with higher confidence than single-metric approaches.

**Limitations:**
- Validation limited to idealized energy balance model; applicability to high-dimensional GCMs unclear
- Requires high-resolution spatiotemporal data; computational cost of DAHM analysis not discussed
- No comparison with other multi-indicator early-warning frameworks

**TL;DR:**
Multi-scale analysis of slow modes, extreme statistics, and field coherence reveals tipping signatures beyond critical slowing down in a climate model.

# AeroMELD Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces AeroMELD, a mathematically principled machine-learning framework that creates low-dimensional representations of atmospheric aerosol populations while preserving their linear mathematical structure. This enables physically meaningful process operators and hybrid ML-physics modeling that existing autoencoders cannot support.

**Method:**
- Constructs permutation-invariant linear encoders via scale-shape decomposition
- Explicitly represents total particle concentration; latent shape derived from barycentric combinations of per-particle embeddings
- Encodes weighted particle populations directly (not binned distributions) with nonlinearity moved to diagnostic mapping stage
- Preserves latent linearity while maintaining Deep Sets expressiveness

**Data:**
Particle-resolved aerosol simulation data used as ground truth; diagnostic targets include size-resolved mass/number distributions, CCN spectra, optical coefficients, and immersion-freezing behavior.

**Climate Relevance:**
Aerosol composition and mixing state critically affect aerosol-cloud interactions, radiative forcing, and ice nucleation—key uncertainties in climate modeling. Accurate, flexible aerosol representation improves climate prediction fidelity.

**Implications:**
Enables learning of aerosol microphysical process evolution (emissions, mixing, chemical transformation) directly in latent space within hybrid ML-physics models, potentially accelerating climate simulations while maintaining physical consistency.

**Limitations:**
Experiments focus on diagnostic reconstruction; actual process evolution learning in latent space remains future work. Scalability to full Earth system models not yet demonstrated.

**TL;DR:**
AeroMELD creates interpretable, physics-preserving low-dimensional aerosol representations suitable for hybrid machine learning-physics climate models.

# Inunda: GPU-Native Differentiable Flood Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Inunda is a GPU-accelerated, fully differentiable shallow water equation solver that combines physics-based hydraulic accuracy with modern automatic differentiation for parameter calibration. It enables high-resolution flood inundation modeling over large domains in minutes while maintaining mass conservation and physical constraints.

**Method:**
- Solves 2D shallow water equations using a mass-conservative local-inertial scheme
- GPU-native implementation with autograd-compatible operators enabling end-to-end differentiability
- Parameters estimated via reverse-mode automatic differentiation (backpropagation) against observational data
- Capable of processing millions of grid cells for multi-day simulations on single GPU

**Data:**
Hurricane Harvey (2017) hindcast in Harris County, Texas; July 2025 Central Texas flash flood with 18-member 1-km convection-allowing precipitation ensemble; Rio Ruidoso post-fire flash flood case study.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical climate adaptation need for accurate, rapid flood forecasting under extreme precipitation events intensified by climate change; demonstrates applicability to post-fire hydrologic recovery in fire-prone regions.

**Implications:**
Enables real-time probabilistic flood forecasting, improved operational flood prediction (outperforming National Water Model v3.0), and spatially explicit recovery monitoring of watershed properties post-disturbance.

**Limitations:**
Limited to 2D shallow water assumptions (may not capture complex 3D flow); generalization to ungauged basins not explicitly discussed; computational requirements for ensemble forecasting at operational scale unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Differentiable GPU solver combines physics-based flood modeling with machine learning calibration, achieving operational-grade accuracy with minutes-scale runtime.

# Research Summary: Beyond Consistent Scenarios

**Main Contribution:**
Extends Cross-Impact Balance (CIB) analysis with Linear Response Theory to move beyond identifying equilibrium scenarios toward quantifying transition effort, identifying true system levers, mapping adjustment sequences, and measuring structural resilience in socio-technical systems.

**Method:**
Exploits mathematical isomorphism between CIB drift matrices and Leontief input-output matrices to derive four closed-form analytical objects: Type I cross-impact multipliers (direct + indirect influence chains), perturbation budgets (network-weighted transition effort), impulse response functions (adjustment sequences and feedback overshoots), and unit-impulse shock profiles (attractor-specific sensitivity).

**Data:**
Expert-elicited interdependency networks encoded as pairwise influence scores; empirically applied to an energy-transition cross-impact matrix with five structural equilibria.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses energy transition assessment by providing structural diagnostics for policy scenarios—identifying which interventions create system-wide leverage, quantifying implementation effort, and revealing vulnerability to external shocks (e.g., geopolitical disruptions, technology failures).

**Implications:**
Enables policymakers and modelers to prioritize interventions based on indirect influence chains rather than direct effects alone; supports design of robust transition pathways by quantifying resilience at different equilibria; transferable to any domain modeling structural interdependencies (e.g., food systems, water-energy-land nexus).

**Limitations:**
Relies on expert elicitation quality; assumes linear response dynamics; does not address temporal evolution between equilibria or stochastic uncertainty in influence scores.

**TL;DR:**
Framework quantifies hidden leverage, transition costs, and shock sensitivity in energy-transition scenarios by applying input-output economics to expert-elicited interdependency networks.
