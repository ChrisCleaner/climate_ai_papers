# AI-Based Regional Emulation for Kilometer-Scale Dynamical Downscaling

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an AI-based Limited-Area Model (LAM) that performs dynamical downscaling from 0.25° to 4-km resolution with strong generalization across diverse forcing datasets, time periods, and climate regimes. Demonstrates that weather prediction AI models can be effectively adapted for long-term regional climate applications.

**Method:**
Neural network-based emulator trained on ERA5 boundary conditions (0.25°, 3-hourly) to predict CONUS404 targets (4-km, hourly). Includes post-processing model for diagnostic variables and is evaluated on deterministic verification metrics comparable to weather-forecasting AI models.

**Data:**
Training: ERA5 (1980–2019) as forcings; CONUS404 as targets. Evaluation: Multiple forcing datasets, non-ERA5 1.0° 6-hourly data, global climate model runs (1980–2010, 2070–2100), and ensemble simulations (2025–2027).

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables high-resolution regional climate projections by downscaling coarse global climate models to kilometer scales, critical for assessing localized climate impacts on water resources, extreme weather, and infrastructure in the Southern Great Plains and southeastern U.S.

**Implications:**
Provides a practical, computationally efficient alternative to traditional dynamical downscaling for generating multi-decadal regional climate scenarios; transferable framework for other regions and climate models.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to two U.S. regions; long-term stability beyond 30 years not tested; generalization to significantly different climate forcings (e.g., different GCMs) not fully explored.

**TL;DR:**
AI-based downscaling model successfully emulates kilometer-scale regional climate dynamics from coarse inputs with stable multi-year performance and robust generalization across forcing datasets and future climate scenarios.

### [Weak 21st-century AMOC response to Greenland meltwater in a strongly eddying ocean model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17235v1)

**Published:** 2026-02-19
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph

# Research Summary: AMOC Response to Greenland Meltwater

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies how Greenland ice sheet meltwater affects Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) weakening through 2100 using a high-resolution eddy-resolving ocean model. Demonstrates that meltwater-induced AMOC weakening (0.6 ± 0.2 Sv) is modest compared to warming-driven weakening and is largely independent of model resolution.

**Method:**
High-resolution (1/10° horizontal) ocean general circulation model with explicit mesoscale eddy representation. Forced with SSP5-8.5 emissions scenario; compares meltwater impact across different ocean states and resolutions to isolate background climate state effects.

**Data:**
Greenland meltwater projections under SSP5-8.5 forcing; model output from 21st-century simulations at 1/10° and coarser resolutions for comparison.

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC weakening is a critical tipping point in climate systems with major implications for Northern Hemisphere heat distribution and sea-level rise. Greenland melt is an accelerating freshwater source that could trigger abrupt circulation changes.

**Implications:**
Suggests that coarse-resolution climate models may adequately capture meltwater effects on AMOC despite missing eddies. Indicates that present-day climate sensitivity to meltwater differs from future projections, complicating paleoclimate interpretations.

**Limitations:**
Single model study; results may not generalize across different ocean model architectures. Limited exploration of potential nonlinear interactions at extreme meltwater rates.

**TL;DR:**
Greenland meltwater causes modest additional AMOC weakening in a high-resolution model, with effects determined more by background ocean state than model resolution.

### [Ensemble-size-dependence of deep-learning post-processing methods that minimize an (un)fair score: motivating examples and a proof-of-concept solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15830v1)

**Published:** 2026-02-17
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Ensemble-Size-Dependent Deep Learning for Fair Climate Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies a critical problem where deep-learning post-processing methods (including transformers) violate fairness assumptions in ensemble forecasts when ensemble size changes, causing apparent skill gains to mask systematic unreliability. Proposes trajectory transformers as a solution that maintains conditional independence across ensemble members.

**Method:**
- Compares two aCRPS-minimizing approaches: linear member-by-member calibration and transformer self-attention
- Demonstrates both introduce structural dependencies that bias the adjusted continuous ranked probability score (aCRPS)
- Introduces trajectory transformers: applies self-attention over lead time (temporal dimension) rather than ensemble dimension, preserving conditional independence required for fair scoring

**Data:**
ECMWF subseasonal forecasting system; weekly mean 2-meter temperature (T₂m) forecasts; training ensembles of 3 and 9 members; real-time forecasts of 9 and 100 members.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses subseasonal forecasting (2-4 week timescale), a critical gap between weather and seasonal prediction. Improves reliability of ensemble temperature forecasts essential for operational climate services.

**Implications:**
Enables practical post-processing with smaller training ensembles without sacrificing forecast reliability across different operational ensemble sizes—reducing computational costs while maintaining trustworthy uncertainty quantification.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to single variable (T₂m) and one forecast system; generalization to other variables, regions, or lead times not demonstrated.

**TL;DR:**
Transformer-based ensemble post-processing can appear skillful while becoming unreliable at different ensemble sizes; trajectory transformers solve this by applying attention temporally rather than across ensemble members.

Global coupled simulation output; ERA5 reanalysis for validation; observational focus on Southern Ocean eddy-rich and eddy-poor regions.
