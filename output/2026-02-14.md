# Research Summary: Function-Space Decoupled Diffusion for CCS Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces Fun-DDPS, a generative framework combining function-space diffusion models with neural operator surrogates to solve ill-posed inverse problems in subsurface characterization for Carbon Capture and Storage with sparse observational data.

**Method:**
- Single-channel diffusion model learns prior distribution over geological parameters (geomodel)
- Local Neural Operator (LNO) surrogate provides physics-consistent guidance via cross-field conditioning
- Decoupled architecture: diffusion handles parameter-space recovery; surrogate enables efficient gradient-based data assimilation
- Validated against asymptotically exact Rejection Sampling posteriors

**Data:**
Synthetic CCS modeling datasets; experiments use 25% observational coverage scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports Carbon Capture and Storage deployment by improving subsurface characterization accuracy—critical for predicting CO₂ plume migration, containment integrity, and long-term storage viability in geological formations.

**Implications:**
Enables reliable CCS site selection and monitoring with limited field data; 11× error reduction over standard surrogates under extreme data sparsity; produces physically consistent realizations suitable for regulatory decision-making and risk assessment.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to synthetic datasets; computational cost of diffusion sampling relative to deterministic methods not explicitly discussed; generalization to real field data requires further investigation.

**TL;DR:**
Decoupled diffusion-neural operator framework achieves 11× improvement in CCS subsurface modeling with sparse data while maintaining physical consistency and rigorous uncertainty quantification.

# CAAL: Confidence-Aware Active Learning for Heteroscedastic Atmospheric Regression

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes CAAL, an active learning framework that efficiently selects samples for labeling when estimating atmospheric particle properties from routine observations. Addresses the heteroscedastic regression challenge where noise varies with input, preventing standard uncertainty-based acquisition strategies from working effectively.

**Method:**
- Decoupled uncertainty-aware training: separately optimizes predictive mean and aleatoric noise estimation to stabilize uncertainty quantification
- Confidence-aware acquisition function: weights epistemic uncertainty by predicted aleatoric uncertainty to avoid sampling in noise-dominated regions
- Distinguishes reducible (epistemic) from irreducible (aleatoric) uncertainty to guide sample selection

**Data:**
Particle-resolved numerical simulations and real atmospheric observations; focuses on estimating particle toxicity and hygroscopicity from air pollutant concentrations and meteorological variables.

**Climate Relevance:**
Particle properties (toxicity, hygroscopicity) are critical for quantifying air pollution impacts on health and climate forcing. Routine observations alone cannot directly measure these properties; CAAL enables efficient inference from available data, expanding atmospheric databases cost-effectively.

**Implications:**
Reduces labeling costs for expensive particle simulations/measurements while improving prediction robustness. Enables better characterization of aerosol-climate interactions and air quality impacts with limited experimental budgets.

**Limitations:**
Not explicitly discussed; framework validation limited to particle properties—generalizability to other heteroscedastic atmospheric regression tasks unclear.

**TL;DR:**
CAAL improves active learning for noisy atmospheric inference by separately modeling epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty to prioritize informative samples over noise-dominated regions.

# Research Summary: Hierarchical Testing of Hybrid ML-Physics Atmosphere Model

**Main Contribution:**
Comprehensive evaluation of NeuralGCM, a hybrid machine learning-physics global circulation model, across multiple timescales and climate forcing scenarios to assess its reliability for Earth system applications.

**Method:**
Hybrid architecture combining a dynamical core with ML-based parameterizations; hierarchical testing framework evaluating: (1) synoptic-scale cyclone dynamics, (2) interannual variability under ENSO forcing, and (3) out-of-distribution responses to +3K/+4K uniform warming scenarios.

**Data:**
Observations, physics-based Earth System Models (ESMs), and synthetic forcing experiments; evaluation metrics include cyclone tracking, teleconnection patterns, and large-scale circulation features.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need to validate ML weather/climate models for predictability across timescales and under future climate conditions—essential for operational forecasting and climate projection reliability.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates ML-physics hybrids can match ESM performance on synoptic phenomena and reproduce large-scale warming responses, potentially enabling faster climate simulations; identifies specific failure modes (cyclone overestimation, nonlinear ENSO responses) requiring targeted improvements before operational deployment.

**Limitations:**
Model shows deficiencies in capturing nonlinear climate responses, biases in teleconnected wave patterns, and stratospheric circulation responses; out-of-distribution generalization remains partially unvalidated.

**TL;DR:**
NeuralGCM successfully captures synoptic and large-scale climate features but exhibits systematic biases in nonlinear responses and upper-atmosphere dynamics, requiring further refinement for climate applications.

# Research Summary: Linear Response and Optimal Fingerprinting for Nonautonomous Systems

**Main Contribution:**
Extends linear response theory and optimal fingerprinting methods to time-dependent (nonautonomous) systems, enabling climate attribution when background states vary with natural forcings rather than remaining static.

**Method:**
- Derives Green-Kubo-type formulas for linear response in time-dependent Markov chains and diffusion processes
- Analyzes pullback measures under general (non-periodic) perturbations
- Extends optimal fingerprinting to multiple time slices simultaneously
- Validates using Markov state modeling discretization

**Data:**
Modified Ghil-Sellers energy balance model with explicit time-dependent reference dynamics; includes CO₂ and localized aerosol forcing scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses detection and attribution of climate change when natural forcings (e.g., solar variability) create time-varying background states—a realistic scenario absent from classical autonomous-system approaches. Enables separation of anthropogenic signals from natural climate variations.

**Implications:**
Improves climate attribution studies by accounting for non-stationary reference conditions; applicable to fingerprinting anthropogenic warming against evolving natural forcings and other complex systems undergoing external perturbations.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to idealized energy balance model; applicability to high-dimensional GCMs and observational data requires further testing. Computational scalability for complex systems not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Develops response theory for time-dependent systems, enabling more accurate attribution of climate change when natural forcings create non-stationary background states.

# Research Summary: AMOC Stability Under Rapid Climate Change

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that AMOC collapse thresholds are not fixed but depend critically on the *rate* of climate forcing, not just the magnitude of warming. Identifies a stabilizing mechanism that operates when forcing increases slowly enough to allow ocean adjustment.

**Method:**
Climate modeling with varying CO₂ increase rates (0.5 ppm/yr vs. intermediate/high-emission scenarios) to isolate forcing-rate dependence. Analysis of surface-interior ocean property coherence and feedback mechanisms (evaporation, sea-ice extent).

**Data:**
Climate model simulations; no external datasets explicitly mentioned. Experiments span multiple warming scenarios (+2.2°C to +5.5°C global warming).

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC is a critical tipping element regulating heat transport and regional climate stability. Current policy-relevant threshold estimates (+4°C) may be misleading if forcing rates matter more than absolute warming levels.

**Implications:**
Mitigation strategy effectiveness depends on *decarbonization speed*, not just final warming limit. Rapid emissions reductions reduce near-term AMOC collapse risk even if long-term warming targets are similar.

**Limitations:**
Single modeling framework; generalization to other climate models unclear. Mechanism identification relies on idealized forcing scenarios; real-world forcing includes non-CO₂ agents and variability.

**TL;DR:**
AMOC collapse risk is path-dependent—slow forcing allows stability at higher warming, but rapid forcing triggers collapse at lower warming, making emissions reduction *rate* as critical as reduction *amount*.
