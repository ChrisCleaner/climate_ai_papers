**Method:**

• Ablation study varying training sample sizes (700–7,000 monthly observations)
• Transfer learning approach: pre-train on CMIP6 with learned model embedding, fine-tune on scarce observations

Synthetic LIM-generated SST fields (known low-order structure); ERSSTv5 observations (~700 samples); CMIP6 climate model ensemble

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical challenge in climate emulation: capturing El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) variability, including its Eastern/Central Pacific asymmetry, with limited observational records (~60 years of monthly data).

**Implications:**
Transfer learning enables accurate climate emulation despite observational data scarcity, potentially improving seasonal forecasting and climate downscaling applications where long instrumental records are unavailable.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to synthetic LIM data; real-world applicability to observed ENSO requires further validation. Computational cost of CMIP6 pre-training not discussed.

**TL;DR:**

- 19 rainfall-triggered landslides in/around Aizawl (2016–2025)

**Implications:**

**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: Abrupt Transition to Superrotation in Idealized GCM

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies the dynamical mechanisms governing whether Earth's tropical atmosphere transitions abruptly or gradually to equatorial superrotation (westerly zonal winds). Demonstrates that this transition type is controlled by meridional temperature gradient and bottom friction in idealized climate models.

**Method:**
Numerical experiments using the Held-Suarez idealized GCM framework, systematically varying parameters to isolate competing feedback mechanisms: a positive tropical wave-jet feedback and a negative extratropical wave absorption feedback near critical latitudes.

**Data:**
Idealized GCM simulations (no observational datasets); parameter sensitivity studies within the Held-Suarez model configuration.

**Climate Relevance:**
Superrotation is a potential atmospheric state in extremely warm climates (e.g., early Earth, high-CO₂ scenarios). Understanding transition mechanisms reduces uncertainty in predicting extreme climate responses and atmospheric circulation changes under extreme forcing.

**Implications:**
Provides mechanistic understanding to improve GCM predictions of atmospheric circulation in warm climate scenarios; helps reconcile conflicting model responses to tropical heating and constrains conditions for abrupt climate transitions.

**Limitations:**
Idealized model framework may not capture full complexity of realistic atmospheric dynamics; results depend on specific model parameterizations (friction, heating profiles); applicability to Earth's actual climate requires validation with comprehensive GCMs.

**TL;DR:**
Superrotation transitions in warm climates are controlled by competition between tropical wave-jet amplification and extratropical wave damping, with outcome determined by temperature gradients and surface friction.
