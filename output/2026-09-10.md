# Neptune: AI Model for Global Ocean Subseasonal Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Neptune is a data-driven neural network framework that emulates global ocean and sea-ice dynamics for subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting up to 60 days ahead, replacing computationally expensive physics-based Ocean General Circulation Models (OGCMs).

**Method:**
Combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for local feature extraction with Spherical Fourier Neural Operators (SFNOs) to capture global cross-scale interactions. Two variants operate at 1° (Neptune-1) and 0.25° (Neptune-025) spatial resolution, forced by prescribed daily atmospheric fields to predict ocean state variables (temperature, salinity, currents, sea surface height, sea ice properties) with daily outputs.

**Data:**
Training data derived from ocean model outputs; specific datasets not detailed in abstract, but includes ocean state variables across surface and water column depths.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical S2S prediction timescale (2-60 days) essential for water management, agriculture, disaster risk reduction, energy planning, and climate-sensitive sectors. Enables high-resolution representation of ocean dynamics that drive regional climate variability.

**Implications:**
Provides computationally efficient alternative to traditional OGCMs for operational forecasting systems; enables rapid iteration and improvement; supports decision-making across multiple climate-dependent sectors.

**Limitations:**
Relies on prescribed atmospheric forcing (not coupled); long-term stability claims require validation; generalization to extreme events or climate change scenarios not explicitly addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Neptune uses hybrid CNN-SFNO architecture to emulate global ocean dynamics for 60-day forecasts, offering a faster, more flexible alternative to traditional circulation models.

# GCMagicc v1: Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
GCMagicc is a hybrid generative emulator combining a simple physical climate model with machine learning to rapidly generate large ensembles of climate variables matching Earth system model resolution without GPU requirements or retraining for new scenarios.

**Method:**
• Hybrid architecture: pairs simplified physical climate model (MAGICC) with ML emulation
• Trained on 32 CMIP6 models + observational/reanalysis data
• Generates 10 climate variables at full ESM resolution
• Scenario-agnostic (no retraining needed for new emissions pathways)

**Data:**
CMIP6 ensemble (32 Earth system models), observational/reanalysis datasets, applied to SSP scenarios, current policies, Paris Agreement pledges, and CMIP7 scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables rapid generation of climate impact ensembles across multiple warming pathways (1.2–6.1°C) critical for IPCC assessments and policy evaluation; demonstrated via 2025 Iranian drought attribution analysis showing strong anthropogenic signal (29% probability of observed severity with forcing vs. 0% without).

**Implications:**
Democratizes access to large-scale climate projections for impact studies; supports policy analysis for Paris Agreement targets; enables rapid assessment of climate-driven risks (drought, food security, geopolitical conflicts) across diverse future scenarios.

**Limitations:**
Complements rather than replaces ESMs; attribution analysis relies on comparison with CMIP6 large ensembles; computational efficiency gains may trade some physical process detail.

**TL;DR:**
Fast ML-enhanced climate emulator generates policy-relevant climate ensembles without retraining, enabling rapid impact assessments across multiple warming scenarios.

# Research Summary: Macroturbulence Scaling Theory for Planetary Atmospheres

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a new scaling theory for atmospheric eddy heat flux that works across weakly supercritical regimes where existing theories fail. The theory successfully predicts both domain-averaged and local eddy behavior across diverse planetary circulation states.

**Method:**
- Analyzes hundreds of idealized GCM simulations varying rotation rates, meridional temperature gradients, stratification, and seasonality
- Uses scaling arguments from zonal momentum balance combined with kinematic eddy mixing effects
- Introduces thermal Rossby number as key non-dimensional parameter
- Accounts for nonlinear eddy momentum flux effects on jet structure

**Data:**
Idealized dry general circulation model simulations spanning wide parameter ranges; validation against simple energy balance model predictions of global near-surface temperature distributions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Eddy heat flux fundamentally controls atmospheric circulation and surface temperature patterns. Current theories assume conditions (inverse energy cascade, supercritical states) rarely met in Earth's atmosphere, limiting predictive capability for climate dynamics.

**Implications:**
Provides theoretical foundation for understanding general circulation across diverse climate states; enables better parameterization of eddy effects in climate models; framework extensible to moist atmospheres for improved weather and climate prediction.

**Limitations:**
Theory developed for dry atmospheres; moisture effects remain unaddressed; validation limited to idealized GCM configurations rather than observations or comprehensive climate models.

**TL;DR:**
New scaling theory explains atmospheric eddy heat flux across realistic planetary circulation regimes where previous theories break down.

# Research Summary: Climate Variability Disentanglement with CNNs

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that U-Net convolutional neural networks can effectively separate forced (anthropogenic) climate variability from internal (natural) variability in observational and model data from 1950–2022, achieving errors of 0.1–0.4°C for surface air temperature—substantially better than polynomial trend methods.

**Method:**
U-Net architecture (encoder-decoder with skip connections) trained on multivariate climate fields from four single-model initial-condition large ensembles, with cross-validation by withholding one model's data. The network learns to isolate forced signals from the combined internal+forced variability.

**Data:**
Five single-model initial-condition large ensembles providing multiple fields (surface air temperature, sea-level pressure, precipitation) spanning 1950–2022; training uses four models, validation on the held-out fifth model.

**Climate Relevance:**
Accurately partitioning forced versus internal variability is essential for climate attribution studies, understanding natural climate oscillations (e.g., ENSO, AMO), and quantifying the anthropogenic warming signal amid natural noise.

**Implications:**
Enables improved detection of forced climate trends in shorter observational records and better characterization of internal variability impacts on regional climate projections and extreme events.

**Limitations:**
Performance degrades for low signal-to-noise variables (precipitation, sea-level pressure); underestimates warming in high-sensitivity models; limited by multi-model agreement and ensemble sampling size; framework would benefit from larger, more diverse training datasets.

**TL;DR:**
U-Nets successfully disentangle anthropogenic from natural climate variability with ~0.2°C accuracy, outperforming traditional methods and enabling better climate attribution.

# PCSDiff: Diffusion-Based Precipitation Forecast Correction

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces PCSDiff, a cascaded diffusion framework that simultaneously corrects systematic biases in 10-day medium-range precipitation forecasts and enhances spatial resolution while maintaining physical plausibility. Addresses a critical operational gap by combining bias correction with downscaling in a single, deployment-ready model.

# Research Summary: Wind and Solar Curtailment Under Negative Prices in Germany

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies the gap between economic incentives and actual curtailment behavior for German wind and solar generators across two decades of support schemes, revealing that policy design fails to align renewable generation with grid needs at negative prices.

**Method:**
• Cohort-level analysis of support scheme incentives (hour-by-hour) for each generator cohort
• Market-based curtailment estimation derived from reanalysis weather data
• Comparison of theoretical incentives against observed behavioral responses

**Data:**
German renewable generation data spanning ~20 years; weather reanalysis data for curtailment estimation; wholesale electricity price records; support scheme policy parameters.

**Climate Relevance:**
High renewable penetration is essential for decarbonization, but grid stability and economic efficiency require renewables to respond flexibly to supply-demand imbalances—a prerequisite for scaling wind and solar without costly infrastructure upgrades or fossil fuel backup.

**Implications:**
Current subsidy structures create perverse incentives: wind generators ignore negative price signals (50% non-compliance at <-50 EUR/MWh), while solar's feed-in tariff shields it from price exposure entirely. This increases system operation costs and grid stress, necessitating price-responsive support mechanisms for future renewable expansion.

**Limitations:**
Analysis focuses on Germany's specific policy context; generalizability to other markets with different support schemes unclear. Behavioral non-compliance drivers (technical constraints, information gaps, risk aversion) not explicitly modeled.

**TL;DR:**
German renewable support schemes fail to incentivize curtailment at negative prices, undermining grid stability and cost-efficiency despite high wind/solar penetration.
