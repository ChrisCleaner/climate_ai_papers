### [Data Driven Air Entrainment Velocity Parameterization by Breaking Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04067v1)

**Authors:** Xiaohui Zhou, Anton S. Darmenov, Kianoosh Yousefi
**Published:** 2026-02-03
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Data-Driven Air Entrainment Velocity Parameterization

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a machine-learning parameterization of air-entrainment velocity (Va) from breaking waves that significantly improves representation of air-sea exchange processes in climate models. Replaces traditional single-variable formulas with a physics-informed neural network using seven wave and wind predictors.

**Method:**
Multilayer perceptron trained on 43-year WAVEWATCH III hindcast data with physically motivated inputs (wind speed, wave height, age, steepness, direction, depth). Model learns nonlinear relationships between sea state and bubble injection, validated against spectral-reference Va and independent HiWinGS observations.

**Data:**
• WAVEWATCH III global wave simulation (43 years) with resolved breaker-front distribution
• HiWinGS field observations for independent validation
• Training captures diverse sea states across latitudes and seasons

**Climate Relevance:**
Air entrainment by breaking waves controls ocean-atmosphere fluxes of CO₂, heat, momentum, and sea-salt aerosols—critical processes for climate feedback loops. Current models systematically misrepresent these fluxes, introducing biases in carbon cycling and aerosol radiative forcing.

**Implications:**
Reduces CO₂ transfer velocity and sea-salt aerosol emission errors by ~order of magnitude. Corrects known biases: overestimation in swell-dominated regions and underestimation in storm tracks. Enables improved coupled atmosphere-wave-ocean model performance for climate projections.

**Limitations:**
Deep-water validation only; coastal/shallow-water applicability unclear. Dependent on WAVEWATCH III simulation quality; potential systematic biases inherited from wave model.

**TL;DR:**
Machine-learning model parameterizes wave-breaking air entrainment with seven physical predictors, dramatically improving air-sea gas and aerosol exchange representation in climate models.

Introduces OPTiMEM (Ocean-Heat-Content Physics and Time Macro Economic Model), a physics-based framework for estimating the social cost of carbon (SCC) that treats SCC as a multidimensional "economic phase space" rather than single values, coupled with a heat conjecture linking weather damages to ocean heat accumulation.

- Couples fossil fuel consumption functions to CO₂ emissions with 18 uncertainty scenarios
- Validates against GHG, temperature, and ocean heat content (OHC) datasets
- Models four GHGs (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, halogenated hydrocarbons) with stochastic damage curves proportional to OHC increase
- Proposes long-term carbon bonds to resolve discount rate disagreements and introduce real-dollar monitoring metrics
- Generates 1:N year loss risk models (1:10, 1:100, 1:1000) for policy application

**Data:**
Historical greenhouse gas concentrations, temperature records, and ocean heat content observations (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**

# MSWEP V3 Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
MSWEP V3 is the first fully global, machine learning-powered precipitation dataset providing hourly estimates at 0.1° resolution from 1979 to present with ~2-hour latency. It significantly outperforms existing precipitation products (median daily KGE of 0.69 vs. 0.61 for ERA5 and 0.46 for IMERG).

**Method:**

**Data:**
Training: 15,959 hourly/daily precipitation gauges; Correction: 57,666 daily and 86,000 monthly gauge stations; Validation: 15,958 independent gauges; Comparison: 19 global gridded precipitation products (ERA5, IMERG-L V7, GSMaP V8, CHIRP, etc.).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need for accurate, timely precipitation monitoring essential for understanding hydrological cycles, extreme weather events, and water resource management under climate variability and change.

**Implications:**
Enables improved water-related risk monitoring, seasonal forecasting, and climate impact assessments. Near real-time availability supports operational hydrology, flood/drought early warning, and climate adaptation planning.

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: Rapid SST Estimation from Sparse Observations

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces Sparse Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (S-DEIM), a hybrid AI approach that reconstructs high-resolution global sea surface temperature (SST) fields from sparse in situ observations (0.2% spatial coverage) without requiring physics-based models.

**Method:**
S-DEIM combines two components: (1) empirical interpolation from instantaneous sparse observations, and (2) a recurrent neural network (RNN) trained on historical SST time series to capture spatiotemporal patterns. The RNN learns temporal dynamics from 33 years of NOAA weekly SST data (1989–2021), enabling rapid inference on new observations.

**Data:**
Training: NOAA high-resolution weekly SST dataset (1989–2021). Testing: January 2022–January 2023 with synthetic sparse sampling (100 observations per timestep).

**Climate Relevance:**
Accurate SST reconstruction is critical for weather forecasting, seasonal climate predictions, and monitoring ocean heat content—a key climate indicator. Enables real-time gap-filling from sparse buoy and ship-based observations.

**Implications:**
Achieves ~40% accuracy improvement over prior empirical methods; 91% of estimates within ±1°C of truth. Computationally efficient (training: ~1 min; inference: <1 sec per timestep). Robust to random sensor placement (1–2% error increase). Enables operational real-time SST monitoring with minimal observational infrastructure.

**Limitations:**
Tested only on synthetic sparse sampling; real-world sensor networks have non-random deployment patterns. Generalization to extreme events or climate regime shifts not explicitly addressed. Requires historical training data representative of target period.

**TL;DR:**
Hybrid RNN-empirical interpolation method reconstructs global SST fields from <1% spatial observations with 40% better accuracy than prior methods, enabling real-time climate monitoring.
