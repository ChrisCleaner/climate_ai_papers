### [Carbon mineralization in CO2-seawater-basalt systems: Reactive transport dynamics and vesicular pore architecture controls](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00710v1)

**Published:** 2026-01-02

**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Carbon Mineralization in Basaltic CO₂ Storage

**Main Contribution:**
Reveals that carbonate precipitation in basaltic rocks is nucleation-controlled and stochastic, with pore network topology—not just chemistry—critically determining mineralization efficiency and permeability evolution during CO₂ storage.

**Method:**
- Flow-through experiments (80°C) with CO₂-acidified seawater on basaltic glass
- Geochemical modeling (reactive transport simulation)
- Multi-scale pore imaging: micro-CT analysis and post-experiment phase identification

**Data:**
Three basalt facies with connected porosity (1.3–32%) vs. total porosity (18–42%); pore coordination numbers (modal = 2); flow rates 0.05–0.005 mL/min; precipitate phases: calcium carbonate and smectite.

**Climate Relevance:**
Basaltic carbon mineralization offers permanent, rapid CO₂ sequestration as an alternative to conventional geological storage, supporting climate mitigation strategies for industrial-scale carbon removal.

**Implications:**
Pore topology vulnerability in basalts demands probabilistic reactive transport models and careful flow-rate optimization; distributed small precipitates pose greater permeability risks than large accumulations, requiring redesigned injection protocols for CCS operations.

**Limitations:**
Seawater chemistry reduces mineralization efficiency compared to freshwater systems; findings based on laboratory conditions may not fully capture field-scale heterogeneity and long-term precipitation dynamics.

**TL;DR:**
Basaltic CO₂ storage efficiency is controlled by stochastic nucleation and pore network geometry rather than thermodynamic equilibrium alone, necessitating probabilistic modeling frameworks.
