# Research Summary: ECMWF Seasonal Forecasts for Climate Attribution

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces counterfactual seasonal hindcasts using ECMWF's coupled forecasting system to perform detection and attribution studies. This novel approach leverages the superior skill and reduced biases of seasonal forecast models for climate forcing sensitivity analysis.

**Method:**
- Modified atmospheric CO2 forcing and ocean/sea ice initial conditions in retrospective forecasts
- Derived observation-based forced ocean temperature signal to amplify or remove anthropogenic warming from initial conditions
- Compared control, enhanced forcing (doubled CO2 + ocean warming), reduced forcing (fixed CO2, removed ocean warming), and aerosol-only experiments
- Analyzed impacts on temperature trends while monitoring prediction skill and model drift

**Data:**
ECMWF coupled seasonal forecasting system hindcasts with modified forcing scenarios; observation-based estimates of forced ocean temperature signals.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical gap in climate attribution: free-running models fail to reproduce observed decadal trends despite accurate forcing. Seasonal forecast models offer better trend reproduction through frequent reinitialization, making them suitable for isolating anthropogenic climate signals.

**Implications:**
Establishes counterfactual hindcasts as a diagnostic tool for quantifying model responses to CO2 and aerosol forcing while identifying systematic model deficiencies in climate sensitivity mechanisms.

**Limitations:**
- Coupled system fails to sustain strengthened tropical Pacific temperature gradients (Bjerknes feedback limitation)
- Aerosol forcing shows minimal impact due to omission of indirect aerosol effects
- Results specific to ECMWF system; generalizability unclear

**TL;DR:**
Seasonal forecast models enable improved detection and attribution of anthropogenic climate forcing by better reproducing observed trends while revealing fundamental model limitations in tropical Pacific dynamics.

# Research Summary: AI Climate Models and Climate-Zone Responses

**Main Contribution:**
Evaluates whether AI-based climate models generalize climate-zone distributions under warming in physically meaningful ways by comparing their responses to traditional physics-based models under +4K SST forcing.

**Method:**
- Applied Köppen-Geiger climate-zone classification as a diagnostic framework
- Compared AIMIP Phase 1 AI models against physics-based AMIP models
- Assessed baseline skill, per-zone responses (temperature, precipitation, specific humidity), and spatial departure patterns
- Tested generalization under prescribed warming scenarios

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis climatology (1979-2014) as baseline; AIMIP Phase 1 and AMIP model outputs under +4K SST forcing.

**Climate Relevance:**
Climate zones are shifting with global warming; accurate regional projections depend on whether AI models capture underlying thermodynamic and hydrological scaling laws rather than merely interpolating training data.

**Implications:**
Only the hybrid physics-AI model (NeuralGCM-HRD) produced physically consistent zone reorganization. Pure emulators showed systematic failures linked to land-cell treatment, suggesting architectural constraints limit their suitability for climate projection without physics constraints.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to AIMIP Phase 1 models; findings may not generalize to newer architectures or different forcing scenarios; land-cell treatment identified as critical but not fully resolved.

**TL;DR:**
Most AI climate models fail to generalize climate-zone responses consistently with physical laws, except hybrid physics-AI approaches, raising concerns for their use in climate projections.

# WaveGraph: Decadal Wave Reconstruction with Graph Neural Networks

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces WaveGraph, a Graph Neural Network model that accurately emulates basin-scale ocean wave dynamics on unstructured computational meshes for extended periods (17 years) without drift or reinitialization—a significant advance over existing deep learning approaches limited to regular grids and short forecasting horizons.

**Method:**
- Architecture: Multiscale GNN combining unstructured model mesh with uniform graph representation
- Approach: Autoregressive emulation of spectral wave model outputs
- Outputs: Significant wave height, mean period, mean direction
- Key innovation: Handles unstructured discretization with coastal resolution (2-3 km) while capturing basin-scale dynamics

**Data:**
- Training: Bias-corrected simulation data from Mediterranean Sea
- Validation: Buoy observations and satellite data
- Temporal span: 17-year continuous reconstruction period

**Climate Relevance:**
Long-term wave reconstructions are critical for understanding coastal climate impacts, sea-level rise interactions, and extreme event statistics. Decadal-scale wave data enables improved coastal risk assessments and climate model validation.

**Implications:**
- Efficient alternative to computationally expensive spectral wave models for coastal management and climate studies
- Enables rapid scenario testing and historical reconstruction without full model reintegration
- Applicable to other unstructured-domain geophysical simulations

**Limitations:**
- Validation limited to Mediterranean; generalization to other basins unclear
- Model stability drivers identified (wind forcing vs. wave history) but physical interpretability could be deeper

**TL;DR:**
GNNs can stably emulate wave models on unstructured meshes for 17-year periods, enabling efficient decadal coastal wave reconstructions for climate applications.
