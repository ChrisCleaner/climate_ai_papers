**Published:** 2026-07-17
**Categories:** cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# DELUGE: Continental-Scale Pluvial Flood Damage Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
DELUGE is a multimodal deep learning framework for daily pluvial flood damage prediction at ~1 km resolution across the continental United States. It introduces interpretable conditioning modules that expose hydrological response parameters while outperforming traditional ML baselines by 9-30% on dollar-weighted metrics.

**Method:**
- Architecture: Multimodal deep learning with parametric Value Modulator and Temporal Modulator in the hydrometeorology branch
- Conditioning: Foundation-model embeddings (AlphaEarth) + terrain descriptors enable interpretability-by-design
- Scope: Targets top 100 highest-claim 75 km cells (~81% of national pluvial flood claims) rather than full CONUS coverage
- Evaluation: Spatial block holdout validation; metric emphasizes rare, high-cost claims via dollar-weighted PR-AUC

**Data:**
- NFIP claims (2017-2022), spatially and temporally corrected
- Structured around hazard, exposure, and vulnerability components
- Baseline comparisons: Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM

**Climate Relevance:**
Pluvial flooding accounts for 45% of U.S. flood insurance claims and is harder to predict than riverine/coastal flooding; improved daily forecasting at scale supports climate adaptation and disaster risk management.

**Implications:**
Enables operational continental-scale flood damage prediction; interpretable conditioning scheme is transferable to other geospatial prediction tasks, advancing practical AI-climate applications.

**Limitations:**
Focuses on high-claim regions rather than full national coverage; limited to 2017-2022 training period; computational feasibility for real-time deployment not explicitly addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Deep learning model with interpretable foundation-model conditioning predicts daily pluvial flood damage at national scale, outperforming baselines on operationally relevant high-cost claims.

### [Improved Global Ocean Heat Content Estimation by Modeling Vertical Spatio-Temporal Dependence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11832v1)

**Authors:** Thea Sukianto, Donata Giglio, Mikael Kuusela
**Published:** 2026-07-13
**Categories:** stat.AP, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Ocean Heat Content Estimation via Spatio-Temporal Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an improved statistical method for mapping global ocean heat content (OHC) by jointly modeling two ocean pressure layers while accounting for their vertical correlation, reducing uncertainty estimates by up to 15% compared to independent layer mapping.

**Method:**
• Bivariate locally stationary Gaussian processes for joint spatial modeling of shallow and deep ocean layers
• Conditional simulations to capture cross-layer dependencies
• Unified uncertainty quantification framework eliminating complications from separate layer summation

**Data:**
Argo profiling float observations spanning 2004–2022, with sparse sampling at greater depths.

**Climate Relevance:**
Ocean heat content is the primary metric for tracking Earth's energy imbalance and climate system heat accumulation; reliable uncertainty estimates are essential for detecting statistically significant warming trends.

**Implications:**
Enables more robust regional and global OHC anomaly detection, improving confidence in climate monitoring assessments and supporting detection of climate change signals in ocean observations.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to two-layer partitioning; applicability to finer vertical resolution or other oceanographic variables not addressed; method validation restricted to 2004–2022 period.

**TL;DR:**
Joint statistical modeling of correlated ocean layers reduces OHC uncertainty by 15%, improving climate change detection reliability.

**Published:** 2026-07-14
**Categories:** econ.GN

# Research Summary: Beyond Consistent Scenarios

**Main Contribution:**
Extends Cross-Impact Balance (CIB) analysis with Linear Response Theory to move beyond identifying equilibrium scenarios toward quantifying transition effort, identifying true system levers, predicting adjustment sequences, and measuring structural resilience in socio-technical systems.

**Method:**
Exploits mathematical isomorphism between CIB drift matrices and Leontief input-output matrices to derive four closed-form analytical objects: Type I cross-impact multipliers (aggregating direct/indirect influence chains), perturbation budgets (network-weighted transition effort), impulse response functions (adjustment sequences and feedback overshoots), and unit-impulse shock profiles (attractor-specific sensitivity).

**Data:**
Expert-elicited interdependency networks encoded as pairwise influence scores; empirically applied to an energy-transition cross-impact matrix with five structural equilibria.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses energy transition dynamics by providing quantitative tools to assess feasibility, sequencing, and robustness of pathways toward decarbonization—critical for climate mitigation planning where policy consistency and system-wide leverage points matter.

**Implications:**
Enables policymakers and modelers to identify which interventions have greatest cascading effects, estimate realistic transition costs/timelines, anticipate feedback-induced overshoots, and evaluate vulnerability to external shocks—improving design of energy and climate policies.

**Limitations:**
Relies on expert elicitation quality; assumes linear response dynamics; applicability to highly nonlinear climate tipping points unclear; validation against empirical transition data not demonstrated.

**TL;DR:**
Quantifies hidden leverage points, transition effort, and shock resilience in energy-transition scenarios by combining network analysis with input-output economics.

### [Which Green Technology to Subsidize? Evidence from Electric Vehicles in South Korea](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14446v1)

**Authors:** Youngjin Hong, In Kyung Kim, Frank Verboven
**Published:** 2026-07-16
**Categories:** econ.GN
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Green Technology Subsidies for Electric Vehicles

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an economic framework comparing subsidy effectiveness across emission-reducing technologies, demonstrating that intermediate technologies (HEVs) may outperform cleaner alternatives (BEVs) depending on market conditions and electricity grid carbon intensity.

**Method:**
Discrete choice demand model incorporating mileage heterogeneity as a key driver of fuel-type selection; uses revealed preference data to estimate substitution elasticities between vehicle technologies and simulate counterfactual subsidy allocations.

**Data:**
South Korean passenger vehicle market data capturing consumer choices across battery electric vehicles (BEVs), hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), and conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses optimal climate policy design by quantifying emissions reductions from alternative subsidy strategies; accounts for lifecycle emissions including electricity generation carbon intensity, a critical variable for EV climate benefits.

**Implications:**
Policy makers should consider technology-specific substitution patterns rather than assuming cleanest-technology-first approaches; HEV subsidies may deliver superior near-term emissions reductions in markets with coal-heavy electricity grids or low EV adoption willingness; BEV subsidies become optimal only after grid decarbonization (~45% reduction in carbon intensity) or consumer preferences shift substantially.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to South Korean market; results may not generalize to regions with different grid compositions, consumer preferences, or existing vehicle fleets; does not address non-climate externalities (air quality, congestion).

**TL;DR:**
Subsidizing intermediate hybrid vehicles reduces emissions more effectively than battery electric vehicles in South Korea until electricity grids decarbonize or consumer EV adoption increases substantially.
