# SwAIther-Precip Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a lead-time-aware statistical downscaling framework that converts global AI weather model precipitation forecasts (0.25° resolution) into probabilistic kilometer-scale fields over complex terrain. Achieves 48% CRPS improvement over raw forecasts while maintaining spatial variability fidelity.

**Method:**
Two-stage architecture: (1) U-Net with feature-wise linear modulation corrects lead-time-dependent biases in coarse AIFS forecasts; (2) Diffusion model generates fine-scale spatial variability conditioned only on corrected precipitation. Lead-time conditioning is key—training across all lead times improves long-range performance by 13% at 6-day forecasts versus lead-time-specific models.

**Data:**
AIFS (AI Foundation System) global forecasts and CombiPrecip radar-gauge observational dataset over Switzerland; evaluation spans 5-6 day lead times.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in medium-range precipitation forecasting over complex alpine terrain where global models fail to resolve orographic and convective processes. Enables skillful local hazard prediction (floods, landslides) from global AI models.

**Implications:**
Provides operational pathway for translating coarse-resolution global AI forecasts into actionable kilometer-scale probabilistic precipitation fields for regional risk management and early warning systems.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to Switzerland; generalization to other complex terrain regions unclear. Computational cost of diffusion stage not explicitly discussed. Effective resolution (~4 km on 1 km grid) indicates some smoothing despite high spectral fidelity.

**TL;DR:**
Lead-time-aware bias correction enables efficient km-scale probabilistic downscaling of global AI precipitation forecasts, reducing error by 48% while preserving spatial variability.
