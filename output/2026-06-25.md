# Research Summary: ML-Based Cloud Microphysics Parameterization for ICON

**Main Contribution:**
Develops and successfully couples a machine learning-based cloud microphysics parameterization with the ICON climate model, achieving numerically stable decade-long simulations. Demonstrates that stable online coupling requires physical constraints and careful training data curation, not just offline performance.

**Method:**
Two-stage ML architecture: (1) classifier identifying active grid cells, (2) regressor predicting cloud microphysical tendencies. Physical constraints enforced include mass positivity and overshoot prevention. Trained on 5 km resolution convection-permitting simulation data, then applied to coarse-scale (~100 km) climate simulations.

**Data:**
Training data sourced from a global convection-permitting ICON simulation at 5 km resolution. Model evaluated against observed climate statistics over decade-long coupled simulations.

**Climate Relevance:**
Cloud microphysics represents a major uncertainty source in Earth system models due to its nonlinear, scale-dependent nature. Improved parameterizations directly enhance climate projection reliability and reduce systematic biases in precipitation and cloud feedbacks.

**Implications:**
Provides a pathway toward hybrid ESMs combining physics-based and ML components. Eliminates two classical tuning parameters, potentially reducing model degrees of freedom and improving generalizability across climate states.

**Limitations:**
No systematic improvements in long-term mean-state biases achieved yet compared to classical graupel scheme. Stability required extensive physical constraint engineering, suggesting domain-specific knowledge remains essential for ML climate applications.

**TL;DR:**
ML cloud microphysics parameterization achieves stable decade-long climate simulations when coupled with physical constraints, though performance parity with classical schemes requires further development.

**Published:** 2026-06-22
**Categories:** physics.geo-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Climate-Driven Landslide Risk in Mizoram

**Main Contribution:**
Applies rate-and-state friction (RSF) mechanics to 19 rainfall-triggered landslides in Mizoram to quantify how climate-driven extreme precipitation intensifies synchronous (zero-warning) slope failures. Demonstrates that normalized pore-pressure (χ) cleanly partitions failures into dynamically distinct regimes with different warning windows.

**Method:**
• Rate-and-state friction block-slider framework with pore-pressure inversion
• 1D infiltration model propagating satellite rainfall to failure depth
• RSF parameter inversion to reproduce observed failure dates across three hydraulic conductivity scenarios
• Dimensionless metric χ = μ₀ΔP/(aσ'_f) to classify failure dynamics

**Data:**
• 19 rainfall-triggered landslides (2016–2025) in/around Aizawl
• GPM satellite-derived rainfall
• CMIP6 extreme-rainfall projections (SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5)
• Miocene shale-dominated lithology characterization

**Climate Relevance:**
Projects that synchronous failures (no warning time) increase from ~56% currently to ~72% under high-emission scenarios (SSP5-8.5), driven by intensification of extreme precipitation events in northeast India.

**Implications:**
Enables physics-based failure-timing prediction for a high-hazard region; highlights urgent need for early-warning systems as climate change compresses decision windows for evacuation and mitigation.

**Limitations:**
Limited to single lithology/region; hydraulic conductivity scenarios introduce uncertainty; CMIP6 projections carry inherent climate model spread.

**TL;DR:**
Climate-intensified extreme rainfall will shift Mizoram's landslides toward synchronous failures with minimal warning time, escalating multi-site disaster risk.

**Limitations:**

### [PRecover 1.0: Process Rate Recovery with Machine Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20165v1)

**Authors:** Miriam Simm, Tom Beucler, Corinna Hoose
**Published:** 2026-06-18
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# PRecover 1.0: Process Rate Recovery with Machine Learning

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces PRecover, a machine learning post-processing framework to recover unmeasured microphysical process rates from standard weather model output, enabling detailed analysis of precipitation formation and aerosol-cloud interactions without storing computationally expensive intermediate variables.

**Method:**
Trains random forests, gradient boosting, and feed-forward neural networks using a two-step classification-regression approach to predict warm-rain and ice microphysical process rates from cloud variables. Includes physics-based baseline comparisons and provides calibrated prediction intervals via conformalized quantile regression.

**Data:**
High-resolution ICON model simulations over Europe with two-moment bulk microphysics scheme; trained on instantaneous and accumulated process rates (10-minute output intervals).

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates are critical for understanding precipitation formation pathways and aerosol-cloud interactions—key uncertainties in climate and weather prediction models. Recovering these rates improves model interpretability without increasing computational cost.

**Implications:**
Enables retrospective analysis of archived model output; reduces storage requirements for future simulations; facilitates detailed process-level validation against observations; supports improved parameterization development.

**Limitations:**
Recovery accuracy degrades significantly for process rates accumulated over longer intervals (>10 minutes); some processes (autoconversion, rain melting, heterogeneous ice nucleation) cannot be accurately recalculated from standard output alone; spatial transferability demonstrated only across two European case studies.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning recovers missing cloud microphysical process rates from model output, enabling detailed precipitation analysis without computational overhead.

# Research Summary: BESS Scheduling Under Distribution Fee Structures

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies how distribution tariffs fundamentally reshape battery energy storage (BESS) profitability and operational strategies in electricity markets. Demonstrates that grid fee structures create non-monotonic value relationships, particularly when storage is co-located with generation and consumption assets.

**Method:**
Mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) optimization model of BESS operations with rolling-horizon framework to account for forecast uncertainty and decision horizons. Four system configurations tested: standalone storage, and BESS paired with consumption, generation, or both.

**Data:**
German electricity market price data; analysis covers day-ahead market operations under varying distribution fee scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
BESS are critical flexibility assets for integrating variable renewable energy (wind/solar). Understanding fee structures' impact on storage deployment directly affects grid capacity to absorb high renewable penetration without curtailment or fossil fuel backup.

**Implications:**
Regulatory frameworks must align tariff designs with renewable integration goals—poorly structured fees can undermine storage investment despite technical viability. Co-location with generation/consumption offers resilience to fee changes, informing distributed energy resource (DER) deployment strategies.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to German market context; results may not generalize to different tariff structures or market designs. Single-node analysis; does not capture locational value or network congestion effects.

**TL;DR:**
Distribution fees significantly alter BESS profitability and operation, with co-located systems showing greater resilience than standalone storage.
