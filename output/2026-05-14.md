### [Toward AI-Driven Digital Twins for Metropolitan Floods: A Conditional Latent Dynamics Network Surrogate of the Shallow Water Equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13761v1)

**Authors:** Phillip Si, Yuan Qiu, Omar Sallam...
**Published:** 2026-05-13
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CE
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Summary: AI-Driven Digital Twins for Metropolitan Floods

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces CLDNet, a neural surrogate model that replaces expensive shallow water equation solvers for flood forecasting, achieving 115× speedup while maintaining accuracy for metropolitan-scale basins.

**Method:**
- Conditional Latent Dynamics Network: low-dimensional latent ODE driven by rainfall forcing
- Coordinate-based neural decoder conditioned on static terrain features (elevation, slope, Manning roughness)
- Pointwise decoding enables arbitrary query locations without fixed grid constraints
- Trained end-to-end on GPU; inference via neural ODE integration

**Data:**
- Synthetic 250,000-cell Texas benchmark
- 114 real-rainfall Stage IV storms over Des Plaines River basin (~4.2M cells at 30m resolution)
- Validation against USGS gauge observations; reference solver validated on April 2013 flood-of-record (NSE: 0.57–0.94)

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables rapid ensemble flood forecasting and data assimilation critical for urban climate adaptation; handles extreme precipitation events at operational timescales.

**Implications:**
Practical deployment for real-time flood early warning, scenario planning, and integration with ensemble weather forecasts; decouples computational cost from spatial resolution, scaling to larger watersheds.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to two case studies; generalization to other basins/climate regimes not demonstrated; comparison baselines (VAE–ConvLSTM, FNO) require regular grids, limiting direct comparison.

**TL;DR:**
Neural ODE surrogate reduces 55-minute flood simulations to 29 seconds while preserving forecast skill for metropolitan basins.

# GHGbench Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces the first unified, multi-entity benchmark for entity-level carbon emission prediction, spanning company and building sectors with standardized datasets, canonical evaluation splits, and comprehensive baseline comparisons to address fragmentation in existing resources.

**Method:**
Evaluates multiple modeling approaches including gradient-boosted trees, tabular foundation models, MLPs, FT-Transformers, and multimodal fusion architectures; employs multi-seed paired-bootstrap statistical testing for rigorous baseline comparison; incorporates remote-sensing embeddings for multimodal learning.

**Data:**
Company track: 32,000+ company-year records from 12,000+ firms with Scope 1+2 and Scope 3 emissions plus financial/sectoral features. Building track: 491,591 harmonized building-year records from 13 sources across 26 cities (10 U.S., 15 Australian, 1 Singapore) with climate covariates and remote-sensing data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses corporate and building-level emissions quantification—critical for Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse-gas accounting under climate disclosure frameworks (e.g., GRI, TCFD standards) and net-zero transition planning.

**Implications:**
Enables standardized ML model development for emissions prediction; facilitates cross-region transfer learning for climate policy and corporate sustainability reporting; identifies where multimodal data compensates for tabular generalization failures.

**Limitations:**
Reveals "catastrophic city transfer" failure mode; sector-factor lookup approach shows systematic ceiling; building emissions prove structurally harder than company-level prediction, suggesting domain-specific challenges remain unresolved.

**TL;DR:**
GHGbench provides the first unified benchmark for predicting company and building emissions, revealing that out-of-distribution generalization and multimodal remote-sensing data are critical bottlenecks for real-world deployment.

### [Assessment of cloud and associated radiation fields from a GAN stochastic cloud subcolumn generator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11968v2)

**Published:** 2026-05-12
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.LG

# Research Summary: GAN-Based Cloud Subcolumn Generator

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a machine learning-based stochastic cloud subcolumn generator using CVAE-GAN and U-Net architectures to improve representation of subgrid cloud variability in Earth System Models, addressing limitations of traditional analytical cloud overlap methods.

**Method:**
Two-stage deep generative model combining Conditional Variational Autoencoder with Generative Adversarial Network and U-Net architecture; generates 56 stochastic subcolumns representing cloud occurrence and optical depth profiles from coarse-resolution model grids.

**Data:**
Trained on merged CloudSat-CALIPSO satellite observations of height-resolved cloud optical depth; evaluated against Räisänen generator and ISCCP-style cloud property histograms.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a fundamental ESM limitation: cloud-radiation interactions at subgrid scales critically influence Earth's energy balance and climate sensitivity, yet models operate at ~100 km resolution while clouds span meters to kilometers.

**Implications:**
Reduces global-mean shortwave cloud radiative effect bias by 3× and halves RMSE in cloud property distributions; enables more accurate radiative transfer calculations if computational acceleration is achieved, potentially improving climate projections.

**Limitations:**
Computational feasibility on CPUs remains undemonstrated; generalization to different climate regimes or future climates not explicitly addressed; training data limited to specific satellite instruments with known biases.

**TL;DR:**
A deep generative model trained on satellite data significantly improves how climate models represent subgrid cloud variability and associated radiation, reducing a major source of climate modeling uncertainty.

# Research Summary: Generative Climate Downscaling

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a diffusion-based generative framework that downscales coarse climate model outputs to high resolution (50× increase) while preserving multivariate dependencies between meteorological variables. Reduces inter-variable correlation errors by >4× compared to existing methods, enabling accurate compound hazard assessment.

**Method:**
Diffusion-based generative model combined with bias correction that jointly models multiple meteorological variables rather than treating them independently, preserving their statistical relationships during downscaling.

**Data:**
Five meteorological variables over Japan derived from general circulation models (GCMs); specific datasets not detailed in abstract but typical inputs include temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity, and pressure.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in climate risk assessment: GCM outputs are too coarse (~100 km) for regional decision-making, yet traditional statistical downscaling breaks inter-variable correlations essential for compound hazards (simultaneous heat + drought, drought + wildfire).

**Implications:**
Enables more reliable regional climate risk assessment for infrastructure planning, agricultural adaptation, and disaster preparedness; particularly valuable for Japan's vulnerability to compound weather extremes.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to Japan region; computational cost of generative models not discussed; long-term stability of preserved correlations under climate change not addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Generative downscaling preserves variable relationships while achieving 50× resolution increase, improving compound climate risk detection.

# AIMIP Phase 1 Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Establishes the first systematic intercomparison framework for AI-based weather and climate models, analogous to traditional climate model intercomparisons. Provides standardized evaluation protocols and publicly available datasets to assess AI model performance against conventional physics-based approaches.

**Method:**
- Common experimental design: AI models simulate atmospheric conditions given prescribed historical sea surface temperatures (1979-2024)
- Standardized output formats and training constraints (reanalysis-based training)
- Five evaluation criteria: biases, trends, El Niño response, temporal variability, out-of-sample generalization

**Data:**
Historical reanalysis data (1979-2024) used for training; AIMIP Phase 1 dataset publicly released for community evaluation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses whether AI models can replicate observed climate dynamics, including long-term warming trends and climate teleconnections (El Niño), which are critical for climate projections and impact assessments.

**Implications:**
Enables objective comparison of AI vs. physics-based models for operational weather/climate prediction; identifies architectural choices affecting model reliability; supports broader adoption of AI in climate science through transparency and validation.

**Limitations:**
Some AI models underestimate historical warming trends; significant divergence in out-of-sample predictions suggests potential generalization challenges for future climate scenarios beyond training period.

**TL;DR:**
Systematic evaluation shows AI weather models match conventional models in many metrics but reveal concerning gaps in trend simulation and out-of-sample generalization, requiring further development before operational climate forecasting.

# GPROF-IR Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a CNN-based infrared precipitation retrieval algorithm that improves single-channel IR precipitation estimates by leveraging temporal information from half-hourly geostationary observations, addressing inconsistencies in merged satellite precipitation products.

**Method:**
Convolutional neural network trained to extract precipitation signals from IR observations while incorporating temporal context across consecutive satellite images; designed for seamless integration with existing GPROF-NN PMW retrievals to ensure climatological consistency.

**Data:**
Passive microwave (PMW) and infrared (IR) satellite observations; validation against independent global reference measurements; applicable to geostationary data from 1998 onward; integration planned for IMERG V08 merged product.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables improved global precipitation monitoring at half-hourly resolution—critical for understanding convective processes, water cycle dynamics, and extreme weather events; particularly valuable for regions lacking PMW coverage or during temporal gaps in microwave observations.

**Implications:**
Provides more consistent precipitation estimates for climate modeling, weather forecasting, and hydrological applications; reduces artifacts in merged products caused by PMW-IR inconsistencies; extends high-quality precipitation records backward to 1998.

**Limitations:**
Performance remains below PMW accuracy over ocean surfaces and in shallow precipitation regimes; primarily benefits continental land masses, suggesting sensor-specific physical constraints persist despite neural network optimization.

**TL;DR:**
A CNN-based IR precipitation retrieval improves single-channel satellite estimates by exploiting temporal information, advancing merged precipitation products for global climate and weather applications.

# Research Summary: Cascading Food Supply Vulnerabilities

**Main Contribution:**
This study quantifies global food security risks by modeling cascading disruptions across interconnected natural gas, fertilizer, and crop supply chains. It reveals that complete trade isolation would eliminate up to 22% of global caloric consumption, with vulnerabilities increasing substantially since the 1990s.

**Method:**
Cascading-impact model integrating bilateral trade networks across three supply chain layers (natural gas → fertilizers → crops). The model tracks shock propagation from upstream bottlenecks through downstream food production across 208 countries and 20 geopolitical blocs (1992-2023).

**Data:**
International trade data for natural gas, NPK fertilizers (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium), and 11 staple crops representing ~70% of plant-based calories; historical period spanning 31 years.

**Climate Relevance:**
While not explicitly climate-focused, the work identifies structural vulnerabilities that climate disruptions could exploit—extreme weather affecting gas/fertilizer production or crop yields would propagate through tightly coupled systems, amplifying food security risks beyond direct climate impacts.

**Implications:**
Identifies upstream gas and fertilizer sectors as critical leverage points for resilience building; highlights need for strategic reserves and supply chain diversification, particularly for import-dependent regions (EU vulnerability doubled since 1990s).

**Limitations:**
Assumes complete trade isolation (unrealistic scenario); doesn't model partial disruptions, price mechanisms, or adaptive responses; limited food stock resilience data (assumes <3 months for half of humanity).

**TL;DR:**
Global food systems face cascading collapse risks from upstream supply chain disruptions, with vulnerabilities concentrated in fertilizer and energy sectors and increasing over time.
