# AI-Based Regional Emulation for Kilometer-Scale Dynamical Downscaling

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an AI-based Limited-Area Model (LAM) that performs dynamical downscaling from 0.25° to 4-km resolution with strong generalization across diverse forcing datasets, time periods, and climate regimes. Demonstrates that weather prediction AI models can be effectively adapted for long-term regional climate applications.

**Method:**
Neural network-based emulator trained on ERA5 boundary conditions (0.25°, 3-hourly) to predict CONUS404 targets (4-km, hourly). Includes post-processing model for diagnostic variables. Evaluated on deterministic verification metrics comparable to weather-forecasting AI models.

**Data:**
Training: ERA5 (1980–2019) as forcings; CONUS404 as targets. Evaluation: Multiple forcing datasets, non-ERA5 1.0° 6-hourly data, global climate model runs (1980–2010, 2070–2100), and ensemble simulations (2025–2027).

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables high-resolution regional climate projections by downscaling coarse global climate models to kilometer scales. Captures future climate-change signals and maintains stability across 30-year simulations, critical for regional impact assessments.

**Implications:**
Provides computationally efficient alternative to traditional dynamical downscaling for climate impact studies. Transferable framework applicable to other regions; reduces computational barriers for climate adaptation planning.

**Limitations:**
Limited to two U.S. regions (Southern Great Plains, southeastern U.S.); generalization to other geographic domains unclear. No explicit discussion of uncertainty quantification or physical constraint enforcement.

**TL;DR:**
AI-based dynamical downscaling model generalizes robustly across forcing datasets and climate regimes, enabling practical kilometer-scale regional climate projections from global models.

# Research Summary: Meter-Scale Surface Weather Recovery

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that a predictable component of meter-scale (10 m resolution) near-surface weather variability can be statistically recovered from coarse atmospheric data and high-resolution surface observations, reducing forecast errors by 6-29% relative to ERA5 reanalysis.

**Method:**
Conditions coarse atmospheric state variables on sparse surface station measurements and satellite Earth observation data using a machine learning inference approach to generate spatially continuous fields of wind, temperature, and humidity at continental scale.

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis (coarse atmospheric forcing), surface station networks (sparse ground truth), and high-resolution Earth observation imagery (land cover and topography characterization) across the contiguous United States.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical gap in weather and climate modeling: sub-grid-scale surface heterogeneity drives local climate variations (urban heat islands, land-cover-dependent evapotranspiration, topographic wind effects) that influence energy budgets, precipitation patterns, and ecosystem responses—currently unresolved in operational forecasts.

**Implications:**
Enables improved local climate projections, enhanced agricultural and renewable energy forecasting, better representation of urban-rural climate interactions, and more accurate boundary conditions for high-resolution regional climate models.

**Limitations:**
Approach is demonstrated for inference rather than prediction; unclear whether temporal predictability extends beyond conditioning on static surface features; computational feasibility at global scale not addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning can recover ~30% of previously unresolved meter-scale weather variability by combining coarse atmospheric models with high-resolution surface data.

# Research Summary: ISEW Modelling and Policy Evaluation

**Main Contribution:**
First dynamic macroeconomic simulation of the Index of Sustainable Economic Welfare (ISEW) using the COMPASS ecological model. Demonstrates that ISEW better captures policy effects than GDP but requires complementary frameworks like the Doughnut to fully represent environmental boundaries.

**Method:**
• Integrated ISEW into COMPASS—an ecological macroeconomic model incorporating biophysical planetary boundaries and social thresholds
• Simulated three policy scenarios: carbon taxation, income redistribution, working-time reduction, and combined policies
• Dynamic modelling approach to capture policy interactions and temporal effects on welfare indicators

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; likely uses historical economic, social, and environmental datasets integrated into the COMPASS model framework.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses carbon pricing and environmental cost accounting in economic welfare metrics. Tests how climate/environmental policies (carbon tax) interact with social policies to affect sustainable welfare outcomes.

**Implications:**
Provides policymakers with a tool to evaluate multi-policy packages beyond GDP metrics. Suggests combined policy approaches yield stronger sustainability gains than isolated interventions. Indicates ISEW should be paired with biophysical boundary frameworks for comprehensive sustainability assessment.

**Limitations:**
ISEW alone omits full environmental costs of growth; requires complementary frameworks (Doughnut) for complete environmental guidance. Working-time reduction shows ISEW decline, suggesting potential trade-offs not fully explored.

**TL;DR:**
Dynamic ISEW modelling reveals combined social-environmental policies improve sustainable welfare better than GDP captures, but comprehensive boundary frameworks are necessary for complete policy guidance.
