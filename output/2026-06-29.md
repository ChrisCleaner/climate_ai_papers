### [Learning Climate Variability from Scarce Data with Diffusion Models: A Test Case for ENSO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27094v1)

**Authors:** Lluis Palma, Vincent Verjans, Amanda Duarte...
**Published:** 2026-06-25
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Diffusion Models for ENSO Variability

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that diffusion models can learn climate variability modes from scarce observational data by leveraging pre-training on synthetic/model data. Identifies critical sample size thresholds (~7,000 monthly observations) needed for accurate representation of ENSO dynamics.

**Method:**
Diffusion models trained on Linear Inverse Model (LIM) synthetic data, with ablation studies on training sample size and parameterization choices. Transfer learning approach: pre-train on CMIP6 climate model ensemble with learned embeddings, then fine-tune on sparse observational records (ERSSTv5).

**Data:**
Synthetic tropical Pacific SST fields from LIMs; ERSSTv5 observational data (~700 monthly samples); CMIP6 climate model ensemble for pre-training.

**Climate Relevance:**
ENSO (El Niño-Southern Oscillation) is Earth's dominant interannual climate variability mode. Accurate emulation of ENSO's asymmetric structure (Eastern vs. Central Pacific) is critical for seasonal forecasting and climate impact assessment.

**Implications:**
Transfer learning enables skillful climate emulation despite observational data scarcity—a widespread constraint for regional/long-timescale phenomena. Applicable to other sparse climate variables (paleoclimate, extreme events).

**Limitations:**
Validation restricted to synthetic LIM data; real observational modes may be more complex. Computational cost of pre-training not discussed. Generalization to non-tropical systems unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Pre-trained diffusion models overcome data scarcity to accurately capture ENSO variability, requiring 10× fewer observations than naive training.

**Published:** 2026-06-24
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.AI
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Diffusion Models for Sea State Sampling

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a diffusion-based generative model for probabilistic global sea state estimation conditioned on 5-day wind forcing histories. Extends beyond deterministic bulk variables to predict partition-related variables and derived quantities like Stokes drift and mean square slope.

**Method:**
• Diffusion-based generative model (non-autoregressive sampling)
• Conditions on global wind forcing history (5-day window)
• Directly samples complex conditional distributions of sea state
• Trained on 30-year WAVEWATCH-III hindcast data

**Data:**
30-year global WAVEWATCH-III hindcast dataset providing spectral wave model outputs for training and validation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses computational bottlenecks in coupled earth system modeling by enabling efficient, probabilistic sea state information to be integrated into climate simulations. Sea state dynamics are critical for ocean-atmosphere coupling and climate feedback mechanisms.

**Implications:**
• Enables ensemble-based probabilistic wave forecasting operationally
• Reduces computational cost relative to spectral wave models
• Facilitates online coupling of sea state to climate models
• Provides calibrated ensemble spread for uncertainty quantification in maritime applications

**Limitations:**
Not explicitly stated; potential constraints include: generalization to extreme events, regional applicability, and validation against independent observational datasets beyond hindcast comparisons.

**TL;DR:**
Diffusion models enable fast, probabilistic sea state sampling with extended variable coverage, promising efficient integration into climate simulations.

### [From stable online coupling to decade-long climate simulations: A machine learning parameterization for cloud microphysics in ICON](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23829v1)

**Authors:** Ellen Sarauer, Mierk Schwabe, Philipp Weiss...
**Published:** 2026-06-22
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: ML-Based Cloud Microphysics Parameterization for ICON

**Main Contribution:**
Develops and successfully couples a machine learning-based cloud microphysics parameterization to the ICON Earth system model, achieving numerically stable decade-long climate simulations. Demonstrates that stable online coupling requires physical constraints and careful training data curation beyond offline model performance.

**Method:**
Two-stage ML architecture: (1) classifier identifying active grid cells, (2) regressor predicting cloud microphysical tendencies. Physical constraints enforced include mass positivity and overshoot prevention. Trained on 5 km resolution convection-permitting simulation data; applied to coarser-resolution climate simulations.

**Data:**
Training data from global convection-permitting ICON simulation at 5 km resolution; validation against decade-long coupled simulations and observed climate metrics.

**Climate Relevance:**
Cloud microphysics parameterization is a major source of uncertainty in ESM projections. ML approach addresses scale-dependent nonlinearities that traditional schemes struggle to represent, potentially improving climate prediction skill.

**Implications:**
Provides pathway toward hybrid ESMs combining physics-based and ML components. Eliminates two tuning parameters from classical schemes, reducing model degrees of freedom. Foundation for improved cloud representation in next-generation climate models.

**Limitations:**
No systematic improvements in long-term mean-state biases compared to classical graupel scheme despite stable coupling. Generalization to unseen atmospheric regimes remains a concern; training-application domain gap requires careful management.

**TL;DR:**
ML cloud microphysics parameterization achieves stable decade-long climate simulations when physical constraints are enforced, though performance gains over traditional schemes are not yet realized.

### [Boundary condition fidelity for bottom-hole pressure and CO2 plume prediction in geological carbon storage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27515v1)

**Authors:** Romal Ramadhan, Seyyed A. Hosseini, Larry W. Lake
**Published:** 2026-06-25
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.geo-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Boundary Condition Fidelity for CO2 Storage Simulation

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies optimal boundary condition treatments for truncated-domain simulations of geological carbon storage, demonstrating that corner pore-volume conservation is critical for accurate bottom-hole pressure (BHP) and CO2 plume prediction.

**Method:**
Compares ten boundary-condition approaches (uniform pore-volume multipliers, transmissibility modifiers, corner-adjusted corrections, layered and gradual modifiers) against full-domain reference simulations using RMSE, normalized RMSE, peak pressure deviation, and Intersection over Union (IoU) metrics in homogeneous and heterogeneous reservoir models.

**Data:**
Synthetic reservoir simulation datasets; no empirical field data mentioned. Tests conducted on both homogeneous and heterogeneous permeability structures.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports carbon capture and storage (CCS) deployment by improving simulation accuracy for CO2 injection operations, a key mitigation strategy for reducing atmospheric CO2 concentrations.

**Implications:**
Enables computationally efficient reduced-domain simulations without sacrificing prediction fidelity, reducing computational cost while maintaining regulatory-grade accuracy for CO2 storage site characterization and monitoring.

**Limitations:**
Synthetic-only validation; lacks comparison with field-scale injection data. Findings may not generalize to extreme heterogeneity, anisotropy, or multi-phase flow regimes beyond CO2-brine systems.

**TL;DR:**
Corner pore-volume conservation combined with gradual transmissibility modification achieves <3.7% pressure error and >97% plume prediction accuracy in truncated-domain CO2 storage simulations.

### [Frictional timescales and the impact of climate change-driven extreme weather on rainfall-triggered landslides in Mizoram, NE India](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23281v1)

**Authors:** Pritom Sarma, Krishnendu Paul
**Published:** 2026-06-22
**Categories:** physics.geo-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Climate-Driven Landslide Risk in Mizoram

**Main Contribution:**
Applies rate-and-state friction (RSF) mechanics to 19 observed rainfall-triggered landslides in Mizoram to quantify how climate-intensified extreme rainfall shifts slope failures toward unpredictable, synchronous regimes with minimal warning time.

**Method:**
- Rate-and-state friction block-slider framework to model slow-to-fast failure transitions
- 1D infiltration model propagates satellite rainfall (GPM) to failure depth under three hydraulic conductivity scenarios
- RSF parameters inverted to reproduce observed failure dates; dimensionless pore-pressure parameter χ used to classify failure regimes

**Data:**
- 19 rainfall-triggered landslides (2016–2025) in/around Aizawl, Mizoram
- GPM satellite-derived rainfall
- CMIP6 extreme-rainfall projections (SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5 scenarios for northeast India)

**Climate Relevance:**
Projects that synchronous (zero-warning) landslide failures increase from ~56% currently to ~72% under high-emission scenarios (SSP5-8.5), driven by intensification of extreme precipitation events in a warming climate.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates urgent need for real-time early-warning systems in high-risk regions; highlights that climate change may render traditional warning windows obsolete for clustered, multi-site failures during extreme rainfall events.

**Limitations:**
Regional focus (Aizawl); limited to shale-dominated lithology; assumes 1D infiltration; validation limited to 19 events over 9-year window.

**TL;DR:**
Climate-intensified extreme rainfall in northeast India will shift most landslides toward unpredictable synchronous failure, eliminating warning time for disaster mitigation.

# Research Summary: Seasonal Climate Predictability Beyond Chaos Limits

**Main Contribution:**
Explains why seasonal climate phenomena like Indian Summer Monsoon Rainfall (ISMR) can be predicted 18+ months ahead despite chaotic dynamics. Demonstrates that lag synchronization between coupled climate oscillators enables extended predictability beyond deterministic chaos limits.

**Method:**
- Models two interacting oscillatory climate systems using coupled Complex Ginzburg-Landau Equations
- Employs complex order parameters to capture internal dynamics of coupled systems
- Analyzes lag synchronization between ISMR and Global El Niño-Southern Oscillation (G-ENSO) predictor
- Generates aperiodic yet strongly correlated time series to explain long-lead predictability

**Data:**
Indian Summer Monsoon Rainfall (ISMR) observations and Global ENSO indices; specific datasets not detailed in abstract.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses the fundamental paradox of seasonal climate predictability—monsoon systems exhibit chaotic weather yet show predictable multi-seasonal signals through coupling with ocean oscillations like ENSO, critical for monsoon-dependent regions.

**Implications:**
- Advances theoretical foundation for seasonal forecasting across multiple climate systems
- Enables improved monsoon prediction for agricultural and water resource planning in South Asia
- Provides framework for identifying predictable signals in other coupled climate subsystems

**Limitations:**
Abstract lacks validation metrics, skill scores, or comparison with operational forecasting models; generalizability to other climate systems unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Lag synchronization between coupled climate oscillators (ISMR-ENSO) enables predictability far beyond chaos limits through Complex Ginzburg-Landau dynamics.
