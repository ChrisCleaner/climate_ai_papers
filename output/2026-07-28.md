# Research Summary: Spatial Generalization in ML-based Weather Models

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces spatial generalization tests (planet rotation/reversal) to evaluate whether ML weather models learn physically consistent relationships rather than regional climate patterns. Demonstrates that GraphCast and NeuralGCM fail these tests despite strong present-day performance.

**Method:**
• Coordinate transformations: reverse/rotate planet in longitude/latitude while adapting boundary conditions and forcings
• Comparison against physics-based GCMs (which pass with rounding errors only)
• Analysis of variable mappings to identify spurious correlations vs. causal relationships

**Data:**
Present-day weather/climate data used to train GraphCast and NeuralGCM (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in ML model robustness: as boundary conditions shift due to sea ice loss, land-use change, and ocean warming, models trained on present-day climate must generalize to novel climate states. Spatial generalization is prerequisite for temporal generalization under climate change.

**Implications:**
ML-based weather/climate models require explicit physical consistency constraints in architecture and training to avoid overfitting to regional patterns. Current state-of-the-art models may fail in altered climate regimes despite accurate near-term forecasts.

**Limitations:**
Tests applied to only two models; unclear whether failures stem from architecture, training data, or loss functions. Generalization to other ML architectures not addressed.

**TL;DR:**
ML weather models learn regional climate correlations rather than universal physics, failing to generalize to spatially transformed scenarios—a critical vulnerability for climate change applications.

# BeDA: Building Decarbonisation-Disclosure Auditor

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces BeDA, a multimodal LLM-based audit tool to assess whether corporate building-sector disclosures contain sufficient data (carbon intensity per m²) for regulatory stranding assessments. Reveals a critical reporting gap: only 21.5% of firms disclose operational carbon intensity in standardized metrics.

**Method:**
Multimodal LLM instrument that extracts and validates standards-compliance scores from corporate disclosures. Reliability tested across model families with inter-extractor agreement (κ=0.95-0.97) and convergent validation against three external criteria.

**Data:**
Global firm panel of 2,246 companies (2003-2023); stratified analysis of 200 firm-reports (general built-environment sector) and 519 real-estate firm-reports; 215 reports with constructible carbon intensity metrics.

**Climate Relevance:**
Buildings represent 34% of global final energy use and 37% of energy-related CO₂ emissions. Identifies a measurement bottleneck preventing enforcement of emerging stranding regulations (NYC Local Law 97, EU EPBD recast) designed to align building portfolios with 1.5°C pathways.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates that 39% of analyzable real-estate portfolios already exceed CRREM 1.5°C intensity limits. Provides a scalable monitoring tool for targeted disclosure mandates; suggests regulatory focus should address reporting gaps rather than relying on credibility signals.

**Limitations:**
Screening tool only—does not forecast stranding risk. Portfolio size confounds credibility-readiness relationship. Regional variation (Europe 64-74% vs. US 37% disclosure rates) limits generalizability.

**TL;DR:**
LLM audit reveals most building-sector firms lack standardized carbon-intensity reporting needed for climate stranding regulation, creating an enforceable disclosure gap.

Meta-analysis of 4,720 elasticity estimates spanning 90 years reveals price responsiveness has remained remarkably stable despite technological transformation.

**Data:**

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**
- Price-based demand management is insufficient; policymakers must invest in enabling infrastructure (smart grids, storage, demand response contracts)
- Time-of-use pricing alone does not increase total consumption responsiveness

**Limitations:**
Meta-analysis quality depends on underlying study heterogeneity; elasticity estimates vary by market structure, consumer demographics, and measurement methodology, potentially obscuring context-specific effects.

**TL;DR:**
Ninety years of data show electricity demand remains stubbornly price-inelastic regardless of technology; decarbonization strategies cannot rely on price signals alone.

# Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Predicting Nitrous Oxide Flux

**Main Contribution:**
Applies Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to predict agricultural N₂O emissions by embedding mechanistic equations from the DayCent process-based model as physics constraints. Demonstrates that physics constraints improve out-of-distribution generalization despite reducing in-distribution accuracy.

**Method:**
MLP-based PINN trained with a hybrid loss function combining data-driven MSE and physics residuals derived from DayCent biogeochemical equations. Physics weighting hyperparameter λ controls the trade-off between fitting observations and satisfying mechanistic constraints.

**Data:**
Multi-site agricultural dataset spanning four geographically distinct US agricultural sites; evaluated using both holdout validation and leave-one-site-out cross-validation.

**Climate Relevance:**
N₂O is the dominant ozone-depleting substance and third-largest anthropogenic greenhouse gas contributor (>70% from agriculture); accurate flux prediction is critical for emissions inventories and climate mitigation strategies in agricultural systems.

**Implications:**
PINNs offer a pathway to improve model transferability across different soil conditions and agricultural regions, potentially enabling better emissions forecasting for climate policy without requiring extensive site-specific calibration.

**Limitations:**
Physics constraints degrade in-distribution performance; cross-site generalization remains poor (negative R² on held-out geographically distinct site), suggesting the approach requires further development for practical deployment across diverse agricultural regions.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed neural networks improve N₂O flux prediction robustness across unfamiliar sites but struggle with true cross-site generalization, trading in-distribution accuracy for out-of-distribution plausibility.

First mechanistic interpretability study of a weather foundation model (Microsoft's Aurora) fine-tuned for air quality prediction, revealing that the model captures statistical patterns rather than learning explicit atmospheric chemistry processes.

**Method:**
- Controlled chemical perturbations applied to model forecasts tested against known photochemical relationships
- Internal representation analysis via sparse autoencoders to identify causally relevant components
- Comparison of learned mechanisms against process-based chemical transport models

**Data:**
Reanalysis data used for pretraining Aurora; model fine-tuned on atmospheric chemistry observations for air quality forecasting.

**Climate Relevance:**
Air quality forecasting is critical for environmental health and policy decisions; foundation models offer computational advantages over traditional chemical transport models but their reliability for policy applications depends on whether they learn actual chemistry versus exploiting training data artifacts.

**Implications:**
High forecast skill alone is insufficient validation for AI systems informing environmental policy; mechanistic interpretability testing should complement benchmark metrics to ensure models capture real atmospheric processes rather than statistical regularities.

**Limitations:**
Study focuses on single model (Aurora); framework applicability to other foundation models not yet demonstrated; sparse autoencoders may not fully capture all relevant internal mechanisms.

**TL;DR:**
Aurora forecasts air quality accurately but learns meteorological patterns rather than explicit atmospheric chemistry, raising concerns about its reliability for policy applications without mechanistic validation.
