**TL;DR:**

# HydroFirn Research Summary

Develops the first large-scale multidimensional model for firn hydrology on the Greenland Ice Sheet, moving beyond existing one-dimensional approaches. Enables realistic simulation of meltwater dynamics and ice layer formation across spatial heterogeneities.

**Method:**
- Multiphase, thermomechanical numerical model coupling unsaturated-saturated flow, heat transfer, and phase change
- Novel computational efficiency: pressure equation solved only in saturated regions, reducing computational burden
- Handles spatially heterogeneous boundary conditions and dynamic ice layer formation

**Data:**

**Implications:**

- Enables more accurate sea-level rise predictions

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**

**Method:**

• Variable-importance methods: attributes variance in energy use to specific drivers (prices, policies, GDP, climate)

**Data:**
Panel dataset of 50 U.S. states plus DC (2006–2022); includes energy consumption, prices, GDP, climate variables, and state-level efficiency policy indicators.

**Climate Relevance:**
Identifies policy levers for reducing energy-related emissions. Shows efficiency regulations and pricing mechanisms are more effective than relying on economic growth or climate factors alone for lowering energy intensity.

**Implications:**
Pricing mechanisms and regulatory policies are primary drivers of energy efficiency gains. Results support carbon pricing and efficiency standards as effective climate mitigation strategies; suggests GDP growth alone won't reduce per capita energy use without complementary policies.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to U.S. states; cross-state variation may not generalize internationally. Causality between policies and outcomes inferred from correlations rather than experimental design.

**TL;DR:**
Efficiency policies and energy prices explain ~39% of cross-state energy variation, far outweighing GDP and climate factors, positioning regulation and pricing as primary levers for emissions reduction.
