# Research Summary: Tropical Cyclone Frequency Under Climate Warming

**Main Contribution:**
Reconciles conflicting tropical cyclone frequency projections by demonstrating a consistent late-century decline driven by nonlinear interactions among environmental factors. Shows that global TC frequency will decline by 2–8% per 2°C of warming, with confidence intervals narrowing when conditioned on specific warming levels.

**Method:**
Environment-dependent probabilistic TC model driven by 14 CMIP6 climate models under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 emissions scenarios. Counterfactual decomposition analysis isolates individual and interactive contributions of environmental predictors (absolute vorticity, vertical wind shear, relative humidity, potential intensity) to frequency changes.

**Data:**
CMIP6 multi-model ensemble (14 models); intermediate and high-emissions scenarios; historical and future projections through end-of-century.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical uncertainty in TC hazard assessment under climate change. TC frequency projections directly inform disaster risk management, infrastructure planning, and climate adaptation strategies in vulnerable tropical and subtropical regions.

**Implications:**
Enables probabilistic risk assessment conditioned on warming scenarios; supports policy decisions on coastal resilience and disaster preparedness with reduced uncertainty bounds; demonstrates that nonlinear environmental interactions dominate over individual factor contributions.

**Limitations:**
Model-dependent results; probabilistic framework may not capture rare extreme events; regional variations masked by global aggregation; assumes environmental predictors adequately represent TC physics.

**TL;DR:**
Nonlinear interactions among atmospheric variables drive a consistent global tropical cyclone frequency decline of 2–8% per 2°C warming, reconciling previously divergent model projections.
