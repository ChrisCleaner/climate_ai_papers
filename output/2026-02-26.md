# AI-Based Regional Emulation for Kilometer-Scale Dynamical Downscaling

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an AI-based Limited-Area Model (LAM) that performs dynamical downscaling from 0.25° to 4-km resolution with strong generalization across diverse forcing datasets, time periods, and climate regimes. Demonstrates that weather prediction AI models can be effectively adapted for long-term regional climate applications.

**Method:**
Neural network-based emulator trained on ERA5 boundary conditions (0.25°, 3-hourly) to predict CONUS404 targets (4-km, hourly). Includes post-processing model for diagnostic variables and is evaluated on deterministic verification metrics comparable to operational weather AI models.

**Data:**
Training: ERA5 (1980–2019) as forcing, CONUS404 as target. Evaluation: Multiple forcing datasets (ERA5 and non-ERA5), present-day simulations (2021–2024), and global climate model downscaling (1980–2010, 2070–2100 periods).

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables high-resolution regional climate projections by downscaling coarse global climate models to 4-km scales, critical for capturing local climate impacts in the Southern Great Plains and southeastern U.S. Demonstrates stability across 30-year climate change scenarios.

**Implications:**
Provides computationally efficient alternative to traditional dynamical downscaling for regional climate assessments. Transferable framework for adapting AI weather models to climate applications; supports ensemble climate projections without ensemble spread collapse.

**Limitations:**
Limited to two regional domains; long-term stability beyond 30 years not tested; generalization to other climate regions or forcings unclear.

**TL;DR:**
AI-based regional downscaling model generalizes robustly across forcing datasets and climate regimes, enabling practical 4-km resolution climate projections from global models.

### [Weak 21st-century AMOC response to Greenland meltwater in a strongly eddying ocean model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17235v1)

**Published:** 2026-02-19

# Research Summary: AMOC Response to Greenland Meltwater

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies Greenland meltwater's impact on Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) weakening through 2100 using a high-resolution eddy-resolving ocean model. Demonstrates that meltwater-induced AMOC weakening (0.6 ± 0.2 Sv) is modest compared to warming-driven weakening and is insensitive to model resolution.

**Method:**
High-resolution (1/10° horizontal resolution) ocean model simulation under SSP5-8.5 emissions scenario with explicit mesoscale eddy representation. Comparison between high and low-resolution runs to isolate resolution effects; analysis of large-scale AMOC controls to explain state-dependent meltwater sensitivity.

**Data:**
SSP5-8.5 climate forcing scenario; Greenland meltwater input; model output through 2100; comparison across different horizontal resolutions.

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC weakening is a critical climate tipping point affecting Northern Hemisphere heat distribution and regional sea-level rise. Greenland ice sheet melt is an accelerating freshwater source; understanding its AMOC impact is essential for 21st-century climate projections.

**Implications:**
Reduces structural uncertainty in climate models by demonstrating that eddy-resolving simulations and meltwater inclusion produce consistent results. Suggests background ocean state (not model resolution) drives meltwater sensitivity, informing future climate model design priorities.

**Limitations:**
Single model study; results may not generalize across different ocean model frameworks; SSP5-8.5 only (high-emissions scenario).

**TL;DR:**
Greenland meltwater causes modest additional AMOC weakening independent of model resolution, with ocean state being the dominant control.

# Research Summary: ISEW Modelling and Policy Response

**Main Contribution:**
First dynamic macroeconomic simulation of the Index of Sustainable Economic Welfare (ISEW) using the COMPASS model, evaluating how carbon taxation, income redistribution, and working-time reduction affect sustainable welfare outcomes.

**Method:**
Integrated ISEW into COMPASS, an ecological macroeconomic model incorporating the Doughnut framework (biophysical planetary boundaries + social thresholds). Simulated policy scenarios dynamically to capture interconnected effects on the ISEW indicator.

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; appears to use COMPASS model's existing macroeconomic and environmental datasets, though specific sources are not detailed.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses climate policy effectiveness by modeling carbon tax impacts on sustainable welfare. Quantifies trade-offs between emissions reduction, economic activity, and social wellbeing—critical for climate-aligned policy design.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates that combined policies (carbon tax + redistribution + working-time reduction) maximize sustainable welfare gains. Suggests ISEW provides better policy guidance than GDP alone, though the Doughnut framework offers more comprehensive environmental accountability for policymakers.

**Limitations:**
ISEW omits full environmental costs of growth; working-time reduction shows counterintuitive negative ISEW effects requiring further investigation. Model-dependent results; real-world policy implementation complexity not addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Dynamic ISEW modeling reveals that combined social-environmental policies improve sustainable welfare better than GDP metrics capture, but comprehensive boundary frameworks better guide climate-aligned policymaking.
