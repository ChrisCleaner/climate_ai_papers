# Research Summary: Rapid SST Estimation from Sparse Observations

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces Sparse Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (S-DEIM), a hybrid AI approach that reconstructs high-resolution global sea surface temperatures from sparse in situ measurements (0.2% of grid points) without requiring physics-based models.

**Method:**
Combines empirical interpolation with recurrent neural networks (RNNs): the instantaneous component uses sparse observations directly, while the temporal component learns historical SST patterns from 33 years of NOAA data. The two-term formulation balances immediate observational constraints with learned spatiotemporal dynamics.

**Data:**
Training: NOAA weekly high-resolution SST dataset (1989–2021). Testing: January 2022–January 2023. Sparse input: 100 in situ observations per reconstruction.

**Climate Relevance:**
Accurate, rapid SST reconstruction is critical for weather forecasting, climate monitoring, and understanding ocean-atmosphere interactions. Current sparse observational networks limit forecast skill; S-DEIM bridges this gap without computationally expensive assimilation models.

**Implications:**
Enables real-time SST field reconstruction from buoys, ships, and autonomous platforms. One-minute offline training and sub-second inference make it operationally viable for weather centers and climate services. Robust to random sensor placement (1–2% error degradation).

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to one test year; generalization to extreme events or climate regime shifts unclear. Requires historical training data; applicability to data-sparse regions or novel climate states not addressed.

**TL;DR:**
S-DEIM reconstructs global SST fields from 0.2% sparse observations using RNNs, achieving 40% accuracy improvement over prior methods with real-time computational efficiency.

# StormDiT: Precipitation Nowcasting Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
StormDiT introduces a unified generative AI model that bridges the 2-6 hour "gray zone" in precipitation forecasting by learning coupled atmospheric physics without decomposing weather into separate components. The model achieves stable 6-hour predictions of convective precipitation at 6-minute resolution with superior probabilistic calibration.

**Method:**
Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture treating weather evolution as a holistic spatiotemporal problem; learns entangled atmospheric processes (e.g., convection initiation from boundary advection) end-to-end rather than decomposing into deterministic advection and stochastic diffusion components.

**Data:**
7,720 precipitation events from China (training); SEVIR public benchmark (validation/testing); heavy-rainfall test set for evaluation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses critical gap in extreme precipitation warning systems—the 2-6 hour horizon where traditional extrapolation fails and numerical weather prediction is computationally prohibitive. Essential for disaster mitigation in regions vulnerable to flash flooding and severe convection.

**Implications:**
Enables operational early-warning systems with quantified uncertainty; demonstrates AI can capture non-local physical precursors (outflow boundaries) implicitly, validating emergent understanding of convective organization without explicit physics encoding.

**Limitations:**
Training limited to Chinese precipitation events; generalization to other regions/climates unclear; computational cost and operational deployment requirements not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
A unified generative AI model achieves stable 6-hour precipitation forecasting in the critical 2-6 hour gap by learning coupled atmospheric physics holistically rather than through component decomposition.
