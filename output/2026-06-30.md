### [Routes to rare events with optimally timed perturbations: a Tent Map is all you need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29703v1)

**Authors:** Justin Finkel
**Published:** 2026-06-29
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, nlin.CD, physics.data-an
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Routes to Rare Events with Optimally Timed Perturbations

**Main Contribution:**
Establishes a theoretical framework for optimizing the "advance split time" (AST) parameter in rare event sampling (RES) algorithms. Derives a simple log-ratio rule that determines when to perturb simulations to most efficiently generate extreme event scenarios.

**Method:**
• Uses dynamical systems theory applied to the Logistic and Tent maps (1D chaotic systems)
• Derives optimal AST as the time required for initial perturbations to amplify to the magnitude of the target extreme event
• Generalizes findings via maximum-entropy criterion to extend beyond idealized cases

**Data:**
No empirical datasets; analysis is theoretical using archetypal mathematical models (Logistic and Tent maps).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses computational bottlenecks in extreme weather simulation—a critical challenge for climate risk assessment. Rare event sampling is essential for understanding low-probability, high-impact events (hurricanes, heat waves, precipitation extremes) where observational data is sparse.

**Implications:**
Provides principled guidance for hyperparameter selection in RES methods, potentially enabling more efficient computational sampling of extreme climate scenarios and improved risk quantification for decision-makers.

**Limitations:**
Results derived in idealized 1D chaotic systems; applicability to high-dimensional atmospheric models requires further validation. No empirical testing on real climate data or complex models presented.

**TL;DR:**
A theoretical framework optimizes when to perturb climate simulations to efficiently sample rare extreme events, with implications for more computationally tractable risk assessment.

**Method:**

**Limitations:**
Limited to three pilot regions and two variables; perfect-model design may not fully capture real-world observational uncertainties; generative model computational costs not discussed.

**TL;DR:**

• CMIP6 climate model ensemble (pre-training)

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical challenge in climate emulation: capturing El Niño/Southern Oscillation (ENSO) asymmetry and non-Gaussian variability with limited observational records. ENSO is a primary driver of global climate variability on interannual timescales.

**Implications:**

**Limitations:**
• Validation limited to synthetic LIM data; real-world applicability to complex observational modes unclear
• Computational cost of CMIP6 pre-training not discussed
• Generalizability to non-tropical climate modes unexplored

**TL;DR:**
Diffusion models need 7,000+ samples to learn ENSO correctly, but CMIP6 pre-training overcomes observational data scarcity.

**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Boundary Condition Fidelity for CO2 Storage Simulation

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies optimal boundary condition treatments for truncated-domain simulations of geological carbon storage, demonstrating that corner pore-volume conservation is critical for accurate bottom-hole pressure and CO2 plume prediction.

**Method:**
Comparative evaluation of ten boundary-condition approaches (uniform pore-volume multipliers, transmissibility modifiers, corner-adjusted corrections, layered corrections, gradual modifiers) against full-domain reference simulations using metrics: BHP RMSE/NRMSE, peak pressure deviation, and plume Intersection over Union (IoU).

**Data:**
Synthetic homogeneous and heterogeneous reservoir models; no field datasets explicitly mentioned.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports safe and effective geological carbon storage (GCS)—a critical climate mitigation technology for permanent CO2 sequestration. Improved simulation fidelity reduces deployment risk and operational uncertainty.

**Implications:**
Practitioners can reduce computational cost via domain truncation without sacrificing prediction accuracy by adopting corner-adjusted boundary treatments with gradual modifiers. Achieves <3.7% BHP error and >0.97 plume IoU, enabling reliable operational monitoring and safety assurance in CCS projects.

**Limitations:**
Limited to synthetic models; validation against field data or complex geometries not demonstrated. Transmissibility correction shows reservoir-type dependence, suggesting further investigation needed for heterogeneous systems.

**TL;DR:**
Corner pore-volume conservation with gradual transmissibility modifiers optimizes truncated-domain CO2 storage simulations, balancing computational efficiency and prediction fidelity.

### [Two kinds of robustness are not the same: disentangling fault tolerance and low-SNR robustness in multi-domain event detection on real data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29339v1)

**Published:** 2026-06-28
**Categories:** physics.geo-ph, cs.LG, eess.SP

# Research Summary: Robustness in Multi-Domain Event Detection

**Main Contribution:**
Disentangles two distinct types of robustness in event detectors—fault tolerance (sensor failures) and low-SNR robustness (noise)—demonstrating that training methodology (sensor-dropout) rather than architectural complexity drives noise resilience across seismic, DAS, and industrial domains.

**Method:**
- CEPHALON: fault-tolerant detector trained with per-sample sensor-dropout
- Baselines: 1D CNN, temporal convolutional network, compact Transformer
- Unified evaluation framework: 8-channel, 256-sample standardized representation
- Systematic ablations isolating training recipe vs. architectural redundancy effects

**Data:**
Three physically distinct real-world sources: Hi-net seismic waveforms, Utah FORGE 2024 borehole distributed acoustic sensing (DAS), and MAFAULDA industrial vibration data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports induced-seismicity monitoring for CO₂ Capture and Storage (CCS) operations and geothermal energy development—both critical for climate mitigation infrastructure.

**Implications:**
Simple architectures with appropriate training protocols achieve superior low-SNR performance compared to complex models; practitioners should prioritize training strategy over model complexity for noisy real-world deployments in CCS and geothermal monitoring.

**Limitations:**
Limited to binary event detection; generalization to multi-class problems or other geophysical phenomena unclear; evaluation restricted to three specific domains.

**TL;DR:**
Training with sensor-dropout, not model architecture, enables robust event detection in noisy conditions critical for climate-relevant CCS and geothermal monitoring.
