### [Scalable Uncertainty Quantification for Extreme Weather Forecasting via Empirical Neural Tangent Kernels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02886v2)

**Authors:** Jose Marie Antonio Miñoza, Rex Gregor Laylo, Sebastian C. Ibañez
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CE
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: NTK-Based Uncertainty Quantification for Extreme Weather Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes Neural Tangent Kernel-based uncertainty quantification (NTK-UQ) for deep learning weather models that produces calibrated, adaptive prediction intervals without retraining. Identifies architecture-dependent failure modes and proposes a data-driven rule to select optimal decomposition methods (ICA vs. SVD).

**Method:**
- Uses empirical features from last layer of neural weather models with NTK theory
- Applies Independent Component Analysis (ICA) or SVD to feature eigenspectra based on concentration ratio
- ICA exploits heavy-tailed, non-Gaussian structure of extreme events via higher-order statistics (kurtosis, negentropy)
- Inference requires single matrix-vector product per sample; no model retraining needed

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; evaluation on four neural architectures (spectral operators and attention-based models) for extreme weather prediction.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in deep learning weather models: deterministic forecasts lack uncertainty estimates essential for high-stakes extreme weather decisions (tropical cyclones, severe events). Enables risk-aware operational forecasting.

**Implications:**
- 31–37% sharper prediction intervals vs. conformal prediction baseline at 90% coverage
- Adaptive intervals scale with event severity (conformal methods cannot)
- Inference-time only; deployable on existing trained models
- Improves decision-making for emergency response, resource allocation during extremes

**Limitations:**
Architecture-dependent performance; spectral operators require aggressive truncation (k ≤ 10) risking variance collapse; generalization to other extreme weather types unclear.

**TL;DR:**
NTK-based framework provides calibrated, adaptive uncertainty for neural weather models by exploiting heavy-tailed extreme event statistics, achieving sharper intervals than conformal baselines without retraining.

### [Probabilistic storyline attribution using machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02550v1)

**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** stat.AP, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Probabilistic Storyline Attribution Using Machine Learning

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces distributional autoencoders (DAEs) to generate climate counterfactuals for extreme weather attribution by modeling temperature distributions conditional on atmospheric circulation and warming levels. This overcomes limitations of physical models in transferring storyline counterfactuals across different climate forcing states.

**Method:**
• Distributional autoencoders trained on coupled climate model simulations
• Models full spatial distribution of European temperature fields conditioned on: (1) atmospheric circulation state, (2) global warming level
• Derives conditional probability ratios from learned distributions
• Evaluated against factual and counterfactual climate model simulations

**Data:**
Fully coupled climate model simulations; ERA5 reanalysis for observed circulation patterns; illustrative case study uses 2003 European heatwave data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Advances event attribution science—a critical tool for quantifying anthropogenic climate change's contribution to specific extreme weather events. Enables probabilistic assessment of how similar atmospheric patterns would manifest under different warming scenarios.

**Implications:**
Provides quantitative projections of extreme event intensification (e.g., 2003-like heatwave intensity increasing ~3°C by 2053) and changing occurrence probabilities, supporting climate risk assessment and adaptation planning.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to European temperature; generalizability to other regions/extremes unclear; relies on climate model fidelity for training data.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning enables probabilistic climate counterfactuals for extreme weather attribution by conditioning temperature distributions on circulation patterns and warming levels.

# Research Summary: ML for CO2 Storage Dynamics

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a machine learning framework combining convolutional neural network surrogates with Bayesian inference to enable efficient forward prediction and inverse parameter estimation for CO2-brine multiphase flow in geological media, demonstrated on the FluidFlower experimental system.

**Method:**
- CNN surrogate trained on high-fidelity numerical simulations to predict CO2 saturation and dissolved CO2 concentration fields
- Surrogate embedded in Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) framework for Bayesian parameter inference
- Identifies identifiable vs. non-identifiable parameter combinations from experimental observations

**Data:**
High-resolution, time-resolved observations from the FluidFlower controlled laboratory rig (heterogeneous porous media system); training data from numerical simulations across wide parameter ranges.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports geological carbon dioxide storage (CCS)—a critical climate mitigation strategy—by enabling accurate prediction and characterization of CO2 migration in subsurface formations.

**Implications:**
Reduces computational cost by orders of magnitude compared to traditional simulations, making parameter uncertainty quantification tractable for field-scale CO2 storage projects; improves calibration using full-field observations rather than sparse metrics.

**Limitations:**
Demonstrated only on controlled laboratory system; transferability to heterogeneous field-scale geology and long-term storage timescales requires further validation; parameter identifiability remains limited in early plume evolution stages.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed neural network surrogates accelerate Bayesian inference for CO2 storage characterization, enabling efficient uncertainty quantification for climate-critical carbon sequestration applications.

# RIFTES: RTM- and Iteration-Free Temperature-Emissivity Separation

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes RIFTES, a computationally efficient framework that eliminates iterative procedures and radiative transfer modeling (RTM) requirements for land surface temperature (LST) retrieval. Achieves superior accuracy (RMSE 1.06 K in simulations) while reducing computational time by 62–74% compared to existing methods.

**Method:**
- Reformulates the iterative TES algorithm into a closed-form mathematical solution, eliminating computational loops
- Integrates a physics-informed deep residual neural network for atmospheric correction using readily available parameters
- Employs a masking mechanism to flexibly incorporate atmospheric constraints when data are available

**Data:**
- Synthetic radiative transfer simulations for algorithm validation
- In-situ measurements from globally distributed validation sites
- Real satellite data: ECOSTRESS (Ecosystem Spaceborne Thermal Radiometer Experiment on Space Station) and ABI (Advanced Baseline Imager)

**Climate Relevance:**
Accurate LST retrieval is critical for monitoring land-atmosphere interactions, evapotranspiration, soil moisture, and climate feedback mechanisms. Improved efficiency enables near-real-time LST products for climate monitoring and environmental assessment.

**Implications:**
Enables operational deployment on resource-constrained platforms; reduces latency for time-sensitive climate and weather applications; improves global LST monitoring consistency across multiple satellite sensors.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to clear-sky conditions; practical performance on ABI shows higher RMSE (1.97 K) than ECOSTRESS, suggesting sensor-specific uncertainties remain.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed neural network replaces iterative algorithms to retrieve land surface temperature 62–74% faster with 24–32% lower uncertainty.

# Conformal Risk Sharing: Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a distribution-free mechanism for fairly allocating financial costs of rare adverse events across groups while guaranteeing no participant is materially worse off. Combines interpretable cost-sharing policies with conformal calibration to provide certified, individual-level obligation caps without assuming data distributions.

**Method:**
• Split conformal calibration paired with a tunable sharing intensity policy
• Training data optimizes sharing parameters; held-out calibration data generates distribution-free per-agent guarantees
• Validity under exchangeability assumption (no distributional assumptions required)

**Data:**
Synthetic datasets plus real-world applications including precipitation records and energy-cooperative data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to climate risk pooling mechanisms—precipitation variability and energy system impacts are core climate adaptation challenges. Enables equitable burden-sharing for climate-related financial losses (e.g., extreme weather damage, renewable energy shortfalls) across communities or cooperative members.

**Implications:**
Facilitates credible climate risk-sharing agreements by providing transparent, mathematically-certified guarantees. Particularly valuable for energy cooperatives and climate-vulnerable communities seeking insurance-like mechanisms without actuarial assumptions.

**Limitations:**
Exchangeability assumption may not hold under non-stationary climate trends; scalability to large heterogeneous populations unclear; real-world validation limited to specific sectors.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal calibration enables fair, certified cost-sharing for rare climate events with individual-level guarantees and no distributional assumptions.
