### [Evaluating the Generalizability of Foundation Models for Extreme Environmental Events: Case Study of California Wildfire PM2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07951v1)

**Authors:** Yongcan Huang, Li Jiang, Ze Yu Liu
**Published:** 2026-07-08
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Foundation Models for Wildfire PM2.5 Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**  
First systematic evaluation of time series foundation models (TSFMs) for extreme wildfire smoke PM2.5 prediction, revealing that smaller, fully-trained recurrent models outperform larger pretrained foundation models on out-of-distribution extreme events.

**Method:**  
Comparative benchmark of six TSFM configurations (TimesFM, Chronos-2, Moirai-2, Time-MoE—zero-shot and LoRA fine-tuned) versus LSTM/BiLSTM/Transformer baselines using leave-one-incident-out (LOIO) cross-validation. Evaluation metrics: MAE, RMSE, and exceedance F1 at EPA AQI thresholds across 6–24-hour forecasting horizons.

**Data:**  
12-year hourly PM2.5 dataset (2013–2025) from 79 California air quality monitoring sites covering 1,375 wildfire incidents.

**Climate Relevance:**  
Addresses critical gap in forecasting rare, hazardous-level air quality extremes driven by climate-amplified wildfire activity; directly supports public health adaptation to smoke events.

**Implications:**  
BiLSTM achieves superior performance (MAE 5.16 μg/m³, exceedance F1 0.63 at Hazardous threshold) for operational wildfire air quality prediction; challenges assumption that scale and pretraining universally improve environmental forecasting; LoRA fine-tuning can partially bridge the gap but does not eliminate recurrent model advantage.

**Limitations:**  
Single-region focus (California); limited exploration of why recurrent architectures excel at extremes; no analysis of computational cost-benefit tradeoffs.

**TL;DR:**  
Smaller, task-trained recurrent models outperform large foundation models for wildfire PM2.5 extremes, suggesting domain-specific training remains essential for hazardous environmental forecasting.

### [Domain-Adaptive Climate Downscaling Under Temporal Distribution Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05645v1)

**Published:** 2026-07-06
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Domain-Adaptive Climate Downscaling Under Temporal Distribution Shift

**Main Contribution:**
Addresses the temporal out-of-distribution (OOD) problem in climate downscaling by proposing a domain-adaptive deep learning framework that maintains model performance when applied to future climate projections with different statistical distributions than training data.

**Method:**
Combines supervised high-resolution (HR) reconstruction on historical data with domain alignment techniques to bridge the distributional gap between historical and future climate states. The framework uses paired low-resolution (LR) and HR climate model simulations to learn robust downscaling relationships.

**Data:**
Paired LR-HR climate model simulations over the Continental United States, with daily temperature as the primary variable. Training on historical periods and validation on future projection periods with varying temporal distribution shifts.

**Climate Relevance:**
Critical for generating reliable high-resolution climate projections needed for regional impact assessments, adaptation planning, and extreme weather analysis. Addresses a fundamental challenge in translating global climate models to actionable local-scale information under non-stationary climate conditions.

**Implications:**
Enables more trustworthy downscaled climate projections for decision-making in water resources, agriculture, and disaster preparedness. Particularly valuable for complex terrain where topographic effects amplify downscaling challenges.

**Limitations:**
Focused on daily temperature over one region; generalization to other variables (precipitation, wind) and geographic areas requires further validation. Computational cost of domain adaptation not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Domain adaptation improves deep learning-based climate downscaling robustness when models encounter future climate distributions substantially different from historical training data.

# Inunda: GPU-Native Differentiable Flood Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Inunda is a GPU-accelerated, fully differentiable solver for 2D shallow water equations that combines physics-based hydraulic accuracy with automatic parameter calibration via gradient descent, enabling high-resolution flood inundation modeling across large domains in minutes.

**Method:**
- Solves shallow water equations using mass-conservative local-inertial scheme
- GPU-native implementation with autograd-compatible operators enabling end-to-end differentiability
- Parameters estimated through reverse-mode automatic differentiation against observational data
- Integrates with precipitation ensembles for probabilistic forecasting

**Data:**
Hurricane Harvey (2017) hindcast in Harris County, Texas; July 2025 Central Texas flash flood event; Rio Ruidoso post-fire flood case study with gage observations and surveyed high-water marks.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need for rapid, accurate flood prediction under extreme precipitation events intensified by climate change; enables real-time probabilistic forecasting and post-disaster recovery assessment in fire-affected watersheds.

**Implications:**
Operational flood forecasting with 2x improvement over National Water Model; automated calibration of spatially-explicit hydraulic properties; accessible open-source pipeline for coupling physics-based modeling with modern machine learning workflows.

**Limitations:**
Limited to 2D shallow water equations (3D effects not captured); validation on three case studies (generalization to other regions/flood types unclear); computational requirements for real-time operational deployment not fully specified.

**TL;DR:**
Differentiable GPU solver combines physics-based flood hydraulics with automatic calibration, achieving operational-grade accuracy while enabling rapid ensemble forecasting and parameter recovery.

### [Connecting Microseismicity to Lithology via a Model of Slip Avalanches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06886v1)

**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Microseismicity and Lithology via Slip Avalanche Modeling

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that a universal micromechanical model of slip avalanches predicts microseismic event statistics across vastly different scales (nanometers to meters) and diverse geological materials, enabling prediction of induced earthquake sizes and frequencies from subsurface fluid injection operations.

**Method:**
Applies statistical physics model of slip avalanches in slowly deforming solids to connect nanoindentation experiments (nanometer-micrometer scale) with field microseismic catalogs (meter scale). Model explains deviations in layered formations through finite-size effects and stress-integration at mechanically weak bedding planes.

**Data:**
Microseismic catalogs from high-pressure fluid injection in geological basins with varying lithologies; nanoindentation experiments on shale samples across multiple temperatures and mineral compositions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to geologic carbon storage (GCS)—a critical climate mitigation technology—by predicting induced seismicity risks during CO₂ injection. Also relevant to enhanced geothermal systems for renewable energy and unconventional oil/gas operations.

**Implications:**
Enables probabilistic forecasting of triggered earthquake magnitudes based on basin structure and lithology; improves operational safety for carbon storage and geothermal projects; provides quantitative framework for assessing induced seismicity hazards in climate-critical applications.

**Limitations:**
Model validation limited to specific lithologies (primarily shale); applicability to complex, heterogeneous real-world basins with multiple lithologic layers requires further testing.

**TL;DR:**
A universal slip avalanche model predicts induced microseismic statistics across scales, enabling safer geologic carbon storage and geothermal operations critical for climate mitigation.
