# Water Vapor Buoyancy and the African Easterly Jet

**Main Contribution:**

Shows this moisture effect intensifies under global warming, fundamentally altering understanding of tropical circulation dynamics.

**Method:**
Diagnostic analysis using thermal wind balance equations with explicit inclusion of moisture effects on density. Quantifies the competing contributions of meridional temperature and moisture gradients to AEJ strength through reanalysis-based calculations and CMIP6 model intercomparison.

**Data:**
Atmospheric reanalysis data (unspecified product, likely ERA5 or similar) and CMIP6 climate model ensemble outputs for both historical and future warming scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
The AEJ is a critical driver of dust transport and easterly wave genesis (hurricane precursors). Accurate representation of moisture-circulation feedbacks is essential for projecting tropical cyclone activity and Saharan dust impacts under climate change.

**Implications:**
Reveals potential systematic biases in climate models that omit vapor buoyancy from governing equations, undermining reliability of regional climate projections for West Africa and Atlantic hurricane forecasting. Highlights moisture distribution as a first-order control on large-scale circulation.

**Limitations:**
Analysis focuses on time-mean diagnostics; transient moisture-circulation interactions not addressed. Model intercomparison limited to CMIP6; unclear which specific models lack vapor buoyancy treatment.

**TL;DR:**
Moisture gradients reduce the African easterly jet by 30% and strengthen this effect under warming, but some climate models ignore this physics entirely.
