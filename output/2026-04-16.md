# Research Summary: Sea-Level Change from U.S. Vehicle Emissions

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies the long-term sea-level rise impacts of U.S. vehicle emissions using an integrated climate-sea-level modeling framework. Extends EPA analyses by incorporating probabilistic uncertainty and multi-century timescales, revealing substantially larger regional effects than previously estimated.

**Method:**
Couples the FaIR (Finite Amplitude Impulse Response) climate model with the BRICK (Building blocks for Relevant Ice and Climate Knowledge) sea-level model, applying probabilistic weighting to represent joint climate-sea-level uncertainty. Tests multiple emissions scenarios including baseline SSP2-4.5, EPA-consistent mitigation, stalled technology, and pre-regulation counterfactuals.

**Data:**
EPA vehicle emissions data and projections; climate and sea-level model outputs; U.S. coastal downscaling regions (with emphasis on Gulf of Mexico).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses transportation sector contributions to global mean sea-level rise (GMSLR) and regional coastal impacts—critical for understanding sectoral climate mitigation effectiveness and informing vehicle emissions policy.

**Implications:**
Vehicle emissions regulations yield ~1-2 cm GMSLR reduction by 2100 but >6 cm by 2200, with amplified local effects on U.S. coastlines. Demonstrates that near-term EPA estimates underestimate long-term and regional benefits of transportation decarbonization.

**Limitations:**
Apparent reliance on specific model configurations (FaIR/BRICK); regional downscaling methodology not detailed; uncertainty quantification approach requires clarification.

**TL;DR:**
U.S. vehicle emissions reductions provide modest near-term but substantial multi-century sea-level benefits, with regionally amplified coastal impacts.

# MAPL-EMIT: Deep Learning for Global Methane Point Source Detection

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces MAPL-EMIT, a vision transformer framework that automates detection and quantification of methane point sources from space-based hyperspectral imagery, eliminating labor-intensive manual plume identification workflows.

**Method:**
End-to-end vision transformer jointly processes complete radiance spectra from EMIT to simultaneously retrieve methane enhancements, delineate plumes, and localize sources across all pixels in a scene. Trained on 3.6 million physics-based synthetic plumes injected into real EMIT radiance data.

**Data:**
EMIT hyperspectral radiance measurements; 1,084 EMIT granules for testing; validation against airborne data, landfill emissions, and controlled release experiments.

**Climate Relevance:**
Anthropogenic methane is a potent near-term climate forcing agent. Rapid, scalable identification of point sources (landfills, oil/gas facilities) enables targeted mitigation strategies and supports climate accountability at facility scale.

**Implications:**
Shifts methane monitoring from manual annotation to automated, high-throughput processing of global satellite data. Detects 79% of known plumes and identifies twice as many plausible sources as human analysts, enabling rapid emissions inventory updates and facility-level climate action.

**Limitations:**
Validation primarily against NASA benchmarks and known sources; generalization to non-EMIT instruments unclear; false-positive rates require filtering via spectral fit scores and noise estimates.

**TL;DR:**
Vision transformer automates global methane point source detection from satellite hyperspectral data, enabling scalable climate monitoring at facility scale.

**Implications:**
