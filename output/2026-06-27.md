# Research Summary: Climate-Driven Landslide Risk in Mizoram

**Main Contribution:**
Applies rate-and-state friction (RSF) mechanics to 19 rainfall-triggered landslides in Mizoram to quantify how climate-intensified precipitation shifts slope failures toward unpredictable, synchronous regimes with minimal warning time.

**Method:**
- Rate-and-state friction block-slider framework to model slow-to-fast failure transitions
- 1D infiltration model propagates satellite rainfall (GPM) to failure depth under three hydraulic conductivity scenarios

**Data:**
- 19 rainfall-triggered landslides (2016–2025) in/around Aizawl, Mizoram

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**
Demonstrates urgent need for early-warning systems in high-landslide-frequency regions; climate-driven clustering of synchronous failures poses cascading hazard risks to infrastructure and populations in vulnerable mountainous terrain.

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: Abrupt Transition to Superrotation in Idealized GCM

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies the dynamical mechanisms governing whether Earth's tropical atmosphere transitions abruptly or gradually to equatorial superrotation (westerly zonal winds). Demonstrates that this transition type is controlled by meridional temperature gradient and bottom friction in idealized climate models.

**Method:**
Numerical experiments using the Held-Suarez idealized GCM framework, systematically varying parameters to isolate competing feedback mechanisms: a positive tropical wave-jet feedback and a negative extratropical wave absorption feedback.

**Data:**
No observational datasets; study relies entirely on idealized model simulations with controlled parameter variations.

**Climate Relevance:**
Superrotation is a predicted feature of very warm paleoclimates and potential future high-CO₂ scenarios. Understanding transition mechanisms is critical for assessing atmospheric circulation changes in extreme climate states.

**Implications:**
Provides mechanistic understanding to reconcile conflicting model predictions about superrotation onset, improving confidence in climate projections for warm climates and informing model parameterization choices.

**Limitations:**
Restricted to idealized Held-Suarez framework; results may not directly transfer to comprehensive GCMs with realistic physics, topography, and ocean coupling. Uncertainty remains about applicability to Earth-like configurations.

**TL;DR:**
Superrotation transitions depend on competition between tropical wave-jet amplification and extratropical wave damping, controlled by temperature gradients and surface friction.

**TL;DR:**
