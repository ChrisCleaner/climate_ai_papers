# Research Summary: TC Inner-Core Vector Winds from CYGNSS

**Main Contribution:**
Reconstructs full 2D surface wind vectors inside tropical cyclone cores at 1.5 km resolution globally using only sparse scalar wind speed observations from CYGNSS satellite, addressing a critical observational gap in intensity forecasting.

**Method:**
• Score-based diffusion assimilation extended to nonlinear observation operators
• Three TC boundary-layer physics constraints injected to recover wind direction from scalar-only data
• Novel Observation Coverage Sufficiency (OCS) criterion flags reliable reconstructions without external validation

**Data:**
• CYGNSS scalar wind speeds (4,955 snapshots, 249 TCs, 2020–2022)
• Validation: IBTrACS best-track, ERA5, CCMP, Tail Doppler Radar (47 storms), dropsondes
• Coverage: All six active TC basins globally

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables consistent, high-resolution monitoring of TC intensity and structure across undersampled ocean basins lacking routine aircraft reconnaissance, critical for understanding storm behavior in a changing climate.

**Implications:**
• ~79% reduction in maximum wind speed bias vs. operational analyses
• Supports operational intensity forecasting and storm-surge prediction globally
• Demonstrates practical pathway for multi-sensor fusion (CYGNSS + SFMR/SAR/scatterometer)

**Limitations:**
Reconstruction quality depends on CYGNSS coverage sufficiency; performance degrades in sparse-observation scenarios (7.5 m/s RMSE overall vs. 6.9 m/s for coverage-sufficient cases).

**TL;DR:**
AI-driven diffusion assimilation recovers 2D TC wind vectors from satellite scalar observations alone, providing global kilometre-scale inner-core winds for improved forecasting.
