### [Scalable Uncertainty Quantification for Extreme Weather Forecasting via Empirical Neural Tangent Kernels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02886v2)

**Authors:** Jose Marie Antonio Miñoza, Rex Gregor Laylo, Sebastian C. Ibañez
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CE
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: NTK-Based Uncertainty Quantification for Extreme Weather

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes Neural Tangent Kernel-based uncertainty quantification (NTK-UQ) for deep learning weather models that produces calibrated, adaptive prediction intervals without retraining. Identifies architecture-dependent failure modes and provides a data-driven rule to select optimal decomposition methods (ICA vs. SVD).

**Method:**
- Applies empirical NTK theory to last-layer features for post-hoc UQ
- Analyzes variance collapse mechanism: eigenvalue truncation rank determines when GP correction destroys discrimination between extreme/routine events
- Uses Independent Component Analysis (exploiting kurtosis/negentropy) for heavy-tailed extreme weather; SVD for Gaussian-like regimes
- Data-driven selection via eigenspectrum concentration ratio; inference requires single matrix-vector product per sample

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; evaluation on four neural network architectures (spectral operators and attention-based models implied).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in operational extreme weather forecasting: deep learning models match numerical weather prediction speed/accuracy but lack uncertainty estimates essential for tropical cyclone prediction and high-stakes decision-making during extreme events.

**Implications:**
31–37% sharper 90% prediction intervals than conformal prediction baseline; uniquely provides adaptive intervals scaling with event severity, enabling risk-proportional warnings for emergency management and climate adaptation planning.

**Limitations:**
Abstract does not detail computational overhead of ICA decomposition; generalization to other extreme weather types (floods, heatwaves) unclear; evaluation limited to four architectures.

**TL;DR:**
NTK-based post-hoc uncertainty quantification for neural weather models achieves sharper, severity-adaptive prediction intervals by selecting decomposition methods based on feature spectrum properties.

### [Probabilistic storyline attribution using machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02550v1)

**Authors:** Frieder Loer, Maybritt Schillinger, Sebastian Sippel
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** stat.AP, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Probabilistic Storyline Attribution Using Machine Learning

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces distributional autoencoders (DAEs) to generate probabilistic climate counterfactuals for extreme weather attribution. This overcomes the limitation that physical climate models cannot directly simulate how the same atmospheric circulation pattern would manifest under different climate forcing states.

**Method:**
• Distributional autoencoders model full conditional distributions of temperature fields given atmospheric circulation state and global warming level
• Enables derivation of conditional probability ratios comparing factual vs. counterfactual scenarios
• Trained on coupled climate model simulations; validated across multiple climate states

**Data:**
• Fully coupled climate model simulations (training)
• ERA5 reanalysis (atmospheric circulation patterns for case study)
• Climate model counterfactual simulations (validation)

**Climate Relevance:**
Advances event attribution science by quantifying how anthropogenic climate change modulates extreme weather intensity and probability. Directly applicable to understanding forced changes in heat extremes—a key climate impact.

**Implications:**
Enables rapid generation of storyline counterfactuals for policy-relevant scenarios (e.g., "how would the 2003 heatwave differ in 2028/2053?"). Provides probabilistic frameworks for communicating climate risk to stakeholders beyond deterministic model outputs.

**Limitations:**
• Evaluation limited to European temperature; generalizability to other regions/variables unclear
• Depends on climate model quality and representativeness of training simulations
• Assumes circulation patterns remain stationary across climate states (may not hold for all extremes)

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning enables probabilistic climate counterfactuals by modeling temperature distributions conditional on atmospheric circulation and warming level, improving attribution of extreme events to climate change.

### [Multiscale Dynamics of Heatwave Persistence and Intensity Under Climate Change](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02005v1)

**Authors:** Md Tasim Ferdous, Reda Snaiki, Abdelatif Merabtine
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Multiscale Dynamics of Heatwave Persistence Under Climate Change

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an integrated workflow linking heatwave characteristics to underlying atmospheric dynamical modes using multiresolution dynamic mode decomposition (mrDMD). Demonstrates that regional heatwave amplification under climate change is driven by persistence-dominated regimes rather than frequency increases alone.

**Method:**
- Heatwave identification: Fixed 90th percentile threshold (2001-2010 baseline) with 15-day moving window and minimum-duration criterion
- Dynamical analysis: mrDMD applied to daily mean temperature fields to extract coherent multiscale structures
- Integration: Heatwave-conditioned mode participation ratios and spatial alignment analysis (hotspot overlap, Spearman rank correlation) connect event metrics to dynamical modes

**Data:**
CORDEX-NAM12 regional climate simulations (CRCM5 downscaling of CanESM5) over southern Canada; three future periods (2016-2025, 2051-2060, 2091-2100) under SSP5-8.5 emissions scenario.

**Climate Relevance:**
Explains why some regions experience disproportionate heatwave intensification under warming—a critical gap in climate impact assessment. Identifies low-frequency atmospheric modes as physical drivers of persistent heat events.

**Implications:**
Enables process-informed regional heatwave projections for adaptation planning. By late century, continental interior regions face ~26.7-day median heatwave durations with 6.88 K intensity increases, informing infrastructure and public health resilience strategies.

**Limitations:**
Single downscaling model (CRCM5); analysis limited to southern Canada; high-emissions scenario (SSP5-8.5) only; no ensemble uncertainty quantification presented.

**TL;DR:**
Multiscale dynamical analysis reveals that future heatwaves in continental regions will be dominated by persistence rather than frequency, driven by weaker atmospheric damping at low frequencies.

# Conformal Risk Sharing: Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a distribution-free mechanism for fairly allocating financial costs of rare adverse events among group participants while guaranteeing individual obligation caps. Combines interpretable risk-sharing policies with conformal calibration to ensure no participant is materially worse off without assuming known probability distributions.

**Method:**
- Splits data into training and calibration sets
- Trains sharing intensity policy on training data
- Uses split conformal calibration on held-out data to generate per-agent, distribution-free guarantees
- Produces both a redistribution rule and certified obligation caps valid under exchangeability assumption

**Data:**
Synthetic datasets plus real-world applications including precipitation records and energy-cooperative data.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**
Conformal calibration enables certified, fair cost-sharing for climate disasters with guaranteed individual obligation caps and no distributional assumptions.

# RIFTES: RTM- and Iteration-Free Temperature-Emissivity Separation

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes RIFTES, a computationally efficient framework that replaces iterative temperature-emissivity separation (TES) with a closed-form mathematical solution and integrates physics-informed deep learning for atmospheric correction. Achieves superior accuracy (RMSE 1.06 K in simulations) while reducing computational time by 62–74% compared to existing methods.

**Method:**
- Reformulates iterative TES into a non-iterative, closed-form solution through mathematical derivation
- Employs deep residual neural network with embedded radiative transfer physics for atmospheric correction
- Incorporates masking mechanism to flexibly apply atmospheric constraints when available
- Eliminates radiative transfer model (RTM) dependency for improved efficiency

**Data:**
- Synthetic simulation data (validation against split-window, TES, and hybrid algorithms)
- In-situ measurements from globally distributed validation sites
- ECOSTRESS (spaceborne thermal radiometer on ISS) and ABI (Advanced Baseline Imager) satellite data

**Climate Relevance:**
Land surface temperature (LST) is a critical variable for monitoring surface energy balance, evapotranspiration, and land-atmosphere interactions essential to climate modeling and drought assessment.

**Implications:**
Enables rapid, accurate LST retrieval from thermal satellite data with reduced computational burden, facilitating near-real-time monitoring applications and integration into operational climate/weather systems.

**Limitations:**
Paper does not explicitly discuss failure modes under non-clear-sky conditions or performance degradation with extreme atmospheric conditions; generalization to other sensor types unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed deep learning replaces iterative algorithms to achieve faster, more accurate satellite-based land surface temperature retrieval.
