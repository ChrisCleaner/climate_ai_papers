# FESOM2-JAX v1.0 Summary

**Main Contribution:**
A fully differentiable Python reimplementation of the FESOM2 ocean-sea-ice model in JAX that preserves the original's unstructured-mesh physics while enabling GPU acceleration and automatic differentiation for gradient-based optimization.

**Method:**
- JAX-based rewrite of Fortran FESOM2 using large language models for translation, verified kernel-by-kernel against original
- Maintains finite-volume, cell-vertex formulation on unstructured meshes
- End-to-end differentiable via reverse-mode automatic differentiation through full time loops
- Scales from single CPU/GPU to 256 GPUs with identical physics

**Data:**
1958–2019 hindcast at ~1° equivalent resolution using identical forcing as original FESOM2; validation against observations for temperature, salinity, heat content, and sea ice.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables efficient simulation of coupled ocean-sea-ice dynamics at CMIP-class complexity, critical for understanding decadal-scale climate variability and sea-ice feedbacks in global climate projections.

**Implications:**
- Facilitates gradient-based parameter calibration and sensitivity analysis at mesh-vertex resolution
- Allows seamless integration of machine-learning components with physics-based ocean models
- Reduces computational barriers for experimenting with new numerics and parameterizations
- ~113 simulated years/day on 4 GH200 superchips enables rapid ensemble studies

**Limitations:**
Communication bandwidth (not arithmetic) limits scaling; largest tested mesh (~7.4M vertices) scales to 128 GPUs; transferability of hybrid physics-ML developments back to Fortran version requires validation.

**TL;DR:**
First CMIP-complexity ocean model on unstructured meshes made fully differentiable and GPU-native, enabling efficient gradient-based optimization and physics-ML hybrid modeling.

# Research Summary: Network-Based Streamflow Alteration Mapping

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces the Streamflow Alteration Index (SAI), a network-based screening metric that maps how dams and water infrastructure alter streamflow across entire river networks at high spatial resolution, enabling assessment at both gauged and ungauged locations.

**Method:**
• Routes point-based alteration signals (from dams/reservoirs) downstream through a hydrologic routing network
• Generates paired indices (SAI_I for confirmed sources, SAI_II for potential sources) on 0-100% scale
• Validates classification accuracy against independent reference datasets (RHBN gauges, WSC labels)

**Data:**
Ontario Lake and River Routing Product v2 (OLRRP v2); inventory of 643 alteration sources from provincial datasets; Reference Hydrometric Basin Network (RHBN) gauges; Water Survey of Canada (WSC) natural/regulated classifications.

**Climate Relevance:**
Supports climate adaptation by enabling objective assessment of how existing and planned water infrastructure affects downstream hydrology and aquatic ecosystems under changing precipitation/flow regimes.

**Implications:**
Provides scalable, objective screening tool for hydrologic modeling and ecosystem assessment without requiring detailed dam operations data; applicable at national-to-global scales for infrastructure impact planning.

**Limitations:**
Validation shows 74% of regulated gauges classified as conditional/natural (vs. WSC-regulated label), suggesting potential underestimation of alteration in some cases; requires comprehensive infrastructure inventory.

**TL;DR:**
Network-based index maps streamflow alteration from water infrastructure across river systems at high resolution, validated in Ontario and scalable for large-domain applications.

**Data:**

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses building energy demand forecasting—critical for demand-side management, grid stability, and reducing operational carbon emissions in the built environment.

**Implications:**

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to 50 buildings; backbone-aware and client-aware performance variation suggests method requires careful tuning; computational cost of routing mechanism not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Personalized federated sparse adaptation with mixture-of-experts improves building energy forecasting by balancing privacy, transfer learning, and client heterogeneity better than global or purely local approaches.
