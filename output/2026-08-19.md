**Authors:** Shrey Gupta, Yi Ming, George Mohler
**Published:** 2026-08-18
**Categories:** cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Crop-Yield Forecasting During Extreme Drought

**Main Contribution:**
Evaluates machine learning and deep learning models for predicting corn yield under extreme drought conditions, identifying distribution shift as a key challenge and proposing sample weighting and feature selection to improve non-deep learning forecasts.

**Method:**
Compares traditional ML models with deep learning (VITA) using 16 meteorological drivers as predictors. Addresses data sparsity through sample weighting and feature selection to handle the domain shift between historical training data and extreme 2012 drought conditions.

**Data:**
County-level corn yield data from the US Corn Belt with 16 meteorological predictors; characterized by spatial sparsity (missing county yields) and temporal sparsity (subset of daily values per year).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses forecasting crop production during extreme weather events—a critical climate adaptation challenge as drought frequency and intensity increase under climate change.

**Implications:**
Improved yield forecasting under extreme conditions supports agricultural planning, food security assessments, and climate risk management at regional scales; demonstrates that standard ML modifications can enhance robustness when training-test distributions diverge.

**Limitations:**
Deep learning model (VITA) shows limited improvement from proposed modifications despite superior baseline performance; findings specific to corn yield in one region may have limited generalizability.

**TL;DR:**
Sample weighting and feature selection improve traditional ML crop-yield forecasts during extreme drought, though deep learning models remain superior but less responsive to these modifications.

# Research Summary: AI Climate Models and Climate-Zone Response to Warming

**Main Contribution:**
Evaluates whether AI-based climate models generalize climate-zone distributions under warming in physically meaningful ways by comparing their responses to traditional physics-based models under +4K SST forcing.

**Method:**
- Applied Köppen-Geiger climate-zone decomposition to AIMIP Phase 1 AI models
- Compared AI responses against physics-based AMIP models under prescribed warming scenarios
- Assessed baseline classification skill, per-zone responses (temperature, precipitation, specific humidity), and spatial departure structures

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis climatology (1979-2014) used as baseline; models evaluated under +4K sea-surface temperature forcing scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
Climate-zone shifts are a critical indicator of regional climate change impacts. Accurate AI model representation of these shifts is essential for reliable regional climate projections and understanding how precipitation and temperature patterns will reorganize geographically.

**Implications:**
Only the hybrid physics-AI model (NeuralGCM-HRD) reproduced thermodynamic and hydrological scaling relations consistent with established climate physics. This suggests that purely data-driven AI emulators require architectural modifications—particularly in land-cell treatment—to be suitable for climate projection applications.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to AIMIP Phase 1 models; failure modes traced to architectural choices but solutions not fully developed; generalization to other warming scenarios unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Most AI climate models fail to realistically reorganize climate zones under warming due to architectural limitations, with only hybrid physics-AI approaches showing physically consistent responses.

# WaveGraph: Decadal Wave Reconstruction with Graph Neural Networks

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces WaveGraph, a Graph Neural Network model that accurately emulates basin-scale ocean wave dynamics on unstructured coastal meshes over decadal timescales without drift or reinitialization.

**Method:**

- Approach: Autoregressive emulation of spectral wave model outputs (significant wave height, mean period, mean direction)
- Key feature: Operates on unstructured discretization (2-3 km coastal resolution) rather than regular grids

Mediterranean Sea basin-scale simulations; validation against buoy observations and satellite data; 17-year continuous reconstruction period.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Implications:**
Provides computationally efficient alternative to full spectral wave models for coastal risk assessment, climate projections, and operational forecasting on unstructured domains; demonstrates GNN capability for stable long-horizon environmental emulation.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to Mediterranean region; generalization to other basins or climate scenarios not demonstrated; dependence on quality of input bias-corrected simulation data.

**TL;DR:**

### [Quantifying internal variability in large-ensemble climate data with the Wasserstein distance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14455v1)

**Authors:** Yuki Yasuda, Shoichiro Kido
**Published:** 2026-08-14
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, physics.comp-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Wasserstein Distance for Climate Variability

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces a novel metric using 1-Wasserstein distance (optimal transport) to quantify the relative magnitude of internal climate variability in large-ensemble simulations. The metric is distribution-agnostic and computationally simple, requiring only quantile sorting and differencing.

**Method:**
Computes two 1-Wasserstein distances separately for forced and internal variability components, then calculates their ratio as a normalized metric. The approach leverages optimal transport theory to measure distributional differences without assuming Gaussianity or requiring parameter tuning.

**Data:**
Validated on synthetic data (Gaussian, uniform, lognormal distributions) and applied to Community Earth System Model Large Ensemble (CESM-LE), analyzing 2m air temperature and total precipitation under multiple forcing scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses a fundamental challenge in climate science: separating anthropogenic climate signals from natural internal variability. Accurate quantification is essential for attribution studies and constraining uncertainty in climate projections.

**Implications:**
Enables more robust analysis of large-ensemble climate datasets; demonstrates that internal variability's relative contribution decreases with increased forcing. Applicable to any climate variable and ensemble configuration with ≥40 members.

**Limitations:**
Requires minimum ensemble size (~40 members) for stable estimates; validation limited to single model (CESM-LE). Computational efficiency gains over existing methods not explicitly quantified.

**TL;DR:**
Wasserstein distance provides a simple, distribution-free metric for quantifying internal variability in climate ensembles, outperforming existing methods across diverse variable types.

# Research Summary: Paleoclimate Boundary Conditions as Out-of-Sample Tests for Ocean Climate Emulators

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that autoregressive neural network emulators trained on short timescales can generalize to novel orbital forcings in paleoclimate settings, but reveals critical failures in reproducing slow ocean interior dynamics and internal variability—establishing the mid-Holocene as a diagnostic testbed for climate emulator validation.

**Method:**
Autoregressive, full-depth ocean emulators trained on short time steps; evaluated against mid-Holocene climate model output. Includes comparison with baseline models that infer ocean state directly from boundary forcings. Linear superposition analysis decomposes responses to individual forcing components across training epochs.

**Data:**
Mid-Holocene experiment from a numerical climate model; in-distribution but out-of-sample climate scenario with different orbital forcings than training data.

**Climate Relevance:**
Long-term climate projections require emulators that accurately capture forced ocean responses over centuries—a critical gap between weather prediction (short timescales) and climate modeling (long timescales). Paleoclimate scenarios provide ground-truthed validation without requiring future observations.

**Implications:**
Identifies that convergence on training metrics (mean state) does not guarantee capture of dynamical processes needed for forced-response skill. Suggests paleoclimate experiments are valuable for diagnosing emulator failures before deployment to out-of-distribution future climates.

**Limitations:**
Emulators underestimate response amplitudes; fail to reproduce slow internal ocean evolution; testing limited to single paleoclimate scenario (mid-Holocene).

**TL;DR:**
Neural network ocean emulators generalize to novel forcings in paleoclimate but fail on slow dynamics, revealing that training metrics alone cannot validate climate projection skill.

# Research Summary: SST-Prescribed Simulations and Earth's Climate Feedback

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that the standard method of prescribing observed sea-surface temperatures (SSTs) to atmospheric models fails to accurately estimate temporal changes in Earth's climate feedback parameter. Identifies statistical noise and missing atmospheric forcing as critical limitations of this widely-used approach.

**Method:**
Controlled experiments comparing prescribed-SST atmospheric simulations against fully-coupled historical simulations. Uses moving-window regression analysis to detect feedback parameter trends, validated against 4000-year pre-industrial control runs to establish noise baselines.

**Data:**
Observed SST evolution; SSTs from fully-coupled historical climate simulations; 4000-year pre-industrial control simulation output.

**Climate Relevance:**
Earth's feedback parameter directly determines climate sensitivity—the temperature increase per unit of radiative forcing. Accurate feedback estimation is essential for constraining climate projections and understanding how ocean patterns influence global warming response.

**Implications:**
Prescribed-SST experiments cannot reliably infer feedback changes over the observational period (~150 years). Researchers should use fully-coupled models or alternative methods when studying temporal feedback evolution; caution is warranted when interpreting published feedback trends from prescribed-SST studies.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to specific model configurations; results may vary across different atmospheric models or SST datasets. The ~100-year noise threshold may be model-dependent.

**TL;DR:**
Prescribed-SST simulations cannot reliably detect climate feedback changes because statistical noise and missing atmospheric forcing dominate signals shorter than ~100 years.

**Method:**
Generative deep learning framework that assimilates multi-source satellite observations to reconstruct gap-free, kilometer-scale surface currents with physically plausible dynamics learned from high-resolution simulations. Multi-source satellite observations (specific sensors not detailed in abstract); validation/training against high-resolution ocean circulation simulations; application domain: Agulhas Current system.

**Method:**

- SHAP diagnostics to identify failure modes: spatial fragmentation, temporal directional reversals, and mechanism saturation
- Pre-registered cross-regional validation protocol to test applicability boundaries

**Data:**
IMERG V07 satellite precipitation, SRTM DEM (terrain), ERA5 reanalysis (total column water vapor, u/v wind components); study regions: Hunan, Guangxi, Guangdong (China)

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**
