### [Gray Swan Factory: Making Extreme Events from Ordinary Cyclones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00348v1)

**Authors:** Gregory J. Hakim, Aishwarya Agrawal
**Published:** 2026-04-01
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, nlin.CD
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Gray Swan Factory: Making Extreme Events from Ordinary Cyclones

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a gradient descent optimization method using differentiable weather prediction models to generate "gray swans"—plausible but unobserved extreme weather events. Demonstrates the approach by transforming hurricane Fiona (2022) into a Sandy-like scenario with intensified landfall potential.

**Method:**
• Gradient descent optimization on a differentiable weather model loss function
• Minimizes perturbations to reanalysis initial conditions to produce target extreme outcomes
• Constrains perturbation magnitude to maintain physical plausibility
• Identifies critical atmospheric interactions (upper-level trough-hurricane merger) driving extreme scenarios

**Data:**
Atmospheric reanalysis data for hurricane cases (Fiona 2022 and four other Atlantic hurricanes); comparison with historical hurricane Sandy (2012) observations.

**Climate Relevance:**
Extends understanding of extreme cyclone hazards beyond the limited observational record (~50 years), critical for assessing climate-driven changes in hurricane intensity and track behavior under future conditions.

**Implications:**
Applicable to emergency preparedness, infrastructure design standards, insurance risk assessment, and dynamical understanding of hurricane intensification mechanisms. Provides synthetic training data for machine learning applications in extreme weather prediction.

**Limitations:**
• Hurricane core not resolved by optimization model; impact on solution validity unknown
• Method produces plausible scenarios but does not quantify probability of occurrence
• Lacks probabilistic framework for hazard assessment

**TL;DR:**
Uses differentiable weather models with gradient descent to generate physically plausible extreme hurricane scenarios from historical cases, useful for hazard planning despite unquantified probabilities.

# ACE2-NEMO Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
First multi-decadal coupled climate simulations (70 years) integrating a machine-learned atmospheric emulator (ACE2) with a full-depth dynamical ocean model (NEMO). This represents a novel test of ML atmospheric models' viability in coupled Earth-system frameworks beyond weather timescales.

**Method:**
Hybrid dynamical-ML approach coupling ACE2 (trained ML emulator of atmospheric physics) to NEMO ocean model with interactive air-sea exchange. Simulations run for 1950-2020 historical forcing and fixed-1950s control scenarios, with comparisons to EC-Earth3P full GCM outputs.

**Data:**
70-year coupled simulation outputs (1950-2020); EC-Earth3P reference simulations; focus on tropical Pacific variability metrics and CO₂ forcing responses.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in understanding fast atmospheric-slow ocean coupling and climate sensitivity—essential for projecting decadal-to-centennial climate responses. Tests whether ML models can capture realistic air-sea feedback mechanisms governing El Niño and climate forcing responses.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates potential for computationally efficient ML-based atmospheric components in next-generation Earth system models, though current implementation requires refinement for operational climate projection use.

**Limitations:**
Weak tropical Pacific atmospheric feedback produces unrealistic El Niño amplitude; reduced shortwave radiation in ACE2 causes CO₂ response divergence from full GCM; emergent behavior in coupled systems remains partially unexplained.

**TL;DR:**
ML atmospheric emulators show promise in coupled climate models but exhibit deficiencies in tropical feedback and radiative processes requiring further development.

# Research Summary: Land Surface Temperature Influences on Sea Surface Temperature Patterns

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that regional land surface temperature (LST) changes drive remote sea surface temperature (SST) pattern shifts through atmospheric teleconnections, particularly affecting tropical Pacific and Atlantic circulation. Identifies LST as a potential contributor to historical model-simulated SST biases.

**Method:**
• LST nudging experiments in a coupled ocean-land-atmosphere model
• Idealized time-dependent ramp warming simulations
• Stationary Rossby wave analysis to diagnose dynamical pathways
• Historical simulations constrained by observed LST

**Data:**
Coupled climate model output; observational LST constraints for historical validation (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
SST patterns fundamentally control global precipitation, monsoons, and El Niño/La Niña dynamics. Understanding LST-SST coupling is critical for accurate climate projections and attribution of historical climate variability, particularly in regions with large land-ocean interactions.

**Implications:**
• LST uncertainty may explain systematic SST biases in climate models
• Regional land warming (South America, North America, Central Africa) has predictable ocean consequences
• Improved LST representation could enhance seasonal-to-decadal climate prediction skill
• Maritime Continent and Tibetan Plateau show weak teleconnections, suggesting regional specificity

**Limitations:**
Single model study; mechanism validation across multiple models unclear; historical implications remain "tentative" pending further investigation.

**TL;DR:**
Regional land warming triggers atmospheric waves that reshape tropical ocean temperature patterns, with implications for climate model biases and predictability.

### [Calibrated Conformal Prediction Intervals for Microphysical Process Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27699v1)

**Authors:** Miriam Simm, Corinna Hoose, Tom Beucler
**Published:** 2026-03-29
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Calibrated Conformal Prediction for Microphysical Processes

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that conformal prediction methods can provide statistically valid, well-calibrated uncertainty quantification for ML emulators of atmospheric microphysical processes without model retraining. Shows conformalized quantile regression outperforms split conformal prediction for climate variables spanning multiple orders of magnitude.

**Method:**
- Compares two conformal approaches: split conformal prediction (for deterministic emulators) and conformalized quantile regression (for quantile regression emulators)
- Both methods generate prediction intervals with guaranteed statistical validity through distribution-free calibration
- Minimal computational overhead; no modification to underlying ML models required

**Data:**
ML emulators trained on output from ICON (ICOsahedral Nonhydrostatic) model in limited-area numerical weather prediction configuration; six microphysical process rates evaluated.

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates govern cloud formation, precipitation, and aerosol-cloud interactions—critical processes for accurate weather prediction and climate modeling. Uncertainty quantification improves reliability of ML-based climate simulations.

**Implications:**
Enables trustworthy deployment of ML emulators in operational weather/climate models by providing rigorous uncertainty estimates; reduces need for expensive ensemble methods while maintaining statistical guarantees.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to ICON model outputs; generalization to other climate models or observational data not addressed; computational cost comparison with ensemble methods not detailed.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal prediction provides statistically valid uncertainty intervals for climate ML emulators with minimal computational cost, with conformalized quantile regression preferred for multi-scale climate variables.

# Research Summary: Omega-blocks and Compound European Extremes

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that upstream warm conveyor belt (WCB) activity 5+ days ahead can distinguish omega-block scenarios producing severe compound heat-flood extremes over Europe from weaker cases, even when large-scale patterns appear initially similar.

**Method:**
- Ensemble boosting to generate hundreds of physically plausible simulations of omega-prone atmospheric configurations
- Lagrangian air parcel tracking to trace dynamical evolution from remote WCB regions to European impacts
- Fully coupled climate model simulations with storyline analysis comparing high-impact versus low-impact event trajectories

**Data:**
Fully coupled climate model simulations (specific model not named in abstract); analysis focuses on September 2023 case study (France heatwave + Iberian/Greek floods) as motivating example.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses the critical challenge of predicting spatially compounding extremes—simultaneous heat and precipitation disasters across multiple regions—which pose severe societal risks and are expected to intensify under climate change.

**Implications:**
- Enables earlier detection (5+ days) of compound extreme risk by monitoring upstream WCB precursors
- Improves subseasonal-to-seasonal forecasting of multi-region disasters
- Provides mechanistic understanding for climate model development and extreme event attribution

**Limitations:**
Single case study presented; generalizability to other omega-block configurations and seasons unclear; computational cost of ensemble boosting approach not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Remote upstream atmospheric moisture dynamics measurably influence European compound extremes days in advance, detectable via ensemble modeling and Lagrangian tracking.
