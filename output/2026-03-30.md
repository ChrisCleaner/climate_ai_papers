### [Impact of Data-Driven Eddy Parameterization on Climate State in an Idealized Coupled CESM Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25843v1)

**Authors:** Jia-Rui Shi, Pavel Perezhogin, Laure Zanna...
**Published:** 2026-03-26
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Data-Driven Eddy Parameterization in CESM

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that a machine learning-based eddy parameterization (Zanna & Bolton 2020) significantly alters coupled climate dynamics when integrated into a realistic ocean-atmosphere model, producing hemispheric asymmetries and large-scale circulation changes not previously quantified in coupled systems.

**Method:**
Implements a data-driven eddy momentum parameterization featuring upgradient (backscatter) momentum flux within MOM6 ocean model coupled to CESM atmosphere; uses regional heat-budget analysis to decompose temperature responses into dynamical and thermodynamic contributions.

**Data:**
Idealized fully-coupled CESM configuration with eddy-permitting ocean resolution; parameterization trained on principles from Zanna & Bolton (2020) but applied here to coupled system context.

**Climate Relevance:**
Mesoscale eddies drive ~50% of ocean heat transport but remain unresolved in coarse-resolution climate models; accurate parameterization is critical for projecting ocean circulation, heat redistribution, and polar climate sensitivity.

**Implications:**
Results provide a mechanistic baseline for understanding how eddy parameterizations propagate through coupled systems; suggests data-driven approaches can improve climate model fidelity and may refine projections of Southern Ocean heat transport and high-latitude warming.

**Limitations:**
Idealized model configuration limits direct applicability to comprehensive climate models; hemispheric asymmetry results are specific to Southern Hemisphere geometry and may not generalize to Northern Hemisphere or realistic continental configurations.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning-based eddy parameterization energizes ocean eddies and strengthens poleward heat transport, triggering compensatory atmospheric responses that reshape mid-latitude climate patterns in coupled models.
