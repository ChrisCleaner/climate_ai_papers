# HaorFloodAlert: Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
A deseasonalized ML ensemble for 72-hour flood forecasting in Bangladesh's Sunamganj Haor wetlands that addresses backwater dynamics missed by conventional riverine flood models. The system integrates Sentinel-1 SAR upstream monitoring with a calibrated rice damage estimator for operational deployment.

**Method:**
- Weighted ensemble: Random Forest (0.5625) + XGBoost (0.4375)
- Deseasonalization: Removed temperature as spurious seasonal predictor (inflating accuracy by 6.9 percentage points)
- Upstream SAR proxy: Otsu-thresholded change detection on Barak River (Silchar, Assam) for 36-hour lead time

**Data:**
Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, meteorological inputs (temperature removed post-hoc), hydrological observations from Sunamganj Haor (~8,000 km²) and upstream Barak River.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses flash flood vulnerability in monsoon-driven wetland ecosystems where climate variability drives rapid inundation of agricultural zones, directly threatening food security (boro rice harvest) in climate-vulnerable regions.

**Implications:**
Operational 72-hour early warning system for ~8,000 km² basin; enables rice damage quantification for insurance/adaptation planning; transferable framework for other flat, backwater-prone wetlands in South Asia.

**Limitations:**
Limited to 77 events; deseasonalization approach may reduce predictive power during atypical seasonal conditions; SAR-based upstream proxy depends on cloud-free acquisition cadence.

**TL;DR:**
ML ensemble with SAR-based upstream monitoring forecasts haor floods 72 hours ahead by removing seasonal confounders and capturing backwater dynamics riverine models miss.

# Research Summary: TC Inner-Core Vector Winds from CYGNSS

**Main Contribution:**
Reconstructs global 1.5 km-resolution tropical cyclone (TC) inner-core vector wind fields from sparse CYGNSS scalar satellite observations alone, filling a critical gap in direct TC wind measurements across all six active ocean basins.

**Method:**
• Extends score-based diffusion assimilation to handle nonlinear observation operators
• Incorporates three TC boundary-layer physics constraints to infer wind direction from scalar speeds
• Introduces Observation Coverage Sufficiency (OCS) criterion for self-validation without external references

**Data:**
• CYGNSS satellite scalar wind speeds (2020–2022)
• 4,955 snapshots from 249 TCs across six basins
• Validation: IBTrACS best-track, ERA5, CCMP, Tail Doppler Radar (47 storms), dropsonde observations

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical observational gap in TC intensity forecasting and storm-surge prediction—essential inputs for climate adaptation and disaster risk reduction in tropical regions lacking routine aircraft reconnaissance.

**Implications:**
• Reduces maximum wind speed (Vmax) bias by ~75–79% versus operational reanalysis
• Enables practical multi-sensor fusion (CYGNSS + dropsondes, SFMR, SAR, scatterometers)
• Provides globally consistent TC monitoring for all basins, including data-sparse regions

**Limitations:**
Wind speed RMSE of 6.9 m/s on coverage-sufficient cases; reconstruction reliability depends on CYGNSS observation density; validation limited to 47 storms with radar data.

**TL;DR:**
AI-enhanced diffusion assimilation recovers vector winds inside tropical cyclones from satellite scalar observations, enabling global 1.5 km-resolution TC monitoring without aircraft.
