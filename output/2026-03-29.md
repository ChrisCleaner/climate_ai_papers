**TL;DR:**

# Research Summary: Wave Dynamics and Low Cloud Response to Warm Pool Warming

**Main Contribution:**
Challenges the prevailing Convective Quasi-Equilibrium Weak Temperature Gradient (QE-WTG) explanation of the Pattern Effect by demonstrating that Rossby wave dynamics—not thermodynamic adjustment—dominate low cloud responses in the subtropical East Pacific. Reconciles this finding with the observed negative global radiative response by showing the deep tropics, not subtropical clouds, drives the net effect.

**Method:**
High-resolution simulations using the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM) combined with reanalysis of historical observational results to isolate wave-driven versus thermodynamic mechanisms controlling cloud cover anomalies in response to Pacific warm pool warming.

**Data:**
E3SM model output; historical observational datasets (specific sources not detailed in abstract but implied to include satellite cloud observations and reanalysis products).

**Climate Relevance:**
The Pattern Effect is critical for constraining climate sensitivity estimates and projecting future warming. Accurate attribution of cloud feedbacks to physical mechanisms (waves vs. thermodynamics) improves climate model predictions and reduces uncertainty in equilibrium climate sensitivity calculations.

**Implications:**
Suggests parameterizations of low cloud feedbacks in climate models may need revision to properly account for wave-driven dynamics, potentially improving radiative forcing estimates and regional climate projections for subtropical regions.

**Limitations:**
Mechanism attribution relies on model simulations; observational validation of Rossby wave dominance in real-world cloud responses may be challenging given limited direct wave-cloud observations.

**TL;DR:**
Rossby waves, not thermodynamic equilibrium, drive subtropical low cloud changes during warm pool warming, but deep tropical clouds dominate the net negative radiative feedback.
