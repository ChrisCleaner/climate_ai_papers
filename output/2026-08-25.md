### [Tracing the Unlabeled Storm: Cross-Variable Transfer in a Lagrangian Atmospheric JEPA Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22358v1)

**Authors:** K M Anirudh, S Sandeep, Hariprasad Kodamana
**Published:** 2026-08-23
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.geo-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Cross-Variable Transfer in Lagrangian Atmospheric JEPA

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces M-JEPA, a self-supervised deep learning framework that learns atmospheric convection dynamics from continuous proxy variables (OLR, wind, humidity) rather than sparse precipitation data, then transfers this representation to improve monsoon rainfall forecasting without ever observing rainfall during pretraining.

**Method:**
- Architecture: Multiscale Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) operating on Lagrangian patches tracking convective systems
- Pretraining: Learns latent world models from five continuous atmospheric fields via self-supervised prediction
- Transfer: Frozen backbone + shared decoder with parallel probabilistic/deterministic branches for precipitation forecasting
- Validation: Rigorous ablation controls (rainfall-only baseline, random initialization) isolate proxy pretraining contribution

**Data:**
South Asian monsoon region atmospheric fields (OLR, wind components, humidity); daily precipitation observations; comparison against 51-member ECMWF operational ensemble forecasts.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses monsoon predictability—a critical driver of South Asian hydroclimate and socioeconomic impacts. Leverages physical coherence of convective organization (expressed in OLR) over noisy precipitation to capture intraseasonal dynamics governing monsoon variability.

**Implications:**
Achieves competitive ensemble-level skill (CRPS: 6.81 mm/day) on consumer GPU hardware; particularly strong at heavy-rain thresholds and fine scales. Demonstrates feasibility of physics-informed transfer learning for operational seasonal precipitation forecasting in data-sparse regimes.

**Limitations:**
Neighborhood skill and point-scale deterministic metrics favor operational ensemble; single-region evaluation (South Asia); computational cost of pretraining not detailed.

**TL;DR:**
Self-supervised pretraining on atmospheric proxies outperforms direct rainfall supervision for monsoon precipitation forecasting, achieving near-operational skill with minimal compute.

# Research Summary: Scalable Bayesian Framework for Sea-Level Inference

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a computationally scalable Bayesian framework that accurately quantifies uncertainties in modern sea-level change by embedding complete sea-level physics into inverse modeling, addressing systematic biases in conventional satellite-based methods.

**Method:**
- Infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problem formulation (avoiding discretization artifacts)
- Adjoint sea-level theory for physics-based forward/inverse modeling
- Matrix-free iterative solvers with randomized low-rank decompositions
- Implemented in open-source libraries (pygeoinf, pyslfp) enabling laptop-scale computation with trivial parallelization

**Data:**
Satellite gravity observations and ocean altimetry data; demonstrated on joint inversions decomposing regional sea-level change.

**Climate Relevance:**
Sea-level rise is a critical climate impact indicator; accurate uncertainty quantification is essential for coastal risk assessment and climate model validation. Proper treatment of steric (thermal expansion) vs. manometric (mass redistribution) components constrains ice sheet and ocean dynamics.

**Implications:**
Enables rigorous uncertainty propagation in sea-level budgets at observational resolution; supports improved coastal adaptation planning and climate attribution studies by quantifying previously underestimated error margins.

**Limitations:**
Synthetic experiments presented; real-data application shown but full validation against independent observations not detailed. Remaining degeneracies in component separation acknowledged but not fully resolved.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed Bayesian inversion with proper uncertainty quantification transforms satellite sea-level analysis from biased estimates to rigorous probabilistic inference at scale.

# Basin-Scale CO₂ Storage Modeling with AI-Assisted Uncertainty Quantification

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a unified basin-scale model for multiple CO₂ storage projects in North Dakota that quantifies inter-project interference effects and uncertainty, addressing a critical gap in deterministic or scenario-based existing studies.

**Method:**
- Multilevel grid refinement (4-level nested local grids) via constraint-satisfaction optimization
- Power-law averaging for permeability upscaling in coarse regions
- Deep learning surrogate model for rapid uncertainty quantification and global sensitivity analysis
- Comparison of unified vs. standalone project simulations

**Data:**
Publicly available geological data and project specifications for the Broom Creek Formation (North Dakota); 44×10⁶ cell fine-scale model; 5 projects with 18 injection wells.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly supports carbon capture and storage (CCS) deployment by quantifying how neighboring injection operations affect CO₂ containment efficiency and plume migration—critical for scaling CCS as a climate mitigation strategy.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates substantial inter-project interference (e.g., 29% reduction in CO₂ injection for one project in unified model), indicating that basin-scale assessments are essential for accurate permitting and operational planning of regional storage networks.

**Limitations:**
Computational cost remains high (2+ days for fine model); surrogate model accuracy and generalization to other geological formations not explicitly discussed; limited to single basin case study.

**TL;DR:**
AI-accelerated basin-scale modeling reveals that neighboring CO₂ storage projects significantly interfere with each other, requiring unified rather than standalone assessments for climate-critical CCS operations.
