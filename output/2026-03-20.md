# Research Summary: Transition Path Theory for Hurricane Rapid Intensification

**Main Contribution:**
Applies transition path theory (TPT), a stochastic methodology, to identify predictive ocean conditions for hurricane rapid intensification (RI) in the tropical Atlantic. Reveals that thermocline depth is a stronger RI predictor than barrier layer thickness, with translational speed as a critical modulating factor.

**Method:**
Constructs Markov chains from discretized time series of hurricane intensity, intensity changes, translational speed, and upper ocean properties (barrier layer thickness, thermocline depth). Uses TPT statistics to compute first-passage time distributions from source states (e.g., specific barrier layer ranges) to RI target states, identifying minimal-detour transition pathways that reveal causal relationships missed by conventional statistics.

**Data:**
Hurricane and ocean reanalysis datasets covering the eastern Caribbean Sea and western tropical North Atlantic, with emphasis on barrier layer and thermocline measurements influenced by river runoff.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses rapid intensification—a critical climate-driven hazard increasingly observed in warming oceans. Links upper ocean stratification (barrier layers, thermoclines) to RI probability, connecting freshwater input from terrestrial sources to tropical cyclone intensification dynamics.

**Implications:**
Enables improved RI forecasting by identifying ocean preconditions; supports operational hurricane prediction systems. Highlights that faster-moving, stronger systems face elevated RI risk, informing risk assessment and coastal preparedness strategies.

**Limitations:**
Regional focus (eastern Caribbean/western Atlantic) may limit generalizability; relies on reanalysis data quality; memoryless Markov assumption may oversimplify atmospheric-oceanic memory effects.

**TL;DR:**
TPT reveals thermocline depth and translational speed as key predictors of hurricane rapid intensification, outperforming traditional statistical approaches.
