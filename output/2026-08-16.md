### [Paleoclimate Boundary Conditions as an Out-of-Sample Test for the Forced Response of Ocean Climate Emulators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13494v1)

**Authors:** Adam Subel, Laure Zanna
**Published:** 2026-08-13
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Paleoclimate Boundary Conditions as Out-of-Sample Tests for Ocean Climate Emulators

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that autoregressive neural network emulators trained on short timescales can generalize to novel orbital forcings in paleoclimate settings, but reveals critical failures in reproducing slow ocean interior dynamics and internal variability—establishing the mid-Holocene as a diagnostic testbed for climate emulator validation.

**Method:**
Autoregressive, full-depth ocean emulators trained on short time steps; tested against mid-Holocene climate model output (in-distribution, out-of-sample forcing). Includes baseline models that directly infer ocean state from boundary forcings and linear superposition analysis of individual forcing component responses.

**Data:**
Mid-Holocene experiment from a numerical climate model; orbital forcing variations provide controlled, ground-truthed paleoclimate conditions distinct from training climate.

**Climate Relevance:**
Long-term climate projections require emulators that faithfully reproduce forced ocean responses over centuries—a critical gap between weather prediction (short-term) and climate modeling (long-term). Paleoclimate provides an accessible test of forced-response fidelity without requiring future climate simulations.

**Implications:**
Identifies that convergence on training metrics (mean state) does not guarantee capture of dynamical processes needed for accurate forced responses. Suggests paleoclimate experiments are valuable for diagnosing emulator failures before deployment to out-of-distribution future climates.

**Limitations:**
Emulators underestimate response amplitude; fail to capture slow interior ocean evolution; testing limited to single paleoclimate scenario (mid-Holocene).

**TL;DR:**
Neural network ocean emulators generalize to novel paleoclimate forcings but miss slow dynamics, revealing that training-set performance metrics alone cannot validate climate projection skill.

### [Statistical Noise and Missing Forcing Limit Estimates of Earth's Feedback from Prescribed Sea-Surface Temperature Simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13219v1)

**Published:** 2026-08-13
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph

# Research Summary: SST-Prescribed Simulations and Earth's Climate Feedback

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that the standard prescribed sea-surface temperature (SST) method for estimating Earth's climate feedback parameter is fundamentally limited by statistical noise and missing atmospheric forcing. Challenges the validity of inferring temporal changes in feedback from observational SST patterns over the instrumental record.

**Method:**
Controlled experiments comparing prescribed-SST atmospheric simulations against fully-coupled historical simulations; moving-window regression analysis to detect feedback parameter trends; 4000-year pre-industrial control simulation to establish noise baselines and distinguish signal from statistical artifacts.

**Data:**
Historical coupled climate model simulations with prescribed SSTs; pre-industrial control run; observed SST evolution; atmospheric forcing fields.

**Climate Relevance:**
Earth's feedback parameter directly determines climate sensitivity—the warming response to doubled CO₂. Accurate feedback estimation is essential for constraining climate projections and understanding how ocean surface patterns modulate global temperature response to forcing.

**Implications:**
Prescribed-SST experiments cannot reliably detect feedback changes on timescales <~100 years, limiting their use for attributing observed climate sensitivity variations to SST pattern evolution. Researchers should rely on fully-coupled models or alternative methods for feedback attribution studies.

**Limitations:**
Analysis restricted to model-based SSTs; unclear how results generalize to observational SST datasets with different quality/coverage; pre-industrial control may not capture all relevant feedback mechanisms active during forced climate change.

**TL;DR:**
Statistical noise and missing forcing make prescribed-SST simulations unreliable for detecting short-term feedback changes, undermining their use in climate sensitivity attribution.

# Research Summary: AI Forecast Ensembles and Conditional Distributions

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that diffusion model-based ensemble forecasts achieve good marginal skill at individual locations but fail to capture correct joint spatial structure, revealing a fundamental limitation in how AI models sample conditional distributions for weather/climate prediction.

**Method:**
• Diffusion model trained for probabilistic subseasonal forecasting
• Shuffle-based permutation decomposition to isolate failure modes
• Comparison across metrics: energy score (insensitive) vs. variogram score (sensitive to spatial structure)
• Lorenz-96 synthetic experiments (0.7–170 equivalent years) to test generalization

**Data:**
• Eight US East Coast tide gauge stations (sea level from reanalysis)
• Subseasonal lead times
• Synthetic Lorenz-96 system for controlled experiments

**Climate Relevance:**
Coastal sea level forecasting is critical for hazard preparedness and adaptation planning; accurate joint spatial predictions are essential for regional risk assessment and infrastructure planning.

**Implications:**
Current AI ensemble methods may provide misleading confidence in forecast skill when evaluated only marginally. Users must employ spatial correlation metrics (variogram score) alongside traditional scores to detect distribution misspecification in operational forecasts.

**Limitations:**
Study limited to two geographic domains (real: US East Coast; synthetic: Lorenz-96); unclear whether findings generalize to other variables, regions, or diffusion model architectures.

**TL;DR:**
AI ensemble forecasts can be marginally skillful while sampling spatially incorrect conditional distributions—a failure invisible to standard metrics.

**Method:**
- Stylized coalition-formation game with two market-based membership channels (membership premium and outsider drain)
- Robust control theory to assess stability against institutional perturbations (defection, renegotiation, political turnover)
- Analytical separation of free-rider incentives from architecture-specific mechanisms, enabling comparative analysis
- Bistability analysis identifying critical mass thresholds between equilibria

**Data:**
No empirical datasets used; theoretical model with economic primitives (carbon price differentials, export emission intensity) as parameters.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses the design of international climate agreements (Paris Agreement-type coalitions) by quantifying how institutional structures affect participation and durability—critical for achieving global emission reduction targets.
