# Research Summary: MELO for Non-Stationary Climate Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes MELO, a model-agnostic online aggregation method that adapts to distribution shifts by hedging across multiple memory horizons without requiring prior knowledge of regime-change timing or external indicators.

**Method:**
- Wraps base predictors with exponentially weighted least-squares (EWLS) experts at varying forgetting factors
- Aggregates raw and adapted forecasts using MLpol, a parameter-free online aggregation rule
- Provides deterministic oracle inequalities guaranteeing competitive performance against best raw predictor and best time-varying combinations, with sublinear overhead

**Data:**
French national electricity-load forecasting during COVID-19 lockdown (March–May 2020); no external regime indicators or policy covariates used.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to climate-sensitive energy demand forecasting under abrupt behavioral shifts. Addresses the challenge of predicting critical infrastructure loads when distribution patterns change due to policy interventions or climate events without requiring explicit regime labels.

**Implications:**
Enables robust real-time forecasting for grid operators and energy planners during crises or policy transitions; reduces need for manual model retuning and external covariate engineering. Lightweight computational footprint suits operational deployment.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to single domain (electricity load); boundedness assumptions may not hold for all climate variables; no comparison with recent deep learning baselines for non-stationary prediction.

**TL;DR:**
MELO automatically hedges across adaptation timescales to forecast energy demand through distribution shifts without knowing when regimes change, achieving 34.7% RMSE improvement during COVID-19 lockdown.
