### [Scalable Uncertainty Quantification for Extreme Weather Forecasting via Empirical Neural Tangent Kernels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02886v2)

**Authors:** Jose Marie Antonio Miñoza, Rex Gregor Laylo, Sebastian C. Ibañez
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CE
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: NTK-Based Uncertainty Quantification for Extreme Weather Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes Neural Tangent Kernel-based uncertainty quantification (NTK-UQ) for deep learning weather models that produces calibrated prediction intervals without retraining. Identifies architecture-dependent failure modes and proposes a data-driven selection rule between ICA and SVD decomposition for robust uncertainty estimation.

**Method:**
- Uses last-layer empirical features with NTK theory to derive GP-based uncertainty estimates
- Analyzes variance collapse mechanism: aggressive eigenvalue truncation in spectral operators vs. full-rank tolerance in attention models
- Applies Independent Component Analysis (exploiting kurtosis/negentropy) vs. SVD (second-order variance) based on feature eigenspectrum concentration ratio
- Inference requires single matrix-vector product per sample (no retraining)

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; evaluation on four neural network architectures suggests multi-model comparison but specific datasets unnamed.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses critical gap in operational extreme weather forecasting—deep learning models match numerical weather prediction speed/accuracy but lack uncertainty estimates essential for tropical cyclone prediction and high-stakes decision-making.

**Implications:**
31–37% sharper prediction intervals than conformal prediction baseline at 90% coverage; uniquely produces adaptive intervals scaling with event severity, enabling risk-proportional warnings for extreme weather.

**Limitations:**
Dataset details absent; unclear generalization beyond four tested architectures; heavy-tailed assumption may not hold for all weather phenomena.

**TL;DR:**
Architecture-aware uncertainty quantification for neural weather models achieves sharper, adaptive prediction intervals via selective ICA/SVD decomposition without retraining.

### [Probabilistic storyline attribution using machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02550v1)

**Authors:** Frieder Loer, Maybritt Schillinger, Sebastian Sippel
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** stat.AP, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Probabilistic Storyline Attribution Using Machine Learning

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces distributional autoencoders (DAEs) to generate climate counterfactuals for storyline attribution, enabling estimation of how climate change modifies extreme weather events while preserving atmospheric circulation patterns. This overcomes the limitation that physical climate models cannot directly transfer storyline counterfactuals across different climate forcing states.

**Method:**
• Distributional autoencoders model full conditional distributions of temperature fields given atmospheric circulation state and global warming level
• Derives conditional probability ratios from learned distributions
• Trained on coupled climate model simulations; validated against factual and counterfactual model outputs

**Data:**
Fully coupled climate model simulations; ERA5 reanalysis for observed circulation patterns (2003 European heatwave case study).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses climate attribution—quantifying the anthropogenic contribution to observed extreme events. The circulation-conditional approach isolates forced climate change effects from natural variability, critical for understanding event intensification under warming.

**Implications:**
Enables probabilistic projections of how specific extreme weather patterns intensify under future warming scenarios. Demonstrated application: 2003-like European heatwaves would increase ~1°C by 2028 and ~3°C by 2053, with probability ratios doubling to tripling relative to 2003.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to illustrative case study; generalizability to other extreme event types and regions not demonstrated. Dependence on climate model quality for training data.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning enables probabilistic climate counterfactuals for extreme events by conditioning on atmospheric circulation, quantifying how climate change intensifies specific weather patterns.

### [Multiscale Dynamics of Heatwave Persistence and Intensity Under Climate Change](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02005v1)

**Authors:** Md Tasim Ferdous, Reda Snaiki, Abdelatif Merabtine
**Published:** 2026-06-01
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Multiscale Dynamics of Heatwave Persistence Under Climate Change

**Main Contribution:**
Develops an integrated workflow linking heatwave event characteristics to underlying atmospheric dynamical modes using multiresolution dynamic mode decomposition (mrDMD). Demonstrates that regional heatwave amplification under climate change is driven by persistence-dominated regimes rather than frequency increases alone, particularly in continental interiors.

**Method:**
- Heatwave identification: Fixed 90th percentile threshold (2001-2010 baseline) with 15-day moving window and minimum-duration criteria
- Dynamical analysis: mrDMD applied to daily mean temperature fields to extract coherent multiscale structures
- Connection: Heatwave-conditioned mode participation ratios and spatial alignment analyses (hotspot overlap, Spearman rank correlation) between mode-energy footprints and gridded heatwave metrics

**Data:**
CORDEX-NAM12 regional climate simulations (CRCM5 downscaling of CanESM5) over southern Canada; three future periods under SSP5-8.5 (2016-2025, 2051-2060, 2091-2100); reference period 2001-2010.

**Climate Relevance:**
Explains mechanisms driving extreme heat persistence—a critical gap in understanding compound climate risks. Shows that late-century heatwaves will be longer (~26.66 days/event, 69 seasonal days) and more intense (~6.88 K above threshold), with weaker atmospheric damping in interior regions.

**Implications:**
Enables process-informed regional climate projections for infrastructure planning and public health adaptation. Identifies continental interiors as higher-risk zones for prolonged heat stress, informing targeted resilience strategies.

**Limitations:**
Single downscaling model (CRCM5) and regional domain (southern Canada); results may not generalize globally. Fixed threshold approach may not capture changing baseline conditions in early periods.

**TL;DR:**
Multiscale dynamical analysis reveals that future heatwaves will intensify through persistence mechanisms (longer duration, weaker damping) rather than frequency increases, with continental regions at highest risk.

### [Flow Matching for Convective-Scale Precipitation Downscaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00281v1)

**Authors:** Tom Wetherell
**Published:** 2026-05-29
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Flow Matching for Convective-Scale Precipitation Downscaling

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that flow matching, a generative ML framework, outperforms diffusion models (CPMGEM) for downscaling precipitation from 8 km to 2 km resolution. Shows superior spatial skill across multiple precipitation thresholds and neighborhood scales.

**Method:**
Flow matching—a generative framework that learns optimal transport paths between data distributions—trained to map coarse-resolution precipitation fields to fine-resolution outputs. Benchmarked against score-based diffusion models using fractions skill score (FSS) and SAL (Structure, Amplitude, Location) metrics.

**Data:**
Daily precipitation fields over a convective-scale domain centered on Singapore; downscaling from 8 km to 2 km horizontal resolution.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical need for high-resolution precipitation projections in tropical regions where convective processes dominate. Complements dynamical downscaling approaches for climate impact assessments requiring fine-scale precipitation detail.

**Implications:**
Flow matching offers computationally efficient alternative to diffusion models for operational precipitation downscaling, with particular strength in reproducing spatial structures critical for flood risk and water resource applications.

**Limitations:**
Systematic dry bias in upper precipitation tail (underestimation of extreme events); climatological mean underestimation despite superior spatial skill metrics.

**TL;DR:**
Flow matching achieves better spatial precipitation structure than diffusion models in convective-scale downscaling but struggles with extreme precipitation representation.

# Conformal Risk Sharing: Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a distribution-free mechanism for fairly allocating financial costs of rare adverse events among group participants while guaranteeing no individual is materially worse off. Combines interpretable risk-sharing policies with conformal calibration to provide certified, trustworthy obligation caps without assuming known probability distributions.

**Method:**
• Pairs a tunable sharing intensity policy (trained on data) with split conformal calibration (validated on held-out data)

**Data:**
Synthetic datasets plus real-world applications including precipitation records and energy-cooperative data.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**
Conformal calibration enables fair, certified cost-sharing for rare climate disasters without distributional assumptions, with guaranteed individual obligation caps.

- Synthetic simulation data (validation against split-window, TES, and hybrid methods)
