# THOR: Foundation Model for Earth Observation

**Main Contribution:**
THOR is a compute-adaptive foundation model that unifies multi-sensor Earth observation data (Sentinel-1, -2, -3) across heterogeneous resolutions (10–1000 m) in a single architecture. It enables flexible inference-time patch sizing without retraining, allowing dynamic computational-accuracy trade-offs.

**Method:**
• Randomized patch and input image size pre-training strategy enabling variable patch sizes at inference
• Single unified architecture processing SAR (Sentinel-1), optical (Sentinel-2), and thermal/infrared (OLCI & SLSTR) data natively
• Eliminates need for model retraining when adjusting computational budgets or spatial resolutions

**Data:**
THOR Pretrain—a new large-scale multi-sensor dataset combining Copernicus Sentinel constellation data at native resolutions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical Earth observation needs for climate monitoring: land-use change detection, ocean dynamics, atmospheric processes, and climate-society interactions through unified multi-modal satellite data integration.

**Implications:**
Enables rapid deployment across resource-constrained environments (edge computing, developing regions) and diverse downstream applications (crop monitoring, disaster response, sea ice tracking) without model retraining or fine-tuning penalties.

**Limitations:**
Paper abstract does not detail computational benchmarks, inference latency comparisons, or performance degradation across patch size variations; generalization to non-Sentinel sensors unclear.

**TL;DR:**
THOR is the first multi-sensor foundation model enabling flexible, compute-adaptive Earth observation without retraining, advancing practical climate monitoring at scale.

# Research Summary: Interdecadal Bipolar Oscillation in Atmospheric Water Vapor

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies the Interdecadal Bipolar Oscillation (IBO), a ~60–80 year internal climate variability mode in atmospheric water vapor that explains asynchronous warming between Arctic and Antarctic regions. The IBO's phase shift in the late 1980s amplified Arctic warming while suppressing Antarctic change, with potential phase reversal implications for future polar climate.

**Method:**
Empirical mode decomposition and spectral analysis of reanalysis data combined with CMIP6 climate model simulations (historical and pre-industrial control runs) to isolate internal variability from forced climate signals and validate the IBO's robustness across models.

**Data:**
Atmospheric reanalysis datasets (8 decades), CMIP6 historical simulations, and pre-industrial control runs from multiple climate models.

**Climate Relevance:**
Explains the observed Arctic amplification and Antarctic climate lag—critical for understanding polar climate dynamics and predicting future polar responses to greenhouse gas forcing. Water vapor is a key radiative forcing agent in polar regions.

**Implications:**
Projected IBO phase reversal could trigger rapid Antarctic warming and accelerated water vapor increases in the Southern Hemisphere, potentially unlocking new climate tipping points and regional climate risks currently underestimated in projections.

**Limitations:**
Model uncertainty in capturing polar water vapor processes; long timescale (60–80 years) limits observational validation; phase reversal timing and magnitude remain uncertain.

**TL;DR:**
An internal 60–80 year oscillation in polar water vapor explains Arctic-Antarctic warming divergence and may reverse soon, potentially accelerating Antarctic climate change.

# Research Summary: Physics-Guided Deep Echo State Networks for ENSO Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a physics-guided Deep Echo State Network (DESN) that achieves skillful El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) predictions 16-20 months in advance by leveraging physically interpretable climate modes from the extended recharge oscillator framework.

**Method:**
Combines reservoir computing (Echo State Networks) with physics constraints derived from the XRO framework. The approach selects physically meaningful climate modes as inputs rather than raw data, enabling the neural network to learn nonlinear dynamics while maintaining interpretability. Includes mechanistic experiments isolating warm water volume and inter-basin coupling effects, plus error-growth analysis to estimate predictability limits.

**Data:**
Not explicitly detailed in abstract; likely uses observational ENSO indices (Niño3.4) and climate reanalysis data consistent with XRO framework variables (sea surface temperature, thermocline depth, wind stress).

**Climate Relevance:**
ENSO is the primary driver of interannual climate variability affecting global weather patterns, agriculture, and water resources. Extended predictability (16-20 months) enables improved seasonal-to-interannual forecasting for climate impacts.

**Implications:**
Provides operational forecasting capability with minimal computational overhead compared to complex climate models. Identifies nonlinear warm water volume–inter-basin coupling as key to extended predictability, advancing mechanistic understanding of ENSO limits.

**Limitations:**
Predictability ceiling (~30 months) suggests fundamental chaotic limits; transferability to other climate phenomena unclear; validation against operational forecast systems not mentioned.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-constrained reservoir computing achieves skillful 16-20 month ENSO forecasts by learning nonlinear interactions between interpretable climate modes.
