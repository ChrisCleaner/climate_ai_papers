# AIFL: Global Streamflow Forecasting with LSTM

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces AIFL, a deterministic LSTM model for global daily streamflow forecasting that bridges the reanalysis-to-operational forecast gap through a novel two-stage training strategy. Achieves competitive performance across 18,588 basins while maintaining operational transparency and reproducibility.

**Method:**
- Two-stage training: pre-training on ERA5-Land reanalysis (1980-2019) followed by fine-tuning on IFS operational forecasts (2016-2019)
- LSTM architecture trained end-to-end within the CARAVAN ecosystem
- Addresses domain shift between historical reanalysis and operational NWP products

**Data:**
- 18,588 basins from CARAVAN dataset
- ERA5-Land reanalysis (40 years: 1980-2019)
- IFS control forecasts (2016-2019)
- Independent test period: 2021-2024

**Climate Relevance:**
Streamflow forecasting is critical for flood preparedness and water resource management under climate variability. The model's ability to handle extreme events directly supports climate adaptation and disaster risk reduction.

**Implications:**
Provides an operationally robust, transparent baseline for global hydrological forecasting; enables improved flood early warning systems and water management decisions at scale; demonstrates feasibility of bridging reanalysis-forecast gaps for other hydrological applications.

**Limitations:**
Deterministic approach (no uncertainty quantification); performance gap between reanalysis and operational forecasts may persist in regions with sparse training data; evaluation limited to 2021-2024 period.

**TL;DR:**
AIFL uses two-stage LSTM training to forecast global streamflow operationally, achieving state-of-the-art performance while maintaining transparency for flood preparedness.

# Research Summary: Fast Radiative Feedbacks via ML Weather Emulators

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that machine-learning weather emulators trained on historical data can accurately simulate fast radiative feedbacks (atmospheric processes operating on weekly timescales) without retraining on future climate conditions. This enables quantification of precipitation responses to CO₂ perturbations with no recent historical precedent.

**Method:**
ML weather emulators trained on historical meteorological reanalyses are used to simulate radiative-convective equilibrium (RCE) under perturbed CO₂ concentrations. Model outputs are validated against full-physics Earth System Models (ESMs) to assess agreement in fast feedback responses.

**Data:**
Historical meteorological reanalyses (training data); Earth System Model simulations (validation benchmarks); top-of-atmosphere radiative balance and sea-surface temperature boundary conditions from historical records.

**Climate Relevance:**
Fast feedbacks (clouds, water vapor, lapse rate effects) operate on timescales already present in instrumental records and directly influence the global hydrological cycle response to greenhouse gas forcing. Understanding these mechanisms is critical for near-term climate projections.

**Implications:**
ML emulators offer computationally efficient alternatives to ESMs for studying fast climate processes; historically trained models can extrapolate beyond observed conditions without expensive retraining, accelerating climate impact assessments.

**Limitations:**
Approach limited to fast feedbacks; slow feedbacks (ice-albedo, vegetation changes) require different treatment. Validation restricted to ESM comparison; real-world observational validation of extreme CO₂ scenarios remains infeasible.

**TL;DR:**
Historically trained ML weather emulators can accurately predict fast radiative feedback responses to novel CO₂ levels, matching full ESM results without retraining.

### [Ensemble-size-dependence of deep-learning post-processing methods that minimize an (un)fair score: motivating examples and a proof-of-concept solution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15830v1)

# Research Summary: Ensemble-Size-Dependent Deep Learning Post-Processing

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies a critical flaw in using fair scores (aCRPS) to train ensemble post-processing methods when structural dependencies between members are introduced. Proposes trajectory transformers as a solution that maintains ensemble-size independence while improving forecast reliability.

**Method:**
- Compares two post-processing approaches: linear member-by-member calibration and transformer self-attention across ensemble dimension
- Demonstrates both violate conditional independence assumptions required by aCRPS
- Introduces trajectory transformers that apply self-attention over lead time (temporal dimension) rather than ensemble dimension, preserving member independence
- Evaluates on ECMWF subseasonal forecasts using aCRPS and reliability metrics

**Data:**
ECMWF subseasonal forecasting system weekly mean 2-meter temperature (T₂m) forecasts; experiments with training ensembles of 3 vs. 9 members and real-time forecasts of 9 vs. 100 members.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses systematic biases and reliability in subseasonal weather forecasts, critical for medium-range climate prediction and early warning systems where ensemble calibration is essential.

**Implications:**
Enables practical deployment of deep-learning post-processing with variable ensemble sizes without retraining, improving operational forecast skill and reliability across different computational constraints.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to single variable (T₂m) and one forecast system; generalization to other variables, regions, or forecast centers not demonstrated.

**TL;DR:**
Trajectory transformers solve the ensemble-size-dependence problem in deep-learning post-processing by applying self-attention temporally rather than across ensemble members, maintaining statistical fairness.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**

# CAAL: Confidence-Aware Active Learning for Heteroscedastic Atmospheric Regression

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes CAAL, an active learning framework that distinguishes epistemic (reducible) from aleatoric (irreducible) uncertainty to efficiently select samples for labeling atmospheric particle properties. Addresses the heteroscedastic regression problem where noise varies with input, preventing wasteful sampling in high-noise regions.

**Method:**
Two-component approach: (1) decoupled uncertainty-aware training that separately optimizes predictive mean and noise level via distinct loss functions to stabilize uncertainty estimates; (2) confidence-aware acquisition function that weights epistemic uncertainty by predicted aleatoric uncertainty, prioritizing informative samples over noisy ones.

**Data:**
Particle-resolved numerical simulations and real atmospheric observations; routine observations include air pollutant concentrations and meteorological conditions as predictors for particle properties (toxicity, hygroscopicity).

**Climate Relevance:**
Particle properties directly impact aerosol-cloud interactions, radiative forcing, and air quality-health linkages—critical for climate modeling and pollution impact assessment. Reduces reliance on expensive simulations or complex measurements.

**Implications:**
Enables cost-effective expansion of atmospheric particle property databases, improving training data availability for climate and air quality models without proportional increases in measurement/simulation costs.

**Limitations:**
Not explicitly stated; potential constraints include applicability to other heteroscedastic domains and computational overhead of uncertainty decomposition not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Active learning framework that separates noise from model uncertainty to efficiently label expensive atmospheric particle properties under input-dependent noise.

**Method:**
Year-long, fully coupled ocean-atmosphere simulation at kilometer-scale resolution with spectral analysis to decompose LHF variance across spatial scales; comparison with ERA5 reanalysis to identify unresolved processes.

**Data:**
Global coupled simulation output; ERA5 reanalysis for validation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Latent heat flux is a critical ocean-atmosphere heat and moisture exchange pathway. The Southern Ocean drives global climate through thermohaline circulation, making accurate representation of air-sea coupling essential for climate projections.

**Implications:**
Current climate models and reanalysis significantly underestimate fine-scale ocean forcing on the atmosphere (~17% of ocean-driven LHF variance unresolved). Improved representation of mesoscale/submesoscale processes could enhance climate model fidelity and regional atmospheric predictions, particularly for Southern Ocean dynamics.

**Limitations:**
Single-year simulation; non-local atmospheric response mechanisms not fully mechanistically explained; applicability to other ocean basins unclear.

**TL;DR:**
