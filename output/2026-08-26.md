**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Weakly Supervised Seafloor Segmentation for Seagrass Mapping

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a weakly supervised deep learning framework that generates pixel-level seafloor habitat maps from image-level labels alone, eliminating the need for expensive manual pixel-level annotation of side-scan sonar (SSS) imagery.

**Method:**
- ViT-based encoder-decoder with classification branch for weakly supervised semantic segmentation
- Class activation maps (CAM) refined via dense conditional random fields (CRF) tuned for acoustic noise
- Iterative self-training with class-imbalance sampling strategy
- Lovász-Softmax loss function for improved segmentation quality
- Self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled SSS data

**Data:**
Side-scan sonar transects of seagrass meadows; specific dataset size/location not detailed in abstract but includes held-out test transect and field validation sites.

**Climate Relevance:**
Seagrass meadows are critical blue-carbon ecosystems. Efficient, large-scale mapping enables accurate coastal carbon inventory and supports climate mitigation monitoring under carbon accounting frameworks.

**Implications:**
Enables coast-wide seagrass monitoring at operational scale; reduces annotation burden from months to hours; applicable to deep/turbid waters inaccessible to optical sensors; supports evidence-based coastal management and climate policy.

**Limitations:**
Generalizability across different sonar systems/frequencies not explicitly addressed; class imbalance handling suggests data skew; acoustic imagery noise remains a challenge despite CRF refinement.

**TL;DR:**
Weakly supervised learning makes side-scan sonar practical for large-scale seagrass mapping, a key blue-carbon habitat monitoring tool.

### [Data Leakage Inflates Generalizability of Power Outage Prediction Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24665v1)

**Authors:** Yamil Essus, Ranga Raju Vatsavai, Benjamin Rachunok
**Published:** 2026-08-25
**Categories:** cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Power Outage Prediction Model Generalization

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that power outage prediction models exhibit inflated performance metrics due to data leakage via spatial and temporal autocorrelation, and that this obscures poor real-world generalizability across novel geographic regions, time periods, and extreme events.

**Method:**
Compares predictive performance across three test selection strategies of increasing realism: random train-test splits, leave-one-state-out (spatial holdout), and leave-one-event-out (event-based holdout). Evaluates multiple feature sets including weather reanalysis, land-cover data, and embeddings from Prithvi WxC (a GeoAI foundation model).

**Data:**
U.S. East Coast power outage records (2018–2023) combined with weather reanalysis and land-cover datasets; GeoAI foundation model embeddings.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses climate adaptation by assessing infrastructure vulnerability to weather-driven outages. Evaluates whether AI models can reliably predict climate-driven power disruptions for risk management and resilience planning.

**Implications:**
Current publicly available outage prediction models have limited operational value for climate risk assessment. Practitioners should not rely on standard train-test splits for deployment decisions; improved data coverage and realistic evaluation protocols are prerequisites for trustworthy climate-infrastructure models.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to U.S. East Coast; foundation model improvements were inconsistent; study does not propose solutions, only identifies the problem.

**TL;DR:**
Standard evaluation practices mask that power outage prediction models fail to generalize across space, time, and events—limiting their utility for climate-driven infrastructure risk assessment.

# Research Summary: Cross-Variable Transfer in Lagrangian Atmospheric JEPA

Introduces M-JEPA, a self-supervised deep learning framework that learns atmospheric convection dynamics from continuous proxy variables (OLR, wind, humidity) rather than precipitation, then transfers this representation to monsoon rainfall forecasting without ever observing rainfall during pretraining.

**Method:**
- Architecture: Multiscale Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) operating on Lagrangian patches tracking convective systems
- Pretraining: Unsupervised learning on five continuous atmospheric fields to build latent world model
- Transfer: Frozen backbone + shared decoder with parallel probabilistic/deterministic branches for precipitation prediction
- Evaluation: Frozen-backbone probing with two controls (rainfall-only baseline, random initialization) to isolate proxy pretraining contribution

**Data:**
South Asian monsoon region; five continuous atmospheric proxy fields (OLR primary); operational ECMWF 51-member ensemble as benchmark; daily precipitation targets.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses fundamental challenge in monsoon prediction: deep convection organization drives South Asian monsoon variability but is poorly captured by zero-inflated, heavy-tailed precipitation data; proxy variables encode convective structure more coherently.

**Implications:**
Achieves competitive operational-scale performance (6.81 mm/day CRPS vs. 6.89 mm/day ECMWF) on single consumer GPU with 15.4M parameters; particularly strong at heavy-rain thresholds and fine spatial scales, enabling resource-efficient monsoon forecasting.

**Limitations:**
Neighborhood skill and deterministic point metrics favor ensemble; advantage concentrated at specific thresholds rather than uniform improvement; single-region evaluation (South Asia).

**TL;DR:**
Self-supervised pretraining on atmospheric proxies outperforms direct rainfall supervision for monsoon precipitation forecasting, demonstrating that latent convection dynamics transfer effectively to precipitation prediction.
