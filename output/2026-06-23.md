### [Optimal scenario design for climate emulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19302v1)

**Authors:** Christopher B. Womack, Shahine Bouabid, Andrei Sokolov...
**Published:** 2026-06-17
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.LG
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Optimal Scenario Design for Climate Emulation

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that optimizing training datasets themselves—rather than just model architectures—significantly improves climate emulator generalization. A single optimized scenario outperforms emulators trained on six standard climate pathways while using less data.

**Method:**
Uses differentiable sensitivity analysis on a Simple Climate Model (SCM) to iteratively perturb training scenarios, maximizing emulator loss gradients. This identifies "dynamically rich" scenarios that capture diverse physical climate responses without requiring single-forcing experiments.

**Data:**
Compares against ScenarioMIP pathways (standard CMIP6 emissions scenarios); validates optimized scenarios on intermediate-complexity climate models; demonstrates transferability from SCM-optimized to full-complexity model training.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical bottleneck in climate science: full-scale climate models are computationally expensive, limiting available training data for surrogate models. Optimized scenario design reduces computational burden while improving predictive skill for climate projections.

**Implications:**
Enables more efficient use of supercomputing resources for climate modeling; suggests strategic experimental design can replace brute-force scenario expansion; applicable to characterizing responses to different forcing agents (CO₂, aerosols, etc.) without dedicated sensitivity runs.

**Limitations:**
Validation primarily on simplified models; scalability to ultra-high-resolution Earth system models unclear; assumes differentiability of climate model loss landscape may not hold for all systems.

**TL;DR:**
Intelligently designed training scenarios produce better climate emulators than standard emissions pathways, reducing computational costs while improving generalization.

### [Frictional timescales and the impact of climate change-driven extreme weather on rainfall-triggered landslides in Mizoram, NE India](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23281v1)

**Authors:** Pritom Sarma, Krishnendu Paul
**Published:** 2026-06-22
**Categories:** physics.geo-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Climate-Driven Landslide Risk in Mizoram

**Main Contribution:**
Applies rate-and-state friction (RSF) mechanics to 19 rainfall-triggered landslides in Mizoram to quantify how climate change intensifies extreme precipitation and shifts slope failures toward unpredictable, synchronous regimes with minimal warning time.

**Method:**
- Inverts RSF block-slider parameters to reproduce observed failure dates
- Couples satellite rainfall (GPM) with 1D infiltration modeling to propagate pore-pressure transients to failure depth
- Uses dimensionless parameter χ (normalized pore-pressure) to classify failure dynamics into synchronous (χ ≳ 4) vs. delayed (χ ~ 1–3) regimes
- Incorporates velocity-weakening ratio (a/b) to predict failure timing

**Data:**
- 19 observed landslides (2016–2025) in/around Aizawl, Mizoram
- GPM satellite-derived rainfall
- CMIP6 extreme-rainfall projections (SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5 scenarios)
- Miocene shale-dominated lithology characterization

**Climate Relevance:**
Projects that synchronous (zero-warning) landslides increase from ~56% currently to ~72% under high-emissions scenarios (SSP5-8.5), driven by intensified extreme precipitation in northeast India.

**Implications:**
Enables physics-based hazard forecasting for India's highest-risk landslide region; highlights climate-driven escalation of clustered, multi-site failure risk with reduced evacuation windows.

**Limitations:**
Limited to single region/lithology; three hydraulic conductivity scenarios may not capture subsurface heterogeneity; CMIP6 projections carry inherent uncertainty.

**TL;DR:**
Climate-intensified rainfall shifts Mizoram's landslides toward unpredictable synchronous failure, reducing warning time from days to zero.

# Research Summary: Basin-Specific Intensification of Tropical Cyclones

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that tropical cyclone intensification trends are geographically heterogeneous, with significant increases in extreme cyclones only in the North Atlantic basin, contradicting global intensification claims. Establishes a causal chain linking aerosol pollution → sea-surface temperature contrasts → cyclone frequency, but only in the Atlantic.

**Method:**
Time-series analysis (1990–2024) examining correlations between annual aerosol optical depth, relative sea-surface temperature (SST) contrasts, and TC category distribution across four ocean basins. Controls for confounding variables (aerosol changes, ENSO) using statistical regression.

**Data:**
ADT v9.0 (Advanced Dvorak Technique) satellite-derived TC intensity estimates; HURSAT v07b historical TC dataset; basin-specific SST and aerosol optical depth records.

**Climate Relevance:**
Challenges the IPCC consensus on global TC intensification, suggesting regional climate drivers (Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, aerosol forcing) dominate over uniform greenhouse gas warming. Highlights importance of basin-specific attribution for TC risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Refines regional hurricane forecasting and climate projections; suggests Atlantic basin requires distinct risk management strategies. Questions applicability of global TC intensification models to Pacific regions.

**Limitations:**
Limited to 34-year observational window; potential satellite detection biases in intensity estimates; causality inferred from correlation; regional aerosol data quality varies.

**TL;DR:**
Extreme tropical cyclones intensified significantly only in the North Atlantic, driven by aerosol-modulated SST contrasts, contradicting global intensification narratives.

### [PRecover 1.0: Process Rate Recovery with Machine Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20165v1)

**Authors:** Miriam Simm, Tom Beucler, Corinna Hoose
**Published:** 2026-06-18
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# PRecover 1.0: Process Rate Recovery with Machine Learning

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces PRecover, a machine learning post-processing framework to recover unmeasured microphysical process rates from standard numerical weather prediction model output, enabling detailed analysis of precipitation formation and aerosol-cloud interactions without storing computationally expensive intermediate variables.

**Method:**
Trains ensemble ML models (random forests, gradient boosting, feed-forward neural networks) using a two-step classification-regression approach to predict warm-rain and ice microphysical process rates from cloud variables. Provides calibrated prediction intervals via conformalized quantile regression to quantify uncertainty.

**Data:**
High-resolution ICON model simulations over Europe with two-moment bulk microphysics scheme; trained on instantaneous and accumulated process rates at 10-minute output intervals.

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates are critical for understanding precipitation formation pathways and aerosol-cloud interactions—key uncertainties in climate modeling. This approach enables more detailed process-level analysis without prohibitive computational storage costs.

**Implications:**
Allows retrospective recovery of process rates from existing model archives, facilitating deeper investigation of cloud physics in operational weather and climate simulations; demonstrates spatial transferability across different domains and simulation settings.

**Limitations:**
Recovery accuracy degrades significantly for process rates accumulated over longer time intervals (>10 minutes); some processes (autoconversion, rain melting, heterogeneous ice nucleation) cannot be reliably recalculated from standard output alone.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning recovers missing microphysical process rates from weather model output, enabling detailed cloud physics analysis without additional computational overhead.

# Research Summary: BESS Scheduling Under Distribution Fee Structures

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies how distribution tariffs shape battery energy storage (BESS) profitability and operational strategies in renewable-heavy electricity markets. Demonstrates that grid fee structures fundamentally alter whether storage derives value from arbitrage versus self-consumption optimization.

**Method:**
Mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) optimization model of BESS operations with rolling-horizon framework to account for forecast uncertainty. Four system configurations tested: standalone storage and BESS paired with consumption, generation, or both.

**Data:**
German electricity market price data; analysis covers day-ahead market operations under varying distribution fee scenarios.

**Climate Relevance:**
BESS is critical infrastructure for integrating high renewable penetration. Understanding fee-driven operational patterns is essential for designing markets that maximize renewable utilization rather than penalizing storage through tariff structures.

**Implications:**
Regulatory frameworks must account for tariff design when promoting storage deployment. Non-monotonic fee-profitability relationships suggest optimal tariff levels exist; poorly designed fees can undermine storage viability despite technical feasibility. Critical for grid planners designing incentive structures.

**Limitations:**
Analysis limited to German market context; generalizability to other tariff structures or regional markets unclear. Single-node analysis may not capture transmission-level congestion effects on storage value.

**TL;DR:**
Distribution fees significantly alter BESS profitability and shift operational strategies from arbitrage to self-consumption, requiring careful tariff design in renewable-heavy grids.
