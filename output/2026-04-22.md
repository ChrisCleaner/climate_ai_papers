### [Connecting the forward problem to the inverse problem in uncertainty quantification of Earth system models using fast emulators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19500v1)

**Authors:** Ethan YoungIn Shin, Baris Kale, Michael F. Howland
**Published:** 2026-04-21
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Forward-Inverse UQ Coupling for Earth System Models

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that parameter sensitivity from forward uncertainty quantification can systematically identify which observations are most informative for Bayesian calibration of Earth system models, bridging a critical gap between forward and inverse uncertainty analysis.

**Method:**
- Gaussian process emulators trained on ~hundreds of WRF simulations to enable computationally tractable global sensitivity analysis
- Forward UQ via variance-based sensitivity analysis across observation space
- Nondimensional diagnostic measures to identify parameters where signal exceeds observational noise and independent effects dominate interactions
- Bayesian inversion with synthetic observations to validate calibration improvements

**Data:**
Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model simulations for atmospheric turbulence parameterizations; synthetic observations for validation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Atmospheric turbulence parameterization is a major source of uncertainty in weather and climate predictions; improved calibration directly enhances model reliability for climate projections and weather forecasting.

**Implications:**
Enables efficient, principled selection of observational networks for model calibration without expensive trial-and-error; reduces computational burden of uncertainty quantification from O(10⁵) full model runs to manageable emulator-based workflows.

**Limitations:**
Approach demonstrated on single parameterization (turbulence) in one model (WRF); generalizability to other parameterizations and coupled Earth system models requires further validation.

**TL;DR:**
Gaussian process emulators enable fast sensitivity analysis to identify which observations best constrain model parameters, systematically improving Bayesian calibration of Earth system models.

**Data:**

# Research Summary: Skillful Global Ocean Emulation with Correlation-Aware Loss

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a GraphCast-based machine learning emulator for global ocean dynamics that achieves skillful 10-15 day forecasts using a novel correlation-aware loss function (Mahalanobis distance) that outperforms standard MSE by accounting for variable interdependencies.

**Method:**
- Adapts GraphCast architecture for ocean-only predictions driven by prescribed atmospheric forcing
- Replaces MSE loss with Mahalanobis distance to explicitly model correlations between variable tendencies
- Trains on single initial conditions with 24-hour time steps (non-autoregressive approach)
- Validates through spatial correlation analysis of forecasted fields

**Data:**
NOAA's UFS-Replay dataset for training and validation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Ocean dynamics are critical for climate prediction and seasonal forecasting; skillful medium-range ocean emulators improve understanding of ocean-atmosphere coupling and provide better initial conditions for coupled climate models.

**Implications:**
- Enables faster ocean state forecasting for operational weather/climate prediction systems
- Correlation-aware loss acts as a statistical-dynamical regularizer, improving background forecasts for downstream data assimilation in coupled models
- Reduces computational cost compared to traditional ocean models

**Limitations:**
Non-autoregressive design limits forecast horizon; prescribed atmospheric conditions may not capture full ocean-atmosphere feedback; validation limited to medium-range timescales.

**TL;DR:**
A correlation-aware neural network emulator forecasts global ocean states skillfully for 10-15 days by learning interdependencies between ocean variables.
