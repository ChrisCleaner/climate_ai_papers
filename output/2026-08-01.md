### [Memory compression and physical state augmentation favor different AMOC prediction tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28468v1)

**Authors:** Mauricio Herrera-Marín
**Published:** 2026-07-30
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, math.DS, stat.AP
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Memory Compression vs. Physical State for AMOC Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that memory-based (scalar compression) and physics-based (thermohaline structure) approaches optimize different prediction horizons for Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation forecasting. Shows that neither strategy universally dominates across all timescales.

**Method:**
- Compares two neural network strategies: (1) direct physical state augmentation (salinity, temperature, density) and (2) compact scalar memory of observed indices
- Leave-one-family-out cross-validation across 30 CMIP6 trajectories from 8 model families
- Resolvent analysis to explain learned model stability properties
- Ablation studies isolating memory feedback contributions

**Data:**
30 branch-consistent CMIP6 model trajectories from eight model families; SSP5–8.5 emissions scenario; 20-year forecast windows with recursive evaluation at multiple horizons.

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC is a critical climate system component regulating heat transport and regional climate impacts. Improved prediction skill directly supports decadal climate forecasting and early-warning systems for circulation changes.

**Implications:**
Hybrid prediction frameworks should employ physical augmentation for short-term (20-year) forecasts and memory compression for long-horizon recursive predictions. Model-dependent transfer limitations suggest ensemble approaches may be necessary.

**Limitations:**
Model-dependent performance (negative transfer in MIROC); forcing-dependent compression limits in NorESM; validation limited to CMIP6 ensemble without observational testing.

**TL;DR:**
Memory-based and physics-based neural network approaches excel at different AMOC prediction timescales, requiring task-specific model selection rather than universal superiority.

# Weather Emulators at the Frontier of Heat Extremes Predictability

**Main Contribution:**
Comprehensive evaluation of six deep learning weather emulators against traditional dynamical and statistical models for 10-15 day temperature and extreme heat forecasting. Demonstrates that AI models can match or exceed physics-based forecasts in deterministic skill while revealing critical gaps in extreme event representation.

**Method:**
Comparative benchmarking of neural network-based weather emulators (Pangu-Weather, FuXi, ArchesWeather, AIFS, GraphCast, Aurora) using deterministic skill metrics and spectral analysis. Models evaluated against IFS (Integrated Forecasting System) and statistical baselines on near-surface temperature prediction and extreme heat detection.

**Data:**
Global near-surface temperature observations and forecasts; specific datasets not detailed in abstract but standard reanalysis products (likely ERA5) implied for training and validation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in extended-range extreme heat prediction—a key climate adaptation need as warming increases heat wave frequency and intensity. Early warnings at 10-15 day lead times enable public health and infrastructure preparedness.

**Implications:**
AI emulators show promise for operational extended-range forecasting, potentially improving heat early warning systems. However, operational deployment requires resolving spectral blurring and extreme intensity under-representation before replacing or augmenting current systems.

**Limitations:**
Emulators exhibit reduced spectral fidelity ("blurring") and systematically under-predict peak heat intensities compared to IFS. Predictability ceiling at 10-15 days remains fundamentally constrained by atmospheric chaos.

**TL;DR:**
Deep learning weather emulators match traditional forecasts for extended-range temperature but struggle with extreme heat intensity, highlighting both AI potential and remaining challenges for climate-critical predictions.

# HydroAgent Research Summary

**TL;DR:**
