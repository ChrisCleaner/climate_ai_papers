# WetSAM: Wetland Mapping with Temporal-Aware SAM

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes WetSAM, a foundation model-based framework that enables accurate wetland mapping from sparse point annotations by integrating satellite time series data. Addresses the critical gap between SAM's static-image design and the temporal dynamics inherent in wetland ecosystems.

**Method:**
- Dual-branch architecture: (1) temporally prompted branch extends SAM with hierarchical adapters and dynamic temporal aggregation to capture phenological variability; (2) spatial branch uses temporally constrained region-growing for pseudo-label generation
- Bidirectional consistency regularization jointly optimizes both branches
- Leverages multi-temporal satellite imagery to disentangle seasonal/inter-annual wetland dynamics from structural characteristics

**Data:**
Eight global wetland regions (~5,000 km² each); sparse point-level annotations; satellite image time series (specific sensors/platforms not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
Wetlands are critical carbon sinks and biodiversity hotspots; accurate, scalable mapping supports ecosystem monitoring, carbon accounting, and climate adaptation planning. Temporal dynamics tracking enables detection of wetland degradation and hydrological changes.

**Implications:**
Enables cost-effective, high-resolution wetland monitoring at scale with minimal labeling effort. Strong generalization across diverse global regions suggests applicability for operational wetland inventories and climate impact assessments.

**Limitations:**
Abstract does not specify satellite data sources, temporal resolution, or computational requirements; generalization to extreme climate zones unclear.

**TL;DR:**
WetSAM integrates temporal satellite data with SAM to map wetlands accurately from sparse labels, achieving 85.58% F1-score with strong cross-regional generalization.

# Research Summary: Geometry of Aggregate Snowflakes

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a statistical parameterization of irregular snowflake aggregate geometry based on physically-based modeling, capturing the previously-neglected variability in snowflake properties that affects precipitation processes.

**Method:**
- Physics-based aggregation model simulating collision and growth of primary ice crystals
- Generated millions of synthetic snowflakes to characterize geometric distributions
- Fitted lognormal distributions to maximum dimension as function of monomer count and mass
- Derived joint correlations between dimension, aspect ratio, and cross-sectional area for stochastic representation

**Data:**
Synthetic snowflake dataset (millions of aggregates) generated from aggregation model; validated against Doppler radar observations.

**Climate Relevance:**
Snowflake properties directly influence precipitation efficiency, cloud radiative effects, and latent heat release. Most surface precipitation originates as ice; accurate representation is essential for weather and climate model fidelity, particularly for mid-latitude cyclones and orographic precipitation.

**Implications:**
- Enables stochastic snowflake representation in Lagrangian particle models
- Improves simulated fall velocities and aggregation growth rates
- Produces Doppler radar spectra closer to observations, enhancing model-observation comparison for model validation

**Limitations:**
Apparent focus on aggregate geometry alone; microphysical processes (riming, sublimation) and temperature-dependent crystal habits may require separate treatment.

**TL;DR:**
Statistical parameterization of snowflake aggregate variability improves precipitation modeling in weather and climate simulations.

# CROCS: Smart Meter Consumer Segmentation Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes a two-stage clustering framework (CROCS) that segments electricity consumers based on behavioural patterns from smart meter data, addressing limitations of existing methods in capturing consumption diversity and handling real-world data challenges.

**Method:**
- Stage 1: Independent clustering of each consumer's daily load profiles into Representative Load Sets (RLS)
- Stage 2: Consumer-level clustering via Weighted Sum of Minimum Distances (WSMD), a novel set-to-set distance metric comparing behavioural prevalence and similarity
- Stage 3: Community detection on WSMD-induced graph to identify higher-order consumer prototypes

**Data:**
Synthetic and real Australian smart meter datasets; handles anomalies, missing data, and large-scale deployments.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables more effective Demand-Side Management (DSM) and Demand Response (DR) programs critical for grid stability under renewable energy integration and electrification, reducing reliance on fossil fuel peaking capacity.

**Implications:**
Utilities can design targeted DSM/DR programs tailored to consumer behaviour clusters, improving grid flexibility, reducing peak demand, and facilitating renewable energy adoption—key for decarbonization pathways.

**Limitations:**
Not explicitly stated; potential concerns include generalizability beyond Australian context and computational overhead of two-stage clustering at continental scales.

**TL;DR:**
CROCS clusters electricity consumers by behavioural patterns from smart meter data to optimize demand response programs supporting renewable grid integration.
