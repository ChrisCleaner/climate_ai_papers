### [ACE2-NEMO: Coupling an ML atmospheric emulator to a full-depth dynamical ocean model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28704v1)

**Authors:** Bobby Antonio, Kristian Strommen, Pablo Ortega...
**Published:** 2026-03-30
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# ACE2-NEMO Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
First multi-decadal (70-year) coupled climate simulation integrating a machine-learned atmospheric emulator (ACE2) with a full-depth dynamical ocean model (NEMO). Demonstrates feasibility of ML-atmosphere/physics-ocean hybrid systems for climate-scale applications.

**Method:**
• ML atmospheric emulator (ACE2) coupled interactively to NEMO ocean model
• 70-year simulations: historical (1950-2020) and fixed-1950s control runs
• Comparison against EC-Earth3P full-physics coupled model using identical ocean component

**Data:**
Historical forcing (1950-2020) and control simulations; validation against EC-Earth3P outputs.

**Climate Relevance:**
Tests whether ML atmospheric models can realistically simulate slow climate variability (El Niño, decadal oscillations) and climate sensitivity to CO₂ forcing—critical for understanding atmosphere-ocean coupling mechanisms.

**Implications:**
Hybrid ML-physics models could accelerate Earth system modeling by replacing computationally expensive atmospheric components while retaining full ocean dynamics; enables rapid climate sensitivity assessments.

**Limitations:**
• Weak tropical Pacific atmospheric feedback produces unrealistically low-amplitude El Niño variability
• Reduced shortwave radiation in ACE2 causes CO₂ response divergence from EC-Earth3P
• Limited to 70-year integrations; longer-term stability unclear

**TL;DR:**
ML atmospheric emulators coupled to physics-based oceans show promise for climate modeling but require improved tropical feedback representation.

# Research Summary: Calibrated Conformal Prediction for Microphysical Processes

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that conformal prediction methods can provide statistically valid, well-calibrated uncertainty quantification for ML emulators of atmospheric microphysical processes without requiring model retraining. Shows conformalized quantile regression outperforms split conformal prediction for climate variables spanning multiple orders of magnitude.

**Method:**
- Split conformal prediction applied to deterministic emulators
- Conformalized quantile regression applied to quantile regression emulators
- Both methods generate prediction intervals with statistical validity guarantees and minimal computational overhead

**Data:**
ML emulators trained on ICOsahedral Nonhydrostatic (ICON) model output in limited-area numerical weather prediction configuration; uncertainty quantified across six microphysical process rates.

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates govern cloud precipitation formation and aerosol-cloud interactions—critical mechanisms for accurate weather and climate prediction. Reliable uncertainty quantification improves model credibility for climate projections.

**Implications:**
Enables practitioners to deploy existing ML emulators with principled uncertainty estimates, supporting better decision-making in weather forecasting and climate modeling without algorithmic modifications.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to ICON model output; generalization to other climate models or observational data not addressed; computational cost comparison with alternative uncertainty methods not provided.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal prediction methods provide statistically valid uncertainty intervals for climate ML emulators, with conformalized quantile regression preferred for multi-scale atmospheric variables.

# Research Summary: Data-Driven Eddy Parameterization in CESM

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that a machine learning-based eddy parameterization (Zanna & Bolton 2020) significantly alters coupled climate dynamics when integrated into a realistic ocean-atmosphere model, producing hemispheric asymmetries and large-scale circulation changes.

**Method:**
Implements a data-driven eddy momentum parameterization featuring upgradient (backscatter) flux in MOM6 ocean model within fully coupled CESM. Uses regional heat-budget analysis to decompose temperature responses into meridional heat transport versus local surface flux contributions.

**Data:**
Idealized coupled CESM configuration with eddy-permitting ocean resolution; parameterization trained on prior machine learning work (ZB20) but specific training datasets not detailed in abstract.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in climate modeling: mesoscale ocean eddies (~10-100 km) drive heat transport and energy dissipation but are unresolved in coarse-resolution climate models. Improved parameterization directly impacts ocean circulation and poleward heat transport—key drivers of regional climate patterns.

**Implications:**
Results provide benchmark for implementing data-driven parameterizations in comprehensive climate models; suggests eddy momentum effects warrant careful consideration in coupled model development and climate projections, particularly for Southern Hemisphere dynamics.

**Limitations:**
Idealized model configuration may not capture full complexity of realistic bathymetry and forcing; hemispheric asymmetry results may be configuration-specific; transferability to higher-resolution or different model frameworks unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning-based eddy parameterization substantially reshapes ocean-atmosphere coupling and mean climate state through enhanced meridional heat transport, with pronounced Southern Hemisphere effects.

**Implications:**

**Limitations:**

# RG-TTA Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes Regime-Guided Test-Time Adaptation (RG-TTA), a meta-controller that dynamically adjusts adaptation intensity for neural time series forecasters based on distributional similarity to previously-encountered regimes, enabling efficient handling of non-stationary streaming data.

**Method:**
- Computes regime similarity using an ensemble of four statistical metrics (Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Wasserstein-1, feature-distance, variance-ratio)
- Modulates learning rate dynamically: aggressive for novel distributions, conservative for familiar ones
- Implements loss-driven early stopping instead of fixed computational budgets
- Gates checkpoint reuse from regime memory (loads specialist models only if loss improvement ≥30%)
- Model-agnostic wrapper compatible with any forecaster and composable with gradient-based TTA methods

**Data:**
14 datasets: 6 real-world multivariate benchmarks + 8 synthetic regime scenarios; evaluated across 4 forecast horizons (96, 192, 336, 720 timesteps) with streaming protocol.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to climate and weather forecasting where distribution shifts (regime changes) are common—e.g., seasonal transitions, climate anomalies, extreme events—requiring adaptive models that efficiently adjust without retraining.

**Implications:**
Enables operational forecasting systems to autonomously adapt to non-stationary conditions with minimal computational overhead; particularly valuable for resource-constrained climate monitoring and real-time environmental prediction.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to time series forecasting; unclear how method scales to very high-dimensional climate model outputs or multi-modal climate variables; regime memory management strategy not detailed for long-term streaming scenarios.

**TL;DR:**
