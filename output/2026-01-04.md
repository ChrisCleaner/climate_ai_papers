### [Towards mechanistic understanding in a data-driven weather model: internal activations reveal interpretable physical features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24440v1)

**Authors:** Theodore MacMillan, Nicholas T. Ouellette
**Published:** 2025-12-30
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, cs.LG, physics.comp-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Mechanistic Understanding in Data-Driven Weather Models

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that GraphCast's internal representations are interpretable and physically meaningful by applying sparse autoencoders to extract distinct features corresponding to real atmospheric phenomena. Provides a methodology for opening the "black box" of neural weather models through systematic feature analysis.

**Method:**
• Adapted sparse autoencoders from LLM interpretability research to analyze intermediate layers in GraphCast
• Identified interpretable features in neuron space across multiple spatiotemporal scales
• Validated features through causal interventions: selectively modifying tropical cyclone features and observing physically consistent model responses

**Data:**
GraphCast model (DeepMind's neural weather prediction system); analysis focuses on learned representations rather than specific input datasets.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses interpretability of state-of-the-art neural weather models that now match or exceed traditional physics-based forecasting. Enables validation of whether AI models capture genuine atmospheric physics versus spurious correlations—critical for trustworthiness in climate applications.

**Implications:**
• Supports use of neural models as scientifically credible tools for weather/climate prediction and discovery
• Intervention capability enables targeted model improvement and hypothesis testing
• Framework applicable to other data-driven physics models beyond weather

**Limitations:**
Focuses on one model (GraphCast); unclear how generalizable the approach is to other architectures or climate variables; intervention study limited to tropical cyclone case study.

**TL;DR:**
Sparse autoencoders reveal that GraphCast learns physically interpretable atmospheric features, validated through causal interventions on hurricane predictions.

# Research Summary: Optimal Carbon Prices in an Unequal World

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that incorporating global inequality (via regional welfare weights reflecting differences in marginal utility of consumption) into climate policy design leads to more stringent optimal carbon pricing. Shows that inequality-adjusted policies reduce global emissions by 15-21% compared to inequality-insensitive benchmarks.

**Method:**
Theoretical framework identifying conditions under which welfare-weighted optimization increases policy stringency; calibrated economic simulations comparing optimal carbon prices under different welfare weight specifications and regional differentiation constraints.

**Data:**
Calibrated simulations (specific datasets not detailed in abstract); appears to use regional economic parameters and consumption data typical of integrated assessment models.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses carbon pricing mechanisms—the primary policy tool for climate mitigation—by quantifying how distributional equity considerations affect emissions reduction targets and carbon price levels.

**Implications:**
Suggests that equity-conscious climate policy design naturally supports more aggressive emissions reductions without requiring international wealth transfers, potentially aligning climate ambition with fairness objectives in policy negotiations.

**Limitations:**
Abstract does not specify model assumptions, regional coverage, or sensitivity to welfare weight specifications; unclear whether results hold across different climate damage functions or time horizons.

**TL;DR:**
Accounting for global inequality in welfare calculations justifies higher optimal carbon prices and lower emissions targets, bridging climate ambition with distributional equity.
