# Research Summary: Optimal Scenario Design for Climate Emulation

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that optimizing training datasets themselves—rather than just model architectures—can significantly improve climate emulator generalization. A single optimized scenario outperforms emulators trained on six standard climate pathways while using less data.

**Method:**
Uses automatic differentiation through a Simple Climate Model (SCM) to compute gradients of emulator loss with respect to training data perturbations. Iteratively updates scenario parameters to maximize emulator skill on held-out test scenarios, then validates findings on intermediate-complexity climate models.

**Data:**
Compares against ScenarioMIP pathways (standard CMIP6 emissions scenarios). Demonstrates superior performance using one optimized scenario versus six traditional pathways; validates approach on intermediate-complexity model outputs.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical bottleneck in climate science: full-scale climate models are computationally expensive, limiting available training data for surrogate models. Optimized scenario design maximizes information content per simulation, enabling better characterization of climate system responses to different forcing agents (CO₂, aerosols, etc.).

**Implications:**
Reduces computational burden for climate modeling by achieving higher emulator accuracy with fewer expensive model runs. Enables more efficient exploration of climate response space and improved uncertainty quantification in climate projections.

**Limitations:**
Validation primarily on simplified models (SCM and intermediate-complexity); scalability to full Earth System Models unclear. Approach assumes differentiability through climate models, which may not hold for all complex systems.

**TL;DR:**
Optimizing training scenarios through gradient-based methods produces more generalizable climate emulators than using standard emissions pathways, reducing computational costs while improving predictive skill.

# Research Summary: Constrained Hybrid Modelling for Soil Microbial Dynamics

**Main Contribution:**
First framework integrating metagenomics data with process-based soil models to predict biokinetic parameters governing organic matter turnover. Bridges the gap between genomic information and mechanistic soil carbon cycling models.

**Method:**
Neural network trained on metagenome-inferred functional traits to predict biokinetic parameters for a process-based soil organic matter model. Incorporates ecological constraints and literature-based priors to ensure physically realistic predictions of unmeasured state variables.

**Data:**
Synthetic genomic trait datasets of varying complexity and real metagenomic data from soil samples. Training demonstrated effectiveness even with limited datasets.

**Climate Relevance:**
Soil microbial communities are critical regulators of carbon cycling and soil carbon storage—a key feedback mechanism in climate change mitigation. Improved predictive models of microbial-driven organic matter decomposition enhance carbon cycle projections under future climate scenarios.

**Implications:**
Enables parameterization of soil carbon models using readily available DNA sequencing data rather than labor-intensive laboratory measurements. Scalable approach for improving regional and global soil carbon predictions in Earth system models.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to synthetic and single real dataset; generalization across diverse soil types and climates requires further validation. Interpretability of neural network predictions for mechanistic understanding remains unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning bridges genomic data and soil process models to predict microbial-driven carbon cycling with improved accuracy and minimal training data.

# PRecover 1.0: Process Rate Recovery with Machine Learning

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces PRecover, a machine learning post-processing framework to recover unmeasured microphysical process rates from standard weather model output, enabling detailed analysis of precipitation formation and aerosol-cloud interactions without storing computationally expensive diagnostic variables.

**Method:**
• Random forests, gradient boosting, and feed-forward neural networks trained on cloud variables
• Two-step classification-regression approach for warm-rain and ice processes
• Calibrated prediction intervals via conformalized quantile regression
• Physics-based baseline comparison for validation

**Data:**
High-resolution ICON model simulations over Europe in limited-area setup; trained on standard model output variables (cloud water, ice, precipitation fields).

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates are critical for understanding precipitation formation pathways and aerosol-cloud interactions—key uncertainties in climate modeling. Recovering these rates from existing model output enables better process-level validation without additional computational cost.

**Implications:**
Allows retrospective analysis of archived model simulations; reduces storage requirements for future simulations; improves understanding of cloud-precipitation-aerosol coupling in weather and climate models.

**Limitations:**
• Accuracy degrades for process rates accumulated over long intervals (>10 minutes)
• Some processes (autoconversion, rain melting, heterogeneous ice nucleation) cannot be reliably recalculated from stored variables alone
• Transferability demonstrated only across regional domains; global applicability untested

**TL;DR:**
Machine learning recovers missing cloud microphysical process rates from standard model output, enabling detailed precipitation analysis without additional computational overhead.

# Research Summary: XAI for European Electricity Markets

**Main Contribution:**
Combines deep neural networks with explainable AI (SHAP/SSHAP) to identify and quantify drivers of electricity price formation across 39 European bidding zones, addressing the interpretability gap in DNN-based price prediction models.

**Method:**
- Deep neural networks for price prediction
- SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for feature attribution
- SSHAP framework for aggregating interpretability in high-dimensional settings
- Counterfactual analysis of a hypothetical fully integrated EU electricity market

**Data:**
39 European electricity bidding zones; features include renewable generation (solar/wind), gas prices, interconnection flows, and demand variables (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
Quantifies the impact of renewable energy sources on electricity pricing, revealing that solar generation disproportionately influences price formation despite lower generation share—critical for understanding market incentives for renewable deployment under climate transition scenarios.

**Implications:**
Enables policymakers and market operators to understand price dynamics and interdependencies, supporting design of market mechanisms that better incentivize renewable integration and inform cross-border electricity trading strategies in a decarbonizing grid.

**Limitations:**
Abstract does not specify temporal scope, model validation metrics, or whether analysis accounts for seasonal/temporal variations in renewable availability.

**TL;DR:**
XAI reveals that renewables—particularly solar—have outsized influence on European electricity prices despite lower generation share, with implications for climate-aligned market design.
