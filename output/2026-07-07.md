### [A harmonised dataset for Earth system foundation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03298v1)

**Published:** 2026-07-03
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, econ.GN

# WorldTensor: A Harmonised Dataset for Earth System Foundation Models

**Main Contribution:**
WorldTensor is a unified global dataset that harmonises hundreds of environmental and socioeconomic variables onto a standardised 0.25° spatial grid with annual temporal resolution, enabling training of multimodal Earth system foundation models that integrate human and natural systems.

**Method:**
• Regridding heterogeneous datasets to common 0.25° grid and annual timesteps
• Rasterisation of point/vector data into gridded fields
• Temporal reconciliation across daily observations to sparse multiyear snapshots
• Standardised NetCDF output with CF metadata conventions

**Data:**
Integrates reanalysis products, remote sensing, emissions inventories, land use reconstructions, hydrological observations, infrastructure datasets, hazard data, and socioeconomic indicators.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses a critical gap in foundation model training by combining physical climate/weather data with human systems (infrastructure, emissions, socioeconomics), enabling models to learn coupled climate-society dynamics at planetary scale.

**Implications:**
Provides reproducible infrastructure for developing foundation models that can predict coupled environmental-human system responses, improving climate impact assessments and adaptation planning.

**Limitations:**
Temporal coverage varies significantly (daily to sparse multiyear); annual resolution may obscure sub-annual climate variability; data quality and availability disparities across regions not explicitly discussed.

**TL;DR:**
WorldTensor standardises hundreds of climate and socioeconomic variables into a unified gridded dataset designed for training multimodal Earth system foundation models.

# Research Summary: Argo Ocean Heat Content Estimation

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a statistically rigorous framework for mapping global ocean heat content (OHC) from Argo float data with quantified uncertainties. Introduces novel uncertainty quantification methods specifically designed for large-scale spatio-temporal oceanographic datasets.

**Method:**
- Locally stationary Gaussian process modeling of vertically integrated temperature profiles
- Data-driven decorrelation scales estimated directly from Argo observations
- Local conditional simulation ensembles for spatially/temporally correlated uncertainty quantification
- Paired cross-validation technique for uncertainty validation
- Includes climatological time trends in mean field and temporal covariance structure

**Data:**
Argo profiling float measurements (2004-2022) of seawater temperature and salinity in upper 2000 meters of ocean.

**Climate Relevance:**
Ocean heat content is a critical indicator of Earth Energy Imbalance and essential for understanding climate change impacts. Reliable uncertainty estimates enable better assessment of climate system heat redistribution and validation of climate models.

**Implications:**
Produces publicly available OHC anomaly maps with principled uncertainties, enabling improved climate monitoring, model evaluation, and detection of long-term ocean warming trends with quantified confidence intervals.

**Limitations:**
Measurement depth limited to 2000 meters (excludes deep ocean); framework validation demonstrated on 2004-2022 period (relatively recent instrumental record); computational tractability depends on decorrelation scale assumptions.

**TL;DR:**
Novel spatio-temporal statistical framework produces uncertainty-quantified global ocean heat content maps from Argo floats, advancing climate monitoring capabilities.

# Storm Track Self-Reinforcement Through Cloud Radiative Effects

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that cloud radiative effects (not just solar heating gradients) are critical drivers of Southern Hemisphere storm activity, particularly during summer-autumn when insolation gradients are weak. Identifies a self-reinforcing feedback loop between storms, clouds, and sea-surface temperature gradients.

**Method:**
- Satellite observations to quantify cloud albedo effects on meridional surface heating
- Idealized aquaplanet climate simulations to isolate shortwave/longwave cloud radiative effects
- Theoretical model linking storms, clouds, and SST gradients to explain feedback mechanisms

**Data:**
Satellite observations of cloud properties and radiation; output from idealized aquaplanet simulations (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
Reveals previously underappreciated feedback mechanisms sustaining midlatitude storm tracks. Critical for understanding Southern Hemisphere climate dynamics and predicting storm activity under changing cloud regimes in a warming climate.

**Implications:**
- Improved storm-track predictions require accurate cloud radiative parameterizations in climate models
- Cloud albedo and storm-sensitivity properties emerge as key predictive variables
- Highlights multi-scale coupling (storms↔clouds↔ocean) essential for climate projections

**Limitations:**
Idealized aquaplanet simulations may not capture realistic continental geometry and land-ocean contrasts; applicability to Northern Hemisphere requires further investigation.

**TL;DR:**
Cloud radiative feedbacks self-reinforce Southern Hemisphere storm activity by maintaining meridional temperature gradients independent of seasonal insolation changes.

**Implications:**

**Limitations:**

**TL;DR:**
