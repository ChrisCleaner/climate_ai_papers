### [Gray Swan Factory: Making Extreme Events from Ordinary Cyclones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00348v1)

**Authors:** Gregory J. Hakim, Aishwarya Agrawal
**Published:** 2026-04-01
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, nlin.CD
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Gray Swan Factory: Making Extreme Events from Ordinary Cyclones

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a gradient descent optimization method using differentiable weather models to generate "gray swans"—plausible but unobserved extreme weather events. Demonstrates the approach by transforming hurricane Fiona (2022) into a Sandy-like scenario with intensified pressure and altered track.

**Method:**
• Gradient descent optimization on a differentiable weather prediction model
• Loss function minimizes distance to target outcome (e.g., Sandy-like track)
• Constrained perturbations applied to reanalysis initial conditions
• Identifies which atmospheric state variables (extratropical vs. tropical) drive extreme outcomes

**Data:**
Reanalysis initial conditions for Atlantic hurricanes (Fiona 2022 and four others); hurricane Sandy (2012) as reference extreme event.

**Climate Relevance:**
Extends understanding of hurricane hazards beyond the limited observational record (~70 years), critical for assessing compound risks like landfall intensity and track changes driven by extratropical interactions.

**Implications:**
Applicable to infrastructure design, emergency planning, insurance hazard assessment, and synthetic training data generation for machine learning models. Reveals that extratropical-tropical cyclone interactions are key to extreme outcomes.

**Limitations:**
• Hurricane core not resolved by optimization model; impact unknown
• Method produces plausible scenarios but does not quantify probability of occurrence
• No assessment of physical realizability or likelihood in actual climate dynamics

**TL;DR:**
Uses differentiable weather models with gradient descent to generate realistic but unobserved extreme hurricane scenarios for hazard assessment and planning.

# ACE2-NEMO Research Summary

**Main Contribution:**
First multi-decadal coupled climate simulations (70 years) integrating a machine-learned atmospheric emulator (ACE2) with a full-depth dynamical ocean model (NEMO). This represents a novel test of ML atmospheric models' viability in Earth system modeling beyond weather timescales.

**Method:**
Hybrid modeling framework coupling ACE2 (ML-based atmospheric emulator) to NEMO ocean model with interactive air-sea exchange. Simulations span 1950-2020 historical forcing and fixed-1950s control runs, compared against EC-Earth3P full-physics model outputs.

**Data:**
70-year coupled simulations (historical and control); EC-Earth3P comparison simulations; tropical Pacific observations (implicit for validation).

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in understanding atmosphere-ocean interactions across timescales from weather to climate change. Tests whether ML models can capture slow climate variability (El Niño, CO₂ sensitivity) essential for climate projections.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates feasibility of ML components in next-generation Earth system models, potentially enabling faster climate simulations. Identifies specific failure modes (weak tropical Pacific feedback, shortwave radiation biases) requiring improvement before operational use.

**Limitations:**
Unrealistic El Niño amplitude and temporal variability; reduced shortwave radiation in ACE2 causes CO₂ response divergence from physics-based models; single ML emulator tested.

**TL;DR:**
ML atmospheric emulators show promise in coupled climate models but exhibit critical biases in tropical dynamics and radiation that must be resolved for climate prediction applications.

### [Calibrated Conformal Prediction Intervals for Microphysical Process Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27699v1)

**Authors:** Miriam Simm, Corinna Hoose, Tom Beucler
**Published:** 2026-03-29
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Calibrated Conformal Prediction for Microphysical Process Rates

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that conformal prediction methods can provide statistically valid, well-calibrated uncertainty intervals for ML emulators of atmospheric microphysical processes without modifying the underlying models. Shows conformalized quantile regression outperforms split conformal prediction for climate-relevant variables spanning multiple orders of magnitude.

**Method:**
- Compares two conformal approaches: split conformal prediction (for deterministic emulators) and conformalized quantile regression (for quantile regression emulators)
- Both methods generate prediction intervals with guaranteed statistical validity through distribution-free calibration
- Minimal computational overhead; no model retraining required

**Data:**
ML emulators trained on output from the ICON (ICOsahedral Nonhydrostatic) model in limited-area numerical weather prediction configuration; applied to six microphysical process rates.

**Climate Relevance:**
Microphysical process rates govern cloud formation, precipitation, and aerosol-cloud interactions—critical processes for accurate weather prediction and climate modeling. Uncertainty quantification improves model reliability for climate projections.

**Implications:**
Enables practitioners to deploy existing ML emulators with quantified uncertainty bounds, supporting more robust climate and weather forecasting without model redesign or retraining.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to ICON model outputs; generalization to other climate models or observational data not explicitly addressed. Computational cost comparison with alternative uncertainty methods not detailed.

**TL;DR:**
Conformal prediction methods provide reliable, computationally efficient uncertainty quantification for cloud microphysics ML emulators, with conformalized quantile regression performing best across variable scales.

### [Remote Influences of Land Surface Temperature and their Implications for Sea Surface Temperature Patterns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28481v1)

**Authors:** Bosong Zhang, Timothy M. Merlis
**Published:** 2026-03-30
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: LST Remote Influences on SST Patterns

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that regional land surface temperature (LST) changes drive remote sea surface temperature (SST) pattern shifts through atmospheric teleconnections, particularly strengthening tropical Pacific zonal gradients. Identifies a mechanistic pathway linking land warming to equatorial Pacific mean state changes via stationary Rossby waves and coastal upwelling modulation.

**Method:**
- Coupled ocean-land-atmosphere model simulations with LST nudging
- Regional LST perturbation experiments (idealized ramp warming)
- Historical simulations constrained to observed LST
- Dynamical analysis of diabatic heating and Rossby wave responses

**Data:**
Observational LST data used for historical model nudging; model-generated outputs from coupled climate simulations (specific datasets not detailed in abstract).

**Climate Relevance:**
SST patterns are primary drivers of global climate variability and precipitation. Understanding LST-SST coupling is critical for explaining tropical climate modes (ENSO, Atlantic variability) and improving climate model fidelity, particularly given persistent model biases in tropical SST.

**Implications:**
- LST uncertainty may explain model-simulated SST biases in historical simulations
- Regional land-use/land-cover changes could have far-field oceanic consequences
- Improved LST representation in climate models may enhance SST prediction skill
- Identifies South America, North America, and Central Africa as regions with significant remote SST influence

**Limitations:**
Geographic specificity of responses (Maritime Continent and Tibetan Plateau show minimal effects); historical cooling implications remain "tentative"; mechanism may be model-dependent.

**TL;DR:**
Land surface warming over specific continental regions triggers atmospheric teleconnections that reshape tropical ocean temperature patterns, with implications for climate model biases and predictability.

# Research Summary: Omega-blocks and Compound European Extremes

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that upstream warm conveyor belt (WCB) activity 5+ days ahead distinguishes omega-block scenarios producing severe compound heat-flood extremes over Europe from weaker cases, despite similar initial large-scale patterns. Identifies remote atmospheric precursors critical for predicting spatially compounding disasters.

**Method:**
- Ensemble boosting to generate hundreds of physically plausible simulations of omega-prone atmospheric configurations
- Lagrangian air parcel tracking to trace WCB outflow evolution and downstream impacts
- Fully coupled climate model simulations with storyline analysis to isolate dynamical differences

**Data:**
Coupled climate model simulations (specific model not named in abstract); analysis focuses on September 2023 case study (France heatwave + Iberian/Greek floods) as validation example.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses the critical challenge of predicting compound extremes—simultaneous heat and precipitation disasters across multiple regions—which pose amplified societal risks beyond individual extreme events and are expected to intensify under climate change.

**Implications:**
Enables extended-range forecasting (5+ days) of compound extremes by monitoring upstream WCB signatures; improves early warning systems and disaster preparedness for multi-region simultaneous impacts.

**Limitations:**
Analysis appears limited to one case study; generalizability to other omega-block configurations and seasons unclear; computational cost of ensemble boosting approach not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Remote upstream atmospheric precursors in warm conveyor belts can predict European compound heat-flood extremes 5+ days in advance using ensemble boosting and Lagrangian tracking.
