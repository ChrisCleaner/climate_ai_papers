### [Memory compression and physical state augmentation favor different AMOC prediction tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28468v1)

**Categories:** physics.ao-ph, math.DS, stat.AP

# Research Summary: Memory Compression vs. Physical State for AMOC Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Compares two neural network strategies for Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) prediction: augmenting models with explicit physical state (temperature, salinity, density) versus using compact scalar memory. Demonstrates that these approaches excel at different prediction horizons and tasks.

**Method:**
- Leave-one-family-out cross-validation across 8 CMIP6 model families (30 trajectories total)
- Direct 20-year forecasts vs. recursive multi-step predictions
- Resolvent analysis to diagnose why memory stability doesn't guarantee model stability
- Ablation studies isolating memory feedback contributions

**Data:**
CMIP6 branch-consistent trajectories under SSP5–8.5 emissions scenario; analysis includes NorESM and MIROC model outputs.

**Climate Relevance:**
AMOC is a critical climate tipping point affecting heat transport and regional climate stability. Improved prediction methods inform early-warning systems and climate risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Hybrid approaches may optimize AMOC forecasting by selecting memory-based methods for long-horizon projections and physics-augmented models for near-term state changes. Results guide machine learning architecture design for climate emulation.

**Limitations:**
Model-family transfer performance varies (negative transfer in MIROC); findings may be scenario-dependent; resolvent analysis suggests fundamental constraints on memory-only compression at extreme forcing levels.

**TL;DR:**
Memory compression and physical state augmentation serve complementary roles in AMOC prediction, with memory excelling at recursive horizons and physics improving direct forecasts.

# Weather Emulators at the Frontier of Heat Extremes Predictability

**Main Contribution:**
Comprehensive evaluation of six deep learning weather emulators against traditional dynamical and statistical models for extended-range (10-15 day) temperature and extreme heat forecasting. Demonstrates that AI emulators achieve competitive deterministic skill but struggle with extreme event representation.

**Method:**
Benchmarking study comparing neural network-based weather emulators (Pangu-Weather, FuXi, ArchesWeather, AIFS, GraphCast, Aurora) using deterministic skill metrics and spectral analysis. Evaluation focuses on near-surface temperature prediction and extreme heat detection at subseasonal timescales.

**Data:**
Global atmospheric reanalysis data; specific datasets not detailed in abstract but typical for weather emulator training (likely ERA5 or similar).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses critical climate adaptation need: early warning systems for heat extremes become increasingly valuable as warming intensifies frequency and severity of dangerous heat events. **Implications:**

**Limitations:**
Emulators exhibit "blurring" (reduced spectral fidelity) and systematically under-represent peak heat intensities. Traditional IFS model outperforms all emulators in extreme event recall, limiting operational readiness.

**TL;DR:**
Deep learning weather emulators rival physics models in temperature skill at 10-15 day lead times but underestimate extreme heat peaks, requiring further development for reliable heat warning systems.

# HydroAgent: Skill-Orchestrated Flood Forecasting

**Main Contribution:**
Formalizes tacit flood forecaster expertise into an auditable, reproducible AI workflow by embedding Large Language Models (LLMs) within a model-driven framework where explicit rules constrain LLM reasoning. Demonstrates that skill-orchestrated agents can enhance operational flood prediction while maintaining human oversight and interpretability.

**Method:**
- Architecture: LLM-based agent integrated into physics-based flood forecasting workflow with skill modules encoding explicit expert rules and review checkpoints
- Approach: Each skill bounds LLM reasoning to prevent hallucination; guided scheme selection improves model ensemble performance
- Validation: Tested five state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4, Claude, etc.) with 5-fold cross-validation across 129 flood events

**Data:**
South Yamhill River basin (Oregon); 14 major flood events with 129 total validation samples; baseline hydrological model ensemble (average KGE 0.890).

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses operational flood forecasting—a critical climate adaptation challenge as extreme precipitation events intensify. Improves prediction of peak flow and flood volume, essential for early warning systems and emergency response in changing climate conditions.

**Implications:**
Enables transfer of forecaster expertise to operational systems; reduces analytical bottlenecks; supports real-time decision-making with transparent, auditable reasoning; scalable to other hydrological forecasting domains.

**Limitations:**
Performance varies across LLMs (40%-80% accuracy); tested on single basin; does not replace human forecasters; computational costs and LLM availability dependencies not fully explored.

**TL;DR:**
HydroAgent translates expert flood forecasting knowledge into an LLM-guided, rule-bounded workflow that improves operational predictions while maintaining interpretability and human control.
