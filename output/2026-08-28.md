### [Precipitation Downscaling Using Foundation Model-Conditioned Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25858v1)

**Authors:** Victor Nascimento Ribeiro, Jorge Guevara, Jorge Sebastian Moraga...
**Published:** 2026-08-26
**Categories:** cs.CV, cs.LG, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Precipitation Downscaling Using Foundation Model-Conditioned Diffusion

**Main Contribution:**
Compares three conditioning strategies for diffusion models applied to precipitation downscaling, demonstrating that cross-attention conditioning—particularly with pretrained foundation models—outperforms simple concatenation for realistic high-resolution precipitation generation.

**Method:**
Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) conditioned via: (1) channel concatenation of upsampled coarse predictors, (2) cross-attention with learned convolutional encoder, (3) cross-attention with frozen Prithvi WxC weather foundation model encoder. Evaluated using probabilistic (CRPS), distributional, spectral, and extreme-event metrics.

**Data:**
Daily precipitation fields for the Colorado River Basin; models trained on varying dataset sizes (5 years to full dataset) to assess data efficiency.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap between global climate model (GCM) outputs (~100 km resolution) and hydrological impact assessment needs (~1 km). High-resolution, unbiased precipitation fields are essential for water resource management and flood/drought risk assessment.

**Implications:**
Foundation model conditioning enables effective downscaling with limited training data (5 years), reducing computational barriers for climate impact studies. Better extreme event representation improves hazard assessment accuracy.

**Limitations:**
Extreme event evaluation constrained by small sample sizes (>100 mm/day events); evaluation limited to single basin; computational cost comparison incomplete for foundation model approach.

**TL;DR:**
Cross-attention conditioning with pretrained foundation models produces more realistic high-resolution precipitation than concatenation, with particular advantages for extreme events and data-limited scenarios.

# DySCo: Dynamically Consistent Climate Downscaling

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces DySCo, a non-intrusive machine learning framework that downscales coarse global climate model outputs to high spatial resolution while maintaining dynamical consistency with the original GCM trajectories. Addresses the critical challenge of preserving causal relationships in extreme event analysis during statistical bias correction.

**Method:**
- Data-driven reformulation of nudging to generate dynamically paired training trajectories without modifying the GCM
- Two-stage operator trained on paired trajectories to achieve both dynamical and statistical consistency
- Applies minimal, causal corrections to GCM outputs rather than free-form statistical mapping

**Data:**
Community Earth System Model v2 Large Ensemble (LENS2) downscaled toward historical reanalysis in both temporal and spatial dimensions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Enables accurate quantification of rare extreme events (long return periods) at regional scales by combining high-resolution spatial detail with dynamically faithful GCM evolution—essential for understanding climate risks in specific locations.

**Implications:**
Directly applicable to infrastructure design, disaster forecasting, insurance modeling, and storyline-based risk assessment where understanding *how* extremes evolve dynamically (not just their statistical properties) is critical for planning.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to LENS2 and historical reanalysis; generalization to other GCMs or future projection scenarios not explicitly demonstrated; computational cost comparison with alternatives not provided.

**TL;DR:**
DySCo downscales climate models to high resolution while preserving dynamical consistency, enabling physically plausible extreme event analysis for regional risk assessment.

# Research Summary: Video Diffusion for Satellite-based Precipitation Generation

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces HiDFiP, a video-diffusion framework that generates high spatiotemporal fidelity precipitation fields from satellite data by explicitly modeling storm dynamics and lifecycle evolution. Addresses the fundamental limitation that satellite precipitation products lack the spatial organization and temporal coherence of ground-based radar observations.

**Method:**
• Video diffusion model with explicit spatiotemporal modeling (not frame-by-frame)
• Uses IMERG satellite data as primary input
• Incorporates ~40 ERA5/ERA5-Land atmospheric and land surface fields to compensate for satellite temporal information gaps
• Trained and validated against MRMS ground-radar reference data over CONUS

**Data:**
IMERG (satellite precipitation), MRMS (ground-radar reference), ERA5/ERA5-Land (atmospheric/land fields), CONUS region as primary testbed.

**Climate Relevance:**
Satellite data provide the only near-global precipitation observations essential for hydrometeorological research and climate monitoring, but suffer from retrieval artifacts. This work improves global precipitation product fidelity for climate modeling, extreme weather analysis, and water resource assessment.

**Implications:**
Enables generation of radar-grade precipitation records globally beyond radar-covered regions; improves input data quality for hydrological models, flood forecasting, and climate reanalysis; supports long-term consistent precipitation datasets for climate trend analysis.

**Limitations:**
Transferability tested only on one unseen region; computational cost of video diffusion not discussed; generalization to tropical/monsoon regions unclear.

**TL;DR:**
Video diffusion framework reconstructs realistic storm dynamics and spatial organization in global satellite precipitation data by leveraging atmospheric context, achieving radar-equivalent fidelity beyond ground-radar coverage.

### [Tracing the Unlabeled Storm: Cross-Variable Transfer in a Lagrangian Atmospheric JEPA Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22358v1)

**Authors:** K M Anirudh, S Sandeep, Hariprasad Kodamana
**Published:** 2026-08-23
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.geo-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Cross-Variable Transfer in Lagrangian Atmospheric JEPA

**Main Contribution:**
Introduces M-JEPA, a self-supervised deep learning framework that learns atmospheric convection dynamics from continuous proxy variables (OLR, winds, etc.) rather than sparse precipitation data, then transfers this knowledge to improve monsoon rainfall forecasting without ever observing rainfall during pretraining.

**Method:**
- Self-supervised pretraining on five continuous atmospheric fields using Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) operating on Lagrangian patches tracking convective systems
- Frozen backbone transferred to precipitation forecasting via dual-branch decoder (probabilistic + deterministic)
- Rigorous ablation controls: identical architecture trained on rainfall alone, and random initialization baseline

**Data:**
South Asian monsoon region atmospheric fields (OLR, winds, humidity, temperature) tracked in Lagrangian coordinates; evaluation against 51-member ECMWF operational ensemble and observed daily precipitation.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses fundamental challenge in monsoon prediction: deep convection organization is better represented in continuous radiative/dynamical proxies than zero-inflated precipitation, improving predictability of South Asian monsoon variability—a system affecting ~2 billion people.

**Implications:**
Achieves competitive forecast skill (CRPS 6.81 mm/day vs. ECMWF 6.89) on consumer GPU with 15.4M parameters; particularly strong at heavy-rain thresholds and fine scales, enabling accessible, interpretable operational monsoon forecasting grounded in physical dynamics.

**Limitations:**
Neighborhood skill and point-scale deterministic metrics favor the ensemble; framework requires Lagrangian tracking infrastructure; evaluation limited to single monsoon region.

**TL;DR:**
Self-supervised learning on atmospheric proxies outperforms direct rainfall supervision for monsoon precipitation forecasting, demonstrating that latent convective dynamics captured without rainfall labels transfer effectively to precipitation prediction.

# Research Summary: Scalable Bayesian Framework for Sea-Level Inference

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a scalable Bayesian inverse modeling framework that accurately quantifies modern sea-level change and its physical drivers by embedding complete sea-level physics into the inversion process, addressing systematic biases and uncertainty underestimation in standard satellite analysis methods.

**Method:**
- Formulates the inverse problem in infinite-dimensional space to avoid discretization artifacts
- Integrates adjoint sea-level theory into forward/inverse modeling for physically-consistent solutions
- Employs matrix-free iterative solvers and randomized low-rank decompositions for computational efficiency
- Implemented in open-source Python libraries (pygeoinf, pyslfp) enabling laptop-scale computation with trivial parallelization

**Data:**
Satellite gravity observations and ocean altimetry data; demonstrated on synthetic experiments with joint inversion of both data types.

**Climate Relevance:**
Sea-level rise is a critical climate impact indicator; accurate decomposition of sea-level change into steric (thermal expansion) and manometric (mass redistribution) components is essential for understanding climate-driven ocean changes and ice sheet/glacier contributions.

**Implications:**
Enables rigorous uncertainty quantification in sea-level attribution studies, improving confidence in regional sea-level projections and climate model validation; computational accessibility supports broader adoption in operational oceanography and climate monitoring.

**Limitations:**
Validation limited to synthetic experiments; real-data application and comparison with existing operational methods not fully detailed; remaining degeneracies in component separation acknowledged but not fully resolved.

**TL;DR:**
Physics-informed Bayesian inversion with proper uncertainty treatment enables accurate, computationally efficient decomposition of satellite-observed sea-level change into climate-relevant components.

**Limitations:**

# Research Summary: AI Weather Models and Physical Fidelity

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies a fundamental trade-off in AI weather prediction (AIWP) models: their forecast accuracy stems from implicit learning of subgrid-scale effects through data coarse-graining, which paradoxically violates physical principles (thermodynamic arrow of time, butterfly effect) that physics-based models preserve.

**Method:**
Comparative analysis across a hierarchy of systems (reanalysis data → general circulation models → Lorenz equations) examining both forward forecasting and backward backcasting. Models tested include Pangu-Weather and custom neural networks trained on progressively coarse-grained data to isolate the role of scale filtering.

**Data:**
Observation-based atmospheric reanalysis, general circulation model outputs, and the canonical Lorenz 63 chaotic system (enabling ground-truth analysis impossible with real weather).

**Climate Relevance:**
Critical for validating next-generation AI weather/climate models now operationally deployed. Reveals that AIWP accuracy may not reflect genuine physical understanding, raising concerns for long-term climate projections and extreme event prediction where small-scale physics matter.

**Implications:**
AIWP models' success requires rethinking predictability theory and climate emulation strategies. Backcasting emerges as a diagnostic tool to assess physical fidelity. Suggests hybrid approaches may be needed to balance forecast skill with physical consistency for climate applications.

**Limitations:**
Analysis primarily uses idealized systems; real-world atmospheric validation limited. Unclear whether findings generalize to all AIWP architectures or only specific training regimes.

**TL;DR:**
AI weather models achieve accuracy by learning subgrid effects through coarse-graining, but this masks violations of fundamental physics principles critical for climate modeling.

# Research Summary: Temperature Extremes and French Power Infrastructure

**Main Contribution:**
Quantifies extreme temperature statistics across continental France using Bayesian non-stationary extreme value analysis, with explicit uncertainty estimates. Provides high-resolution spatial projections (0.25° grid) of temperature extremes through 2099 under multiple climate scenarios, directly applicable to infrastructure vulnerability assessment.

**Method:**
• Bayesian fitting of non-stationary Generalised Extreme Value (GEV) distribution
• Ensemble approach combining 28 CMIP6 climate models with E-OBS observational data
• Spatial mapping at 0.25° resolution enabling location-specific risk quantification

**Data:**
• CMIP6 multi-model ensemble (28 models)
• E-OBS observational temperature records
• Historical (1850) to future (2099) timespan
• Three emission scenarios: SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses non-stationary climate extremes—a critical gap since traditional stationary assumptions fail under anthropogenic warming. Demonstrates that extreme temperature distributions shift systematically, with implications for infrastructure design standards that assume historical climate stationarity.

**Implications:**
• Occitanie region could experience 57°C extremes by 2080 (high-emission scenario)
• Official French climate adaptation reference (TRACC) potentially underestimates extremes by up to +8°C
• Enables targeted vulnerability assessment for five critical electrical infrastructure sites

**Limitations:**
Model uncertainty propagation and potential biases in CMIP6 ensemble not explicitly discussed; regional-scale downscaling artifacts possible at 0.25° resolution.

**TL;DR:**
Bayesian extreme value analysis of CMIP6 ensemble projects unprecedented temperature extremes across France by 2100, revealing critical gaps in current infrastructure adaptation planning.
