**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Climate-AI Research Summary: FGDSE for EV Charging Infrastructure

**Main Contribution:**
Develops FGDSE (Feature-Governed Dynamic Stacking Ensemble), an interpretable ML framework that predicts equipment faults in EV charging stations 1-30 days ahead by integrating climate stress signals with operational data, enabling preventive maintenance and climate-resilient urban mobility.

**Method:**
- Partitions heterogeneous signals into four feature families (physical, behavioral, contextual, historical)
- Assigns domain-expert base learners to each family plus two deep temporal experts for short/long-term patterns
- Uses horizon-wise gating mechanism to adaptively weight expert predictions across forecast horizons
- Applies SHAP attribution and X-learner for causal treatment effect estimation of climate exposures

**Data:**
25 months of operational records from 13 EV charging stations, including climate variables (temperature, precipitation, humidity), equipment telemetry, and fault events.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly addresses climate adaptation by quantifying how extreme heat, precipitation, and humidity increase charging infrastructure failure risk; identifies extreme heat as the dominant causal driver with amplifying effects over time, enabling climate-adaptive maintenance thresholds.

**Implications:**
Supports transition from reactive to preventive maintenance; reduces unplanned downtime in EV charging networks; provides quantitative climate-adaptation guidelines for urban planners; strengthens low-carbon mobility resilience in climate-stressed cities.

**Limitations:**
Limited to 13 stations (geographic generalization unclear); 30-day forecast horizon may be insufficient for long-lead climate planning; causal analysis restricted to post-level treatment effects.

**TL;DR:**
An ensemble ML system predicts EV charger faults weeks ahead by isolating extreme heat as the primary climate driver, enabling climate-adaptive preventive maintenance for resilient urban mobility.

### [Hard conservation correctors can hide a degrading model when training autoregressive emulators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18416v1)

**Authors:** William E. Chapman, John Schreck, Yingkai Sha
**Published:** 2026-07-20
**Categories:** physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Hard Conservation Correctors in Climate Emulators

**Main Contribution:**
Identifies a critical failure mode in physics-informed climate emulators where hard conservation correctors (e.g., water-budget closure) can mask model degradation during training. Demonstrates that supervising corrected outputs creates a scale degeneracy that allows raw predictions to drift while maintaining apparent budget closure.

**Method:**
- Fine-tuned a global atmosphere emulator with a water-budget hard corrector applied post-hoc
- Compared two training strategies: (1) supervising corrected predictions with hard correction applied, vs. (2) supervising raw predictions while penalizing raw budget imbalance
- Conducted 2×2 ablation study to isolate the failure mechanism
- Tracked both raw model outputs and correction magnitudes throughout training

**Data:**
Global atmospheric model outputs; specific datasets not explicitly named in abstract, but applied to precipitation and moisture budget fields.

**Climate Relevance:**
Conservation laws (mass, water, energy) are fundamental constraints in climate modeling. Ensuring budget closure is critical for physical consistency and long-term stability in climate projections, making this a foundational requirement for operational emulators.

**Implications:**
Practitioners must monitor raw model performance and correction magnitudes separately, not just post-correction budget closure metrics. Supervision strategy significantly impacts whether emulators learn physical constraints or merely compensate for errors through correctors.

**Limitations:**
Single-model case study; generalizability to other conservation laws (energy, mass) and emulator architectures unclear. No comparison with alternative constraint-enforcement methods.

**TL;DR:**
Post-correction budget closure alone is insufficient validation; emulators can degrade while appearing to satisfy conservation laws if correctors are applied after the supervised loss.
