**Published:** 2026-05-06

# Research Summary: ML-Based Gravity Wave Parameterization

**Main Contribution:**
Develops interpretable neural networks to predict orographic gravity wave momentum fluxes at Earth system model (ESM) resolution, demonstrating that ML can improve subgrid-scale parameterizations with physically meaningful learned relationships.

**Method:**
• Train fully-connected neural networks on ERA5 reanalysis data coarse-grained to ESM resolution
• Extract inertia-gravity waves using MODES software (linear wave filtering theory)
• Evaluate model interpretability via SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) explainable AI
• Compare performance against physics-based Lott-Miller parameterization scheme

**Data:**
ERA5 reanalysis (full year for training, separate year for offline evaluation); data processed at multiple scales (full spectrum, subgrid-scale only; global and mountainous terrain subsets).

**Climate Relevance:**
Gravity waves drive upper-atmosphere dynamics and affect momentum transport in climate models. Current ESMs cannot resolve these small-scale processes, requiring improved parameterizations to enhance climate prediction accuracy.

**Implications:**
Demonstrates feasibility of ML-based parameterizations as operational alternatives to traditional physics-based schemes, potentially improving climate model fidelity without increased computational cost.

**Limitations:**
Offline evaluation only (not tested in coupled ESM); performance varies by case (R² = 0.56–0.72); generalization to different climate states or resolutions not explicitly addressed.

**TL;DR:**
Neural networks trained on reanalysis data successfully predict gravity wave momentum fluxes with interpretable physics-informed relationships, offering a pathway toward improved ML-based climate model parameterizations.

**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Drought Prediction in Africa Using DroughtFormer

**Main Contribution:**
Develops DroughtFormer, a CrossFormer-based deep learning model within the CREDIT framework, to predict drought and flash drought across Africa at seasonal-to-subseasonal timescales. Demonstrates that transformer-based ML models can achieve skillful drought forecasts in data-sparse African regions out to 90-day lead times.

**Method:**
Transformer architecture (CrossFormer) incorporating physics-informed constraints (dry air mass, moisture conservation) to predict soil moisture, vegetation health, and drought indices from multi-source reanalysis and satellite data.

**Data:**
ERA5 and GLDAS2 reanalyses; IMERG and MODIS satellite observations; trained/evaluated on African drought events.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical vulnerability in Africa where droughts directly threaten food security and livelihoods dependent on rain-fed agriculture; enables early warning for agricultural drought at subseasonal-to-seasonal scales.

**Implications:**
Provides actionable drought forecasts for agricultural planning and disaster preparedness in vulnerable African regions; demonstrates viability of ML weather prediction in data-limited areas.

**Limitations:**
Lower accuracy in precipitation and flash drought index prediction; struggles with anomaly magnitude representation despite skillful anomaly patterns; performance gap relative to global ML models (GraphCast, AIFS) remains unclear.

**TL;DR:**
A physics-informed transformer model successfully predicts agricultural drought in Africa with 90-day lead time, showing promise for early warning despite challenges with precipitation and anomaly magnitudes.

• Uses classifier-free guidance to steer generation toward extreme events (tropical cyclones)
• Computes odds ratios by comparing probability densities between guided and unguided model outputs
• Applies importance sampling to reduce variance in probability estimates versus standard Monte Carlo

**Data:**
Not explicitly specified in abstract; training data appears to be atmospheric state observations compatible with boundary condition inputs.

**Climate Relevance:**

**Published:** 2026-05-07
**Categories:** physics.soc-ph, econ.GN
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐

# Research Summary: Cascading Food Supply Vulnerabilities

**Main Contribution:**
Develops the first integrated cascading-impact model linking natural gas, fertilizer, and crop supply chains to quantify global food security vulnerabilities. Demonstrates that complete trade isolation would eliminate up to 22% of global caloric consumption, with structural vulnerabilities increasing substantially since the 1990s.

**Method:**
Cascading-impact network model integrating bilateral trade flows across three supply chain layers (natural gas → fertilizer production → crop output). Simulates complete trade isolation scenarios to identify systemic bottlenecks and propagation pathways of upstream shocks downstream to food availability.

**Data:**
Trade data spanning 208 countries and 20 geopolitical blocs (1992-2023); covers natural gas, NPK fertilizers, and 11 staple crops representing ~70% of plant-based calories; includes food stock inventories.

**Climate Relevance:**
Identifies how climate-driven disruptions to energy and agricultural systems (e.g., gas supply shocks, fertilizer production constraints) cascade through interconnected global supply chains, amplifying food security risks beyond direct crop impacts.

**Implications:**
Reveals upstream gas and fertilizer markets as critical resilience leverage points; highlights urgent need for supply chain diversification and strategic food reserves, particularly in import-dependent regions (EU shows 2× vulnerability increase since 1990s).

**Limitations:**
Assumes complete trade isolation (unrealistic scenario); doesn't model partial disruptions, adaptive market responses, or climate-specific failure mechanisms; temporal resolution unclear for recent geopolitical events.

**TL;DR:**
Global food security faces structural collapse risks from tightly coupled energy-fertilizer-crop supply chains, with upstream bottlenecks amplifying downstream shocks.

# Research Summary: MELO for Non-Stationary Climate Prediction

**Main Contribution:**
Proposes MELO, a model-agnostic online aggregation method that adapts to distribution shifts by hedging across multiple memory horizons without requiring prior knowledge of regime-change timing or external indicators.

**Method:**
- Wraps base predictors with exponentially weighted least-squares (EWLS) experts at varying forgetting factors
- Aggregates raw and adapted forecasts using MLpol, a parameter-free online aggregation rule
- Provides deterministic oracle inequalities guaranteeing competitive performance against best raw predictor and best time-varying affine combinations
- Operates via lightweight recursive updates without model retraining

**Data:**
French national electricity-load forecasting during COVID-19 lockdown (March–May 2020); no external regime indicators, lockdown dates, or policy covariates used.

**Climate Relevance:**
Directly applicable to energy-demand forecasting under climate-policy disruptions and extreme events. Electricity load is a critical climate-mitigation metric; accurate forecasting under sudden behavioral shifts (lockdowns, extreme weather) improves grid stability and renewable integration.

**Implications:**
Enables robust real-time forecasting for climate-sensitive systems (energy, water, agriculture) during unprecedented events without requiring explicit external covariates or model retraining—critical for rapid-response climate adaptation.

**Limitations:**
Evaluation limited to single electricity dataset; generalization to other climate variables (temperature, precipitation, emissions) and longer-term regime shifts unclear. Boundedness assumptions may not hold under extreme climate events.

**TL;DR:**
Parameter-free online learning method that automatically adapts to distribution shifts in energy forecasting, achieving 34.7% RMSE improvement during COVID-19 without external regime information.
