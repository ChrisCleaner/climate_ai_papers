### [Domain-Adaptive Climate Downscaling Under Temporal Distribution Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05645v1)

**Authors:** Shuochen Wang, Nishant Yadav, Auroop R. Ganguly
**Published:** 2026-07-06
**Categories:** cs.LG, physics.ao-ph
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# Domain-Adaptive Climate Downscaling Under Temporal Distribution Shift

**Main Contribution:**
Addresses the temporal out-of-distribution (OOD) challenge in climate downscaling by proposing a domain-adaptive deep learning framework that maintains model performance when applied to future climate projections with substantially different distributions than historical training data.

**Method:**
Combines supervised high-resolution (HR) reconstruction on historical data with domain alignment techniques to bridge the distributional gap between historical and future climate states. The framework leverages paired low-resolution (LR) and HR climate model simulations to learn robust downscaling relationships.

**Data:**
Paired LR-HR climate model simulations for daily temperature over the Continental United States, with validation across multiple future periods to assess temporal OOD robustness.

**Climate Relevance:**
Critical for generating reliable high-resolution climate projections under non-stationary conditions. Improved downscaling directly supports regional climate impact assessments, water resource management, and adaptation planning that depend on accurate future temperature fields.

**Implications:**
Enables more trustworthy operational climate downscaling systems for decision-makers. Particularly valuable for complex terrain (high-elevation, topographically intricate regions) and extreme temperature analysis, where traditional bias-correction methods underperform.

**Limitations:**
Study focuses on daily temperature over one region; generalization to other variables (precipitation, wind) and geographic domains requires validation. Computational cost of domain adaptation not discussed.

**TL;DR:**
Domain adaptation significantly improves deep learning-based climate downscaling robustness when models are applied to future climates with different statistical properties than training data.

### [A harmonised dataset for Earth system foundation models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03298v1)

**Authors:** Carlos Rodriguez-Pardo, Massimo Tavoni
**Published:** 2026-07-03
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, econ.GN
**Relevance Score:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

# WorldTensor: A Harmonised Dataset for Earth System Foundation Models

**Main Contribution:**
WorldTensor is a unified global dataset that harmonises hundreds of environmental and socioeconomic variables onto a standardised 0.25° spatial grid with annual temporal resolution, enabling training of multimodal Earth system foundation models that integrate human and natural systems.

**Method:**
- Regridded heterogeneous datasets to common 0.25° grid and annual timesteps
- Rasterised point/vector data into gridded fields
- Reconciled temporal mismatches (daily observations to sparse multiyear snapshots)
- Standardised outputs as NetCDF files with CF metadata conventions

**Data:**
Integrates reanalysis products, remote sensing, emissions inventories, land use reconstructions, hydrological observations, infrastructure datasets, hazard maps, and socioeconomic indicators.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gap in foundation model training by combining physical climate/weather data with human system variables (infrastructure, emissions, socioeconomic indicators), enabling models to learn coupled human-environment dynamics essential for climate impact assessment and adaptation planning.

**Implications:**
Provides reproducible infrastructure for training foundation models capable of understanding bidirectional climate-society interactions at planetary scale, supporting improved climate projections, risk assessment, and policy evaluation.

**Limitations:**
Temporal resolution (annual) may obscure high-frequency climate dynamics; sparse socioeconomic data coverage limits representation of human systems; regridding introduces interpolation artifacts.

**TL;DR:**
WorldTensor standardises 100+ environmental and socioeconomic variables on a common grid to enable foundation models that learn coupled Earth system dynamics.

# Storm Track Self-Reinforcement Through Cloud Radiative Effects

**Main Contribution:**
Demonstrates that cloud radiative effects, rather than insolation gradients alone, maintain Southern Hemisphere storm activity by creating meridional surface heating gradients. Identifies a self-reinforcing feedback loop between storms, clouds, and sea-surface temperature.

**Method:**
- Satellite observation analysis of cloud albedo and radiative effects
- Idealized aquaplanet climate simulations (GCM-based)
- Theoretical model linking storm-cloud-SST coupling to quantify feedback strength

**Data:**
Satellite observations of midlatitude cloud properties and radiative fluxes; model output from aquaplanet simulations with varying cloud-storm interactions.

**Climate Relevance:**
Explains the persistence of Southern Hemisphere storm tracks during austral summer when traditional drivers (insolation gradients) are minimal, revealing a previously underappreciated mechanism sustaining extratropical cyclone activity and regional weather patterns.

**Implications:**
Improved understanding of storm-track dynamics enhances climate model fidelity for projecting future storm behavior under changing cloud regimes. Critical for regional precipitation and wind forecasting in the Southern Hemisphere.

**Limitations:**
Idealized aquaplanet setup may not capture full complexity of realistic continents and ocean circulation; feedback strength depends on two emergent cloud properties that require further observational validation.

**TL;DR:**
Cloud radiative feedbacks self-reinforce Southern Hemisphere storm activity by maintaining thermal gradients independent of seasonal insolation changes.

Demonstrates that positive SSTA signals in the tropical western Pacific, Japan Sea-Kuroshio region, and North Atlantic indicate enhanced predictability for East Asia summer temperature extremes.

**Method:**
Evaluates forecast skill through multi-region SSTA forcing analysis, using real-time observational data combined with model predictions to assess the coherence of large-scale oceanic forcing signals that drive East Asia summer temperature variability.

**Data:**
Latest observational SSTA records and model predictions for three key oceanic regions; real-time monitoring of background climate signals.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses predictability of regional heat extremes in East Asia—a densely populated region vulnerable to summer temperature anomalies. Identifies mechanisms linking ocean-atmosphere coupling to continental heat stress.

**Implications:**
Enables decision-makers to assess forecast confidence for extreme heat events, supporting proactive planning for drought risk and power grid strain. Provides lead time for infrastructure and resource management in affected regions (China, Korea, Japan).

**Limitations:**
Limited discussion of forecast skill metrics, model ensemble details, or validation against historical hindcasts. Credibility assessment relies on SSTA patterns without explicit uncertainty quantification.

**TL;DR:**
Positive ocean temperature anomalies across three regions suggest high predictability for 2026 East Asia summer heat extremes, enabling informed disaster preparedness.

# Research Summary: Stochastic Trajectory Generation from Record Data

**Main Contribution:**
Develops a statistical method to reconstruct complete time series from only record values (extrema exceeding all previous observations) in non-stationary systems. Demonstrates applicability to glacier dynamics where only moraine records are available.

**Method:**
- Constructs a Brownian motion stochastic simulator to generate full trajectories from sparse record data
- Implements Neural-Based Inference (NBI) procedure for hyperparameter optimization
- Handles non-stationary processes without requiring complete historical observations

**Data:**
Glacier des Bossons (French Alps) serves as benchmark case study with well-documented frontal position records interpreted from moraine evidence.

**Climate Relevance:**
Addresses critical gaps in paleoglacial reconstruction by inferring glacier front dynamics from limited field evidence (moraines), enabling assessment of glacier response to climate change over centennial-to-millennial timescales and contribution to sea-level rise.

**Implications:**
- Enables hazard mitigation planning for mountain regions with incomplete glacial records
- Supports water resource assessment in glacier-dependent regions
- Provides framework for reconstructing climate-driven processes where only extreme events are preserved in geological records

**Limitations:**
Method relies on assumption that records follow Brownian motion dynamics; applicability to non-Brownian glacier behavior unclear. Validation limited to single benchmark glacier.

**TL;DR:**
Novel stochastic method reconstructs complete glacier trajectories from sparse moraine records, enabling paleoclimate inference where only extreme positions are preserved.
